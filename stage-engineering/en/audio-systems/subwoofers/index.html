Subwoofer systems

Below about one hundred hertz the wavelength is metres. Moving a Subwoofer by a metre changes which mode is driven. More gain lifts the peaks and the dips together. The design is position, count, and delay, then measurement, not an equalizer on one channel.

Definition

A low-frequency cabinet is nearly omnidirectional on its own. The room, not the grille, draws the coverage: axial modes in an empty rectangle sit near c/2L, with c about 343 m/s. At 50 Hz the wavelength is about 6.9 m. These are geometric estimates for a simple room, not a calculation of a furnished hall, and not a model’s output. The page invents no SPL.

Coupling: sources spaced less than about a quarter wavelength tend to add in phase. Spacing and delay change the direction. Cardioid — an end-fire array or a gradient with a rear driver — needs spacing, delay, polarity, room behind the array, and then a measurement at the rear. Rear rejection is limited in frequency and costs forward output compared with an omnidirectional stack of the same cabinets. It is not a preset.

When is this service needed?

  • Music or picture needs extension

    The programme carries energy below the speech band. A full-range cabinet is not assumed sufficient because of its name.

  • Bass jumps between seats

    One seat roars and the next is empty at the same frequency. That is a room mode, not a lack of global gain.

  • The stage or the neighbour is contaminated

    Rear energy on the microphones or in the next hall. That is when a cardioid technique is discussed, with its conditions.

  • Not a knob

    Turning the bass up on the console after one cabinet was dropped in a corner. Position comes first.

Where it is used

  • Theatres and amplified music rooms

    A crossover to the Line Array or Point Source, and delay so arrivals join in the seating.

  • Cinemas

    Low-frequency extension for the dedicated channel. Isolation from the next screen is the isolation page’s question, not a gain question.

  • Arenas

    A wide audience and long distances. Distribution, and cardioid if required, matter more than one cabinet at mid-grid.

  • Small rooms

    Modes are few and strong. Listener and cabinet position beat any equalizer. Thin felt does not treat 50 Hz.

Parts of the solution

  • Cabinets and count

    Count comes from coverage and coupling, not from a wish for “more bass”. No model output is stated here.

  • Position

    Relative to walls and seats. A corner drives modes a mid-wall place does not. Decide on the plan, then by measurement.

  • Processing and delay

    Crossover, polarity, and a time delay that matches distance. A file stored with the drawing.

  • Cardioid technique

    A defined spacing between elements, a delay that matches it, and a measurement behind the array. The conditions are part of the design.

  • Measurement in the seats

    Several points, not the mix seat. The spread between points is the mode map.

Engineering considerations

  • Wavelength

    343 m/s divided by frequency. 50 Hz is about 6.9 m. A move that looks small in the room is large relative to the wave.

  • Axial mode

    The c/2L estimate for an empty rectangle. A real hall has balconies and seats. The estimate is a start, not a result.

  • Coupling

    Inside about a quarter wavelength, addition is stronger. Beyond that, directional cancellations appear. The figure is a discussion, not a project spacing.

  • Cardioid is not a button

    Without spacing, delay, and a rear measurement, the preset is a name. Rejection changes with frequency and reduces forward efficiency.

  • No named SPL

    This page publishes no cabinet output. The target is in the brief, the capability is in the manufacturer file under its conditions, and the verdict is a measurement.

Materials

  • A matched series in the array

    Mixing different responses breaks the calculated delay. Replacement is from the same family.

  • Floor and load

    A stack is a concentrated load on the slab or the stage. It is reviewed with the structural engineer, not assumed.

  • Low-frequency room treatment

    Thickness, spacing, or a resonant absorber. Thin PET felt does not fix the mode. The coefficient comes from a report, not from this page.

Related systems

  • The main hang

    The crossover to the Line Array or Point Source is measured in the seating, not only on paper.

  • Stage and microphones

    Rear energy raises feedback and mud on stage. Cardioid is one remedy if its conditions are met.

  • Isolation

    Bass crosses partitions more easily than speech. Turning the gain down does not replace a door and a duct.

  • The building structure

    A cabinet on a light tier or an unrated grid shakes what was not designed to vibrate. Coordination is structural.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Name the programme’s lowest frequency and the zone that should match: stalls, balcony, stage.

  2. 02

    Draw possible positions relative to walls, exits, and seats. Do not start from a corner because it is empty.

  3. 03

    Estimate modes with c/2L as a picture of a simple room, then leave it behind at the first measurement.

  4. 04

    If rear rejection is required, choose a cardioid geometry, write the spacing and the delay it needs, and confirm the rear is available to measure.

  5. 05

    Tie the crossover and the delay to the main system. Do not hand over two independent channels.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Cabinets go in the drawn positions, not where a short cable happened to reach.

  2. 02

    Polarity is checked electrically before any judgement by ear.

  3. 03

    Delay is entered from the measured distance, then corrected with the transfer function.

  4. 04

    A cardioid array is measured at the rear before it is called cardioid in the report.

  5. 05

    The file is stored with the position drawing. Moving a cabinet without updating the file brings the complaint back.

Testing

  • A seat map

    Several points across width and depth. One happy point does not pass the layout.

  • The rear if rejection is claimed

    A measurement behind the array across frequency. Without it there is no cardioid handover.

  • No model figure

    No comparison with an invented output. Compare seats with each other and with the brief’s target if one was written.

Commissioning

  • A crossover that is heard and measured

    The seating shows neither a jump nor a hole at the crossover. The curve decides, not the impression of a favourite song.

  • Polarity documented

    The direction of every cabinet is in the file. One reversal cancels the whole row in a band.

  • Cardioid conditions written

    Spacing, delay, and the rear measurement position. If one item is missing, the name is removed from the handover.

Maintenance

  • A move after an event

    Return the cabinets to the marks on the floor. Position is part of the tune.

  • Replacing a channel

    A different driver or amplifier changes gain and polarity. Check again before the show.

  • An extra equalizer on show night

    Cutting a peak in one seat digs a hole in another. Treat the position if the pattern repeats.

Common mistakes

  • The bass knob

    Raising level until one seat smiles. Peaks and dips rise together.

  • A corner cabinet with no measurement

    A corner is not a design. It may suit one room and ruin another.

  • Cardioid in name only

    Enabling a preset with no matching spacing and no rear measurement. The rejection is not there yet.

  • An invented model output

    Writing a named cabinet’s level from memory or from this page. The source is the manufacturer file under its conditions, or a measurement.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why is turning the bass up not enough?

Because a mode is a peak in one seat and a dip in another. Gain multiplies both. Change the position or add a source with a calculation, then measure.

Is cardioid always cleaner?

Only if the spacing and delay are real and there is room to measure behind the array. Otherwise you lose output with no documented rejection.

Do you write the cabinet’s output?

No. There is no model SPL. The manufacturer file under its conditions, then the hall measurement.

Where do I put the first cabinet?

Not automatically in the corner. Try it on the drawing relative to the audience centre and the walls, then confirm by measurement in more than one seat.

Does the subwoofer fix RT60?

No. It can excite a longer low-frequency tail. Treatment and the time belong on the acoustics pages. An equalizer does not shorten the room’s decay.
