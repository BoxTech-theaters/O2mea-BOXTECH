Hearing loops

An AFILS generates a magnetic field that carries the programme to a telecoil. The wire geometry, the amplifier current, the steel in the floor, and the next room all change the result. The verdict is a field-strength meter on a grid at the specified height, not the presence of the cable.

Definition

IEC 60118-4 is the field-performance reference for induction loops. The reference level it defines is 400 milliamperes per metre at the reference frequency. The variation over area and over frequency, and the magnetic noise limit, live in the adopted edition and are read on the meter. This page does not copy the acceptance table and does not turn 400 into a project result. A perimeter cable with no calculation is a drawing, not a system.

Metal loss: reinforcing mesh and steel in the floor weaken the field, especially at higher frequencies, and the sound tends to go dull. Compensation is amplifier headroom and an equalizer confirmed after the floor finish, not from the wire length on the plan. Spill: the field does not stop at the wall. The next hall, or a seat outside the loop, can pick up the programme. Where confidentiality or two adjacent halls matter, a cancelling layout — a figure-eight or a phased array — is used, with geometric conditions and then measurement on both sides.

When is this service needed?

  • An assistive-listening brief

    The local code or the brief requires a loop or an equivalent system. The requirement is met by field performance, not by wire length.

  • A hall with dense steel

    A reinforced slab or a metal floor. A design that ignores the loss fails in brightness after the finish.

  • Sensitive adjacent rooms

    Two halls, or a confidential meeting room. Spill is an audible programme where it must not be. The cancelling layout is part of the design.

  • Not only after the carpet is laid

    Rerouting the wire after the slab is poured is expensive. The route is drawn with the reinforcement, not on opening night.

Where it is used

  • Lecture halls and theatres

    The whole seating area, or the zones the brief names, with accessible seats inside the coverage, not outside it.

  • Conference rooms

    A table and an audience behind it. A perimeter loop can leave the middle weak or spill into the next room.

  • Service counters and ticket points

    A small local loop. It is still measured, and spill toward the clerk or the next customer is still a question.

  • Where a loop is not the tool

    Magnetic interference that cannot be solved, or spill that cannot be accepted. The equivalent system the code names is studied instead of insisting on a wire.

Parts of the solution

  • The wire and its route

    Height, area, and a calculated overlap. A default rectangle is not every room’s design.

  • The amplifier

    Current enough for the loop impedance after metal loss, with headroom that does not clip the programme at peaks.

  • The programme source

    The same signal the audience hears, at a controlled level. A loop on a neglected microphone is not the system.

  • The field-strength meter

    The handover instrument. Without recent calibration and the method the standard describes, the figure is not evidence.

  • A cancelling layout when needed

    A figure-eight or a phased array to reduce spill. The conditions are spacings and current direction, then a measurement in the other room.

Engineering considerations

  • The reference level

    400 mA/m is a definition in IEC 60118-4, not a target this page guesses. The tolerance is in the edition.

  • Metal loss

    It appears once the steel and the finish exist. A survey before the pour is an estimate. The verdict is after the floor is complete.

  • Spill

    The field extends past the loop line. The next room is measured if it is sensitive. It is not assumed silent.

  • Height and orientation

    The standard sets the measurement height and the coil orientation. A reading on the floor, or with a phone, is not the method.

  • Magnetic noise

    Power cables and transformers raise the floor. The standard limits it. This page does not copy the decibel limit.

Materials

  • The loop conductor

    A cross-section that carries the current and voltage the design calculates. A thin wire because it is “hidden” fails thermally or on voltage.

  • Fixing in the floor

    Coordinated with the floor build-up and with rolling loads. Cutting the loop at a movement joint reopens the design.

  • No absorbers here

    PET felt and treatment do not make a field. Mixing the loop with architectural acoustics delays both.

Related systems

  • The sound system

    The loop takes a controlled feed from the same intelligible mix. A poor STI is heard in the coil as well.

  • Isolation between halls

    Magnetic spill is not the wall path. The isolation page does not close it.

  • Electrical power

    Power conduits parallel to the loop are a magnetic-noise source. The two routes are coordinated.

  • Accessible seats

    Loop coverage where the person who needs it sits, not only where it was easy to run wire around the hall.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Name the area, the hearing aids intended, and the rooms that must not hear the programme.

  2. 02

    Draw the known steel: reinforcement, access flooring, and power routes. Metal loss starts from that map.

  3. 03

    Choose the loop geometry for the area and for spill, not the perimeter because it is easier to draw.

  4. 04

    Size the amplifier for impedance and expected loss, with headroom. Not for “what is in the store”.

  5. 05

    Write the measurement grid and height to IEC 60118-4. Handover without a grid is not scheduled.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The wire is laid before the floor is closed, on the approved route, and the joints are photographed.

  2. 02

    It is not cut at a joint or a threshold without a designed splice. A break is a distorted field.

  3. 03

    The amplifier is connected after the actual impedance is known, not only the theoretical impedance.

  4. 04

    Polarity is tested with the programme signal before the formal measurement is called.

  5. 05

    Measure after the metal finishes, not only before. The loss changes when the floor is closed.

Testing

  • The standard’s grid

    Points, height, and orientation as the adopted edition of IEC 60118-4 describes. This page does not rewrite the grid.

  • Frequency response

    Metal loss shows up in brightness. Equalization is confirmed by measurement, not by ear in the corridor.

  • The next room

    If spill is a constraint, measure there too. Assumed silence is not a test.

Commissioning

  • A calibrated meter

    A current calibration certificate. A figure from an unknown instrument is not handed over.

  • The reference programme

    The signal the standard requires, not only the show music. The method is in IEC 60118-4.

  • A report that covers the area

    The map, the height, the response, the noise, and spill if required. The sentence “the loop works” is not a handover.

Maintenance

  • A floor opened later

    Any cut in the wire is spliced to the detail, then the affected measurement zone is repeated.

  • A replacement amplifier

    Current and headroom differ. Do not assume the old preset is still inside tolerance.

  • A change in reinforcement or power routing

    New loss or new noise. A complaint after the alteration goes back to measurement, not to an assumed fault in the hearing aid.

Common mistakes

  • A cable around the room

    A perimeter with no calculation leaves the middle weak or spills to the neighbour. Presence is not performance.

  • Measuring before the metal

    The result changes when the mesh is poured and the floor is closed. An early handover fails on opening day.

  • A phone instead of the meter

    A phone application is not IEC 60118-4. The instrument is a field-strength meter used to the method.

  • Copying 400 as if it were achieved

    The reference level is a definition in the standard, not a reading of this hall. The tolerance and the edition are in the document, not here.

Questions designers and owners ask

Do I run a cable around the hall and stop?

No. Route, amplifier, metal loss, and spill are designed, then the field is measured to IEC 60118-4. The cable is presence, not performance.

What does 400 mA/m mean?

A reference level in the standard. Its tolerance, frequency response, and noise limit are in the edition. This page does not turn the figure into a certificate for your hall.

Why does the loop sound dull?

Usually metal loss that was not compensated after finishes. Equalization is confirmed with the field meter, not by turning the gain up until it distorts.

The neighbour hears the programme in their aid. Why?

Magnetic spill. The acoustic wall does not stop it. A cancelling layout or a new route, then measurement in both rooms.

Does a phone prove acceptance?

No. Acceptance is a field-strength meter used as IEC 60118-4 specifies, with a calibrated instrument.
