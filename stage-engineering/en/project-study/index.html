What this gate is

A useful brief separates a wished capacity from the capacity the rake and the code allow. It names whether success is intelligible speech, music, image, or a cleared floor.

If there is no drawing, approximate dimensions are enough if the sentence says they are approximate. A study that invents precision it does not have is worse than one that says “not known yet”.

What is published now

  • Brief fields

    Venue, place, capacity, condition, scope, and the note.

  • Local copy

    The brief is kept in your browser so it is not lost while you write.

  • No price

    There is no money line in the result.

  • No product

    The form suggests no model number.

When to open this gate

  • Before the first meeting

    So the time is not spent defining the word.

  • When comparing bids

    The same brief is sent to both parties.

  • Not to request a catalogue

    A catalogue without a room is not a study.

How to read the subject

  1. 01

    Write what must succeed, not what you want to buy.

  2. 02

    Say whether the place is new or existing.

  3. 03

    Put capacity as a wish, not a fact, if there is no section.

  4. 04

    Copy the text and attach a drawing when you have one.

What this gate does not do

  • Capacity from dividing the area

    It contradicts the sightline. The calculator warns for that reason.

  • A scope of “everything”

    It prevents an honest price.

  • Believing it was sent

    This edition only copies.

Questions designers and owners ask

Does the brief reach a team?

Not automatically. Copy it and send it by your own channel. No email is published here.

Do I receive a design?

The brief starts a study. A design is a section and a calculation, not a paragraph.

What if I do not know the volume?

Write approximate length, width and height, and say they are approximate.

Do you store my data?

In this browser only, under a local key. There are no user accounts.

Which page do I read before the form?

The library page closest to your problem: RT60, isolation, or sightline.
