Stage lighting

Face light comes from in front of the actor at an angle discussed near 45 degrees so the brow does not swallow the eye, from two sides so the face does not split, and from a position where the audience cannot see the lamp. DMX is data. Power is another circuit. House light brings people in and takes them out. None of this removes a column in the sightline.

Definition

Front light is geometry before it is a fixture. A vertical angle is often planned near 45 degrees so light reaches the eye and the socket does not stay a shadow under the brow, and two angles from the sides so the nose does not become a wall. That is planning, not a law. A low balcony or a short bridge may forbid the angle, and then the position is moved or the shadow is accepted and written down. Compliance with a degree the building cannot reach is not invented. If a seat can see the lamp, or the beam enters the eye, the position is wrong for that seat. Raising intensity increases glare and does not repair it. A followspot needs a position that sees the stage, a path for the operator, and a throw that does not pass through the side row’s eyes.

ANSI E1.11 defines the DMX512-A link. A universe is 512 data slots. A multi-parameter fixture uses more than one slot, so the fixture count is not 512. Power is not the slot: the dimmer or the LED driver is sized from the fixture sheet, and the electrical engineer names which figure was used. Line length and the number of receivers are limited by the standard and the fixture manual. This page does not copy that table. A splitter or a gateway is a decision when the slots run out or when the branch is not what the link allows. A branch does not create an endless universe. House light is for entry, reading, and exit, and whatever the code requires for emergency light may remain even during a scenic blackout. Stage light makes the picture. Lighting does not remove a column, a rail, or a head in the sightline.

When is this service needed?

  • Before the fixtures are bought

    The bridge either reaches the face or it glares the audience. The fixture does not repair the bridge.

  • When data is distributed

    DMX slots are counted from each fixture’s sheet. Assuming one universe covers everything is a false assumption.

  • When house light is separated from stage light

    The circuit for coming in and going out, and the emergency requirement if the code states one, are not the director’s picture.

  • When light is proposed instead of geometry

    A column in the sightline remains a column. No intensity shows what is behind it.

Where it is used

  • A front-of-house bridge

    The angle toward the face, and the lamp outside the seat’s eye. If the beam enters it, the position is the fault, not the fixture.

  • A batten over the stage

    Backlight and side light. On a thrust, the rear beam may strike the opposite audience. The position is checked from those seats.

  • A followspot booth

    A view of the stage, a path for the operator, heat, and a throw that does not sweep the eyes of the front side row.

  • The house

    Entry, reading, and exit light, separate from the cue desk. Emergency light, if required, stays with the code, not with a taste for darkness.

Parts of the solution

  • Front-light positions

    Two sides at least, where the building allows. An angle near 45 degrees is a planning start and is abandoned if the bridge forbids it.

  • The DMX network

    Slots, a topology ANSI E1.11 allows, and a splitter or a gateway when needed. It is not a power cable.

  • Power

    Circuits from the fixture sheet: dimmer or driver. The rack’s place and its ventilation are not the only crossover.

  • The followspot

    A position, an operator, a path, and glare checked from the seats the beam passes over.

  • House light

    Entry, exit, and reading, plus what the code keeps in a blackout. Its control is separate from the stage picture.

Engineering considerations

  • 45 degrees is planning, not law

    The aim is light in the eye without a full brow shadow, and without a beam in the audience’s eye. The building may forbid the figure. This page does not impose it.

  • Glare is a position

    A lamp inside a seat’s field of view is a defect. More intensity does not hide it. It is moved, or shielded from that eye.

  • 512 slots are not 512 fixtures

    ANSI E1.11. A complex fixture eats many slots. Power is an independent circuit, counted from the consumption sheet.

  • The house is not the stage

    Leaving the house at full and then boosting the stage does not make a picture. Switching it all off is checked against the egress requirement, not against the picture alone.

  • Light does not repair a sightline

    A column, a rail, or a head remains a screen. There is no lux figure on this page, and no lux on any page removes geometry.

Materials

  • The fixture

    Consumption and optics come from the maker’s sheet. Replacing it with a wider beam changes glare, not only the slot.

  • The cable

    Data and power on two routes that are not crushed when the batten moves. DMX does not feed the fixture.

  • The hanger

    The fixture is weight on a rated rigging system. Uncalculated scenery steel is not a lighting position.

Related systems

  • Stage design

    The bridge and the batten depend on depth and form. A thrust makes backlight a glare risk. A black box needs points, not paint.

  • Rigging

    A batten loaded with fixtures is a working load. An operator does not move a rail that has people under the lights without a procedure.

  • Picture

    A bright LED screen kills face light. Luminance comes from the screen sheet, not from this page, and coordination comes first.

  • Egress

    Emergency light and aisles come from the adopted code. A blackout cue does not cancel them. This page sets no emergency lux.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Draw the front-light position from the seating section. Discuss 45 degrees, then leave it if the bridge forbids it or it glares a seat.

  2. 02

    Check glare from the side seats and from the opposite bank if the stage is a thrust.

  3. 03

    Count DMX slots from each fixture’s sheet. Add a universe or a gateway when they run out. Do not assume 512 fixtures.

  4. 04

    Turn the power sheet into circuits the electrical engineer names. Separate them from the data cable and from the house-light load.

  5. 05

    Name the followspot booth, the house light, and the emergency requirement. Do not use lighting to hide a sightline obstruction.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Fixtures are installed in the drawn positions, not wherever a clamp remained on the batten.

  2. 02

    The DMX route is separated from power, with slack for the batten’s travel so the connector is not pulled.

  3. 03

    The dimmer or driver rack is ventilated and placed off the only crossover.

  4. 04

    The followspot is tried from its position against the seats its beam passes over.

  5. 05

    House light is handed over on its own circuit. A blackout cue is not programmed over the emergency requirement.

Testing

  • The face from the seat

    The eye is readable from stalls and balcony, and the lamp is not visible to that same seat. A full shadow under the brow means a steeper angle than the form can bear.

  • Slots and power

    The slot count matches the sheet. Current is measured on the expected cue, not on a guess. No watt value comes from this page.

  • Blackout and the exit

    What the code requires for egress light stays visible. A cue test does not cancel the path.

Commissioning

  • A position plan

    Front, back, and followspot, with the seats from which glare was checked. If 45 degrees was used, it is written as planning, not as code.

  • A DMX and power schedule

    The slots of each fixture and its circuit. A universe is 512 slots under ANSI E1.11. This page does not hand over a fixture count equal to the slots.

  • The house separated

    House-light control is independent, and the emergency requirement is named from the code, not from this page.

Maintenance

  • A fixture has drifted

    After work on the batten the angle is restored. An old focus on a tilted fixture glares a new seat.

  • The data network

    A worn connector or a line with no proper end corrupts the whole universe, not one fixture. The check is the link, not a random replacement.

  • Colour media and ventilation

    Burnt media changes the picture. A blocked rack vent takes the dimmer out before the cue is blamed.

Common mistakes

  • 45 degrees treated as a law

    The bridge cannot reach it, or reaches it inside the audience’s eye. The plan is dropped when the building contradicts it.

  • 512 fixtures from one universe

    A slot is not a fixture. A multi-parameter unit uses several slots, and power is not data.

  • A full house competing with the stage

    Or a blackout that cancels exit light. The two circuits are separate, and emergency light is from the code.

  • Intensity to hide a column

    The seat still cannot see. Light does not repair a sightline. The obstruction is geometry.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is 45 degrees mandatory?

No. It is a planning start for front light so the brow does not black out the eye and the audience is not glared. If the building forbids it, the angle changes or the shadow is accepted and written down.

Is a universe 512 fixtures?

No. Under ANSI E1.11 a universe is 512 data slots. A multi-channel fixture takes more than one slot, and power is another circuit.

Why is house light separate?

Because it brings people in and takes them out, and it may carry an emergency requirement from the code. Stage light makes the picture. Merging them spoils both.

Where does the followspot stand?

In a position that sees the stage and that the operator can reach, with a beam that does not sweep the audience’s eyes. A fixture in the aisle with no path is not a followspot.

Do I raise the lighting if a column blocks a seat?

No. Light does not remove the obstruction. The seat is redesigned or accepted as damaged. Extra intensity is glare, not vision.
