Stage design

The stage is what stays inside the last seat’s sightline, not the rectangle behind the proscenium. A wing is where scenery leaves and a performer waits. A crossover is a path that is not seen. The floor is either danced on or loaded with a wagon, and rarely with the same note. The audience line limits all of it.

Definition

Stage design is a volume that is seen and a volume used out of sight. Useful depth is cut by the sightline from the extreme side seat and from the back of a balcony, not by the plan dimension behind the proscenium line alone. The wing must hold the largest piece that has to leave, and a person standing with it. A crossover behind the last masking takes a person and a prop from stage right to stage left without either appearing. If it does not exist, the performer crosses in view or does not cross. The floor is a performance surface and a load path. Dance resilience and a wagon’s point load are different requests.

Proscenium, thrust, and black box are planning choices, not shapes to paste on. A proscenium frames one direction and needs wings and masking or a fly. A thrust wraps the audience, so one scenic wall cannot serve every seat, and backlight becomes glare in the opposite bank. A black box is not black paint: it is a floor, rigging points, and power that allow the stage and the seating to change places. In all three the audience sightline constrains the stage, and a deeper plan does not reverse the constraint. A column, a rail, or a head in the way remains an obstruction. C-value in theatre literature is often discussed around 90–120 mm, then project geometry and the adopted code fix it. That clearance decides which part of the deck stays visible over the head in front. It is not a stage depth printed on this page.

When is this service needed?

  • Before depth is frozen on plan

    The last side seat and the balcony rear are drawn with the depth. The figure behind the opening alone is not playing space.

  • When the form is chosen

    Proscenium, thrust, or black box. Each one changes the wings, the crossover, and the direction of backlight.

  • When wagons or traps are requested

    The wing must hold the wagon while it is offstage, and the floor must take its point load. A trap is a structural decision, not a joinery detail.

  • When depth looks large only on paper

    The side seat sees a narrowing wedge. What lies behind the last masking may be a crossover, not a scene.

Where it is used

  • Proscenium theatres

    One direction, wings, and an upstage crossover. Useful depth is smaller than structural depth if the sightline cuts it.

  • A thrust stage

    Audience on more than one side. One back wall and one entrance are not enough. Backlight threatens the eye opposite.

  • A black box and a studio

    Seating and stage swap. What stays fixed is the floor, the points, the power, and a perimeter that remains a crossover.

  • A school hall asked to be a theatre

    With no wing and no crossover, the drawn depth is an emergency aisle, not a stage. Honesty is to name the limits.

Parts of the solution

  • Useful depth

    From the proscenium line to the last acting line the worst seat can see. Beyond that is hidden scenery or a crossover.

  • The two wings

    A width that holds the largest offstage piece and a person who is not seen from the house. The door does not open into the sightline.

  • The crossover

    Behind the last masking, high enough for a person and a prop. If it is also an exit, it has become a code corridor, not a scenery detail.

  • The floor

    A surface, resilience or the lack of it, and a point load. Floor traps are coordinated with structure before the finish.

  • The frame, or its absence

    A proscenium opening frames. Thrust and black box have no single frame. Masking is drawn with the seat, not after it.

Engineering considerations

  • The seat limits the stage

    The sightline from the side and from the balcony cuts the depth. Deepening the void behind that line adds no visible scene.

  • The constraint is not reversed

    A column, a balcony front, or a rail in the way is not repaired by a larger stage or by stronger light. The obstruction is audience geometry.

  • C-value is not a depth

    Literature discusses about 90–120 mm of vertical clearance over the head in front. The project and the code fix the figure. This page does not turn it into a metre of stage.

  • The crossover is part of the depth

    It is deducted from playing space if the audience can see it, and it is kept if masking hides it from the worst seat, not only from the centre line.

  • The floor is two jobs

    A dance surface wants resilience. A scenery wagon wants stiffness and a point load. One note fails one of them.

Materials

  • The floor surface

    Wood, a dance covering, or paint. Slip and load come from the structural detail. Any fire class comes from the test report of the build-up as built: face, layer, and substrate. This page does not give one.

  • The masking

    Fabric that hides the wing and the crossover. Its absorption and its fire performance are that fabric’s report, not its colour. Hard black may reflect.

  • The wing wall

    A hard surface returns sound to the stage. Softening it into an absorber needs the report of the build-up, not an assumption about cloth.

Related systems

  • Rigging

    What must fly above the useful depth and above the wings. A short tower cancels a drop and flying scenery however deep the plan is.

  • Lighting

    A front bridge that reaches the face, and backlight that does not enter a thrust audience’s eyes. Lighting does not remove a column in the sightline.

  • Seating

    C-value and row height decide where vision begins on the deck. A comfortable seat on the wrong riser eats the depth.

  • Sound

    A hard floor and bare wings return energy to the microphone. Treatment is a later decision, not an excuse to ignore the form.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Draw sightlines from the extreme side seat and the balcony rear before the void depth is fixed.

  2. 02

    Choose a proscenium, a thrust, or a black box, and write what each side sees, not what the centre line sees.

  3. 03

    Place the crossover behind the last masking at the height of a person and a prop, and check that it is hidden from the worst seat.

  4. 04

    Size the wing to the largest piece that leaves, not to a thin person’s aisle on the drawing.

  5. 05

    Separate the performance-floor note from the wagon’s point load. Traps are approved with the structure.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The proscenium line and the stage edge are set out from the sightline drawing, not from an old figure on the plan.

  2. 02

    Wing doors do not open into a sightline and do not cut the crossover.

  3. 03

    The floor is built to the written surface and stiffness. A harder substitute on site changes both dance and load.

  4. 04

    Masking is hung and checked from the side seat. If the crossover shows, the masking is extended or the path is moved.

  5. 05

    Floor traps close flush with the surface. A proud edge is a trip, not a detail.

Testing

  • Sitting in the worst seat

    Mark on the deck what is actually seen from the side and from the balcony. Unseen depth is struck off the playing area.

  • Walking the crossover

    A person with a prop, and a watcher in the side seat. If they appear, the masking or the path is short.

  • Floor match

    The written surface, not the substitute. Any fire class is checked against the report of that same build-up, not against the name of the wood.

Commissioning

  • A useful-depth drawing

    The last acting line and the crossover line are shown. Structural void depth alone is not handed over as a stage.

  • Wing and crossover dimensions

    Written as built, with what the masking hides from the worst seat.

  • Floor and masking reports

    If a class is required, it is the report of the installed build-up. This page holds no certificate.

Maintenance

  • The floor surface

    Refinishing keeps the written friction. Stacked tape becomes an edge. A trap is checked after every use.

  • Masking tied up

    Tying it up for a service exposes the crossover. It is restored before the audience, not at the first interval after a complaint.

  • The wing as a store

    Permanent crates eat the exit path and the performer’s path. Surplus storage is removed, not left until the night.

Common mistakes

  • Depth that is not seen

    A long void the side seat cuts. Area on paper is not a scene.

  • No crossover

    The performer crosses in front of the masking or arrives late. The crossover’s place should have been taken out of the depth on drawing day.

  • A black box by paint

    No points, no power, and no floor that can take a rearranged layout. Colour does not make flexibility.

  • One floor for two requests

    Dance resilience under a loaded wagon, or a hard slab for a dancer. The job is written before the material.

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is a deeper stage better?

Only if the worst seat can see it. Depth beyond the sightline is hidden area. It may serve as a crossover or storage, not as a scene.

Is a black box a black room?

No. Flexibility is a floor, rigging points, and power that allow the audience and the stage to be rearranged. Paint does not provide that.

Where does the crossover go?

Behind the last masking, inside the depth, hidden from the side seat and not only from the centre of the house. Without that, the performer crosses in view.

Does C-value set the stage depth?

It is a viewing clearance over the head in front, discussed around 90–120 mm and then fixed by geometry and code. Its effect is which part of the deck is seen. This page does not turn it into a metre.

Is lighting enough if a column blocks the view?

No. Light does not remove an obstruction. The column or the rail is solved by geometry or accepted as a damaged seat, and it is not hidden by raising intensity.

Technical sources

  • Sightline literature

    C-value is discussed around 90–120 mm, then project geometry and code fix it. This page imposes neither the clearance nor a stage depth.

    Exits, platforms, and rails. This page does not set a legal width for the crossover if it is an exit.

  • The floor and masking test report

    Any fire class of the build-up as installed. Change the face or the layer and the transfer falls. This page has no certificate.
