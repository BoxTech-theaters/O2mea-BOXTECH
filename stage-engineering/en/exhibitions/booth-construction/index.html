Booth construction

A booth drawn with no seat and no joint is rebuilt on site. Construction here is a frame, a face, a screen if needed, a waiting seat, power, and numbered parts that strike. The idea sits on the booth-design page. The ready system sits on the system-stand page.

Definition

Joinery for one show is honest when the form will not repeat and the hours cover its joints. If the show repeats and a clean strike does not, the piece is broken and called a system, and it is not one.

Stability, and an exit if the footprint is large enough under the code, and a materials class if the manual asks, sit with the person responsible for this erection and with the report. This page grants neither a certificate nor a kilogram.

When is this service needed?

  • The form will not stand on octanorm

    Sculpted, or double height within the manual’s limit.

  • One show with a heavy identity

    Joinery is consumed. Do not promise a return if the joint will not strike.

  • Seating and a screen together

    Waiting is part of the footprint. The catalogue supplies the seat, not a free sketch.

  • Before the face is printed

    The door and the store are settled, or a wrong opening is printed.

Where it is used

  • An exhibition hall

    The manual is a contract of height and time.

  • An event inside a hotel

    The corridor is often narrower. Parts are designed for the entrance, not only for the workshop.

  • Not a public stand

    Temporary hospitality is the events page. The load is different.

  • Workshop then site

    What is not trial-struck in the workshop is a surprise in the hall.

Parts of the solution

  • A numbered frame

    Every piece knows its neighbour. Improvisation eats the hours.

  • A face

    Print, wood, or cloth. Fire comes from that build-up’s report if it is asked for.

  • An optional screen

    Indoor LED at aisle distance, not at stadium distance.

  • Seating and storage

    Inside the footprint. A catalogue seat if the wait is long.

Engineering considerations

  • The building entrance

    Piece length passes the door and the lift, not only the truck dream.

  • Strike

    Designed with the build. A window shorter than the part count means a system, not joinery.

  • Power and heat

    A large screen heats a closed store. The path is drawn.

  • The exit

    The code, if the area is large. This page fixes no width.

  • Sound

    A point for a corner conversation. An array is the audio page if the whole hall must hear.

Materials

  • Exhibition timber

    It strikes or it is consumed. Do not promise a building’s life.

  • Welded metal

    A weld that will not strike is an expensive move. A bolted joint may be more honest.

  • A printed face

    It is cut to the approved openings, not to an old render.

Related systems

  • Lighting

    Under hall light. An extra spot glares at the neighbour.

  • LED

    The indoor page. Aisle distance is short.

  • A seat

    From the catalogue. Its centre is not a theatre-row problem, but its width steals footprint.

  • Hall cooling

    A closed booth can stifle. The opening is drawn.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Fix footprint, height, and hours from the manual.

  2. 02

    Draw joints, the door, and storage before the face.

  3. 03

    Test piece length against the building door.

  4. 04

    Name the screen or the print. Not both without a reason.

  5. 05

    Write the strike as a schedule, not as a hope.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    A trial strike in the workshop for a new joint.

  2. 02

    A numbered move. Shortages are found before the hall.

  3. 03

    Installation inside the hours. Delay is announced, not hidden with cutting.

  4. 04

    Power exposed to the inspector as the manual asked.

  5. 05

    The aisle is handed over empty.

Testing

  • A partial strike

    One joint opens and closes before opening.

  • Reading in the hall’s day and evening light

    The face does not depend on the camera flash.

  • Screen heat

    If there is one, after an hour of operation, not after a minute.

Commissioning

  • A signed list

    Parts, power, height.

  • A strike lead

    A name. A booth without a name stays in the hall.

  • What is not accepted

    A form that strikes only by being broken.

Maintenance

  • A second show

    A tired joint is replaced. It is not forced.

  • The face

    A scratch in exposed timber is not hidden without a decision.

  • Storage

    Upright, not leaning. Joinery twists.

Common mistakes

  • A render with no joint

    It is built twice: in the picture and on site with a saw.

  • A promise of permanent reuse

    Exhibition joinery is not household furniture.

  • A stadium screen in a booth

    Pitch and distance are wrong. The indoor page.

  • Ignoring the hotel door

    The piece does not enter. Strike starts in the car park.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is construction the same as design?

No. Design asks what the visitor sees. Construction asks how the piece strikes at night.

Can the booth return?

Only if the joint was designed for it and tried. A promise without a strike is a false trial.

Where does the seat come from?

The seat catalogue if people must wait. Not an exposed stadium chair inside a booth.

Do you include a screen?

If the picture must move. Pitch comes from the indoor LED page at aisle distance.

Where is the fast stand?

The system-stand page. Here, what will not stand on them.

Technical sources

  • The hall manual

    Height, hours, power, and the aisle.

  • The joint drawing

    What strikes and what is consumed. This page draws no particular job.

  • The materials report

    If the organiser requires it. No ready certificate.
