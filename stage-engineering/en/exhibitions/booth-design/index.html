Exhibition booth design

A booth lasts a few days and is still a building: it stands, it stores, it lights a product under the hall’s light and not under the render’s studio, it shows a screen from the aisle distance, it assembles in the hours allowed, then it comes apart. The hall manual on height, power, and rigging beats the picture where they conflict.

Definition

Booth design is a temporary structure inside a hall that has rules. The brand is one face, not the only face: closed storage must hold the samples and the crates needed during the day, or the aisle becomes the store. Lighting is judged with the hall’s real light, not with the exposure of the picture. Power is the sum of screen, lighting, and hospitality before the declaration to the hall. The screen is either an LED whose pitch is compared with the aisle distance, or a projector whose throw ratio comes from the lens table. Move-in, build, and strike hours in the organiser’s manual are part of the design. A joint that cannot be assembled in those hours, or that comes apart only by breaking, is not a booth. Part count, tools, and who owns the crates after closing are drawing items.

Pixel pitch is discussed with the one-arcminute estimate: distance in metres is about 3.4 times pitch in millimetres, from the tangent of 1/60 of a degree. That is a limit for when a pixel shows, not a brand rule and not a comfortable distance for reading text. AVIXA DISCAS exists as a procedure for image size and the viewing task. This page does not perform it and does not output a screen height. Luminance, in nits, comes from the manufacturer sheet at the stated content and environment. No nit value is published here as a specification. A lit hall is not a daylight facade, and an outdoor panel inside a booth glares and heats. Any fire class comes from the test report of the materials as installed: face, print, adhesive, and core. Change one item and the transfer falls. A tall freestanding element needs a base or an anchorage calculated for this erection, under the hall manual and the engineer responsible for it. This page quotes neither a kilogram nor a safety factor.

When is this service needed?

  • Before the plot is hired

    Aisle depth decides screen legibility or projector throw. A narrow plot does not fit an idea designed for a deeper hall.

  • When the hall manual arrives

    Height, amperes, hours, and a rigging ban. A conflict is settled for the manual, not for the render.

  • Before the graphic is printed

    The door, the store, and the joint consume a face. Printing a full wall and cutting an opening late spoils both brand and structure.

  • Before power is declared

    Screen, lighting, and hospitality are added. The breaker does not absorb a surprise on opening day.

Where it is used

  • A shallow exhibition aisle

    The viewer is at two metres or less. A pitch that looks smooth in the picture may show as a grid. The estimate of 3.4 m per millimetre of pitch is a visibility limit, not a promise about text.

  • A corner or an island booth

    More than one face, and a neighbour on the line. Sound and image spill. This page gives no sound pressure level.

  • A hall that forbids rigging

    Everything stands on the floor, within the manual’s height. A hung ceiling in the render is deleted or replaced by a system the hall allows.

  • A booth that returns to another show

    The joint comes apart and the crate closes. Welding, and printing onto a permanent carcass, are a decision not to reuse.

Parts of the solution

  • The structure and the joint

    Parts that assemble inside the manual’s hours and come apart without breaking if the booth will return. The tool is written down, not discovered in the hall.

  • The closed store

    A volume for samples and the daytime crates. Its door does not eat the brand face after printing.

  • The lighting

    On the product, under the hall’s real light. The studio fixture in the render is not the hall’s calculation.

  • The screen or the projector

    A pitch or a throw ratio for the aisle distance. Luminance comes from the sheet. No nit value here, and DISCAS is not performed.

  • The power

    A named sum declared to the hall. The cable route does not cross the aisle bare.

Engineering considerations

  • The hall manual beats the render

    Height, floor load, hours, and rigging. The picture does not hold an exemption.

  • The one-arcminute estimate

    Metres are about 3.4 times pitch in millimetres for the limit at which a pixel shows. Text wants a longer distance or a finer pitch. It is not a brand rule.

  • No nit value on this page

    Luminance comes from the panel sheet at a named content and environment. An exhibition hall is not an outdoor facade.

  • DISCAS is not performed here

    The procedure exists for image size. This page does not calculate it and does not output a screen height. Pitch is an extra question inside that size.

  • Stability for this erection

    A tall freestanding element has its base or anchorage calculated with the hall manual. No kilogram and no safety factor is quoted from here.

Materials

  • The printed face

    Face, adhesive, and core are one build-up. Any fire class comes from the report of that build-up. This page does not give one.

  • An aluminium system or joinery

    Reuse comes from the joint, not from the shape of the render. The system is not a rated structure merely because it is metal.

  • Cable and distribution

    Power and signal are separate, with protection where a visitor walks. A random extension on opening day is an overload and a hazard.

Related systems

  • An LED screen

    Pitch for the aisle distance, front service if there is no rear, and heat inside the booth. No copied nit value.

  • A projector

    Throw ratio from the lens table, and the visitor’s shadow if the beam crosses the aisle. Ambient light lowers contrast.

  • Booth lighting

    Coordinated with the hall light, not with a studio. Glare into the neighbouring aisle is a defect the organiser sees.

  • Sound, if there is any

    It spills to the neighbour. The level is an agreement with the hall, not a figure from this page. No invented SPL.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Start from the hall manual: metres, hours, amperes, and whether rigging is allowed.

  2. 02

    Assign a closed store before the printed face is approved. The door is drawn, then the brand is printed.

  3. 03

    Check the screen: 3.4 m per millimetre of pitch as a visibility limit, or the lens table for throw. Do not perform DISCAS on this page and do not publish a nit value.

  4. 04

    Add the power and name it for the declaration. Add the heat if the screen is large.

  5. 05

    Design the strike with the assembly: part count, tools, crates, and who takes them after closing.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The manual’s height is checked on the first upright before the facade is finished. An overrun is cut, not argued on opening day.

  2. 02

    The joint is assembled with the written tool inside the hour. A part that needs a workshop does not belong in the hall.

  3. 03

    The store closes and the print does not cover it. Cable crosses on a protected route, not in the aisle.

  4. 04

    The screen is read from the real visitor distance before the build crew leaves.

  5. 05

    Strike is tried on one joint in the shop. If it breaks, the design does not come apart.

Testing

  • The real distance

    Text or a picture from the nearest point a visitor stands. The arcminute estimate is a hypothesis. The eye in the aisle is a first judgement. It is not a DISCAS result.

  • Power

    Current draw on the expected content roughly matches the declaration, or the gap is explained. No ampere figure comes from this page.

  • Assembly time

    It is timed with the actual crew, not on the render’s schedule. What exceeds the manual’s window is simplified in the shop.

Commissioning

  • A match to the manual

    Height, power, rigging, and aisle. A clause that is broken is removed before handover to the organiser.

  • Storage and strike

    The closed volume exists, and the crates have an owner after closing. A booth with no strike path is not handed over as reusable.

  • A material report if a class is required

    Face, print, adhesive, and core as installed. This page holds neither a certificate nor a screen nit value.

Maintenance

  • From one show day to the next

    Clamps are checked in the morning. A booth that is partly struck every night, if the hall requires it, is a manual item, not a surprise.

  • Reuse

    A damaged joint is replaced before the next show. Cracked print is not stuck over a tired carcass and called new.

  • The screen

    The spare module is the same pitch if it is LED. Luminance is checked from the manufacturer sheet, not from the memory of the render.

Common mistakes

  • A render with no joint

    A form that cannot be assembled in the hall’s hours or struck without breaking it. A booth is a build method.

  • Storage in the aisle

    No closed volume inside the booth. The crates break the operation and usually the organiser’s rules.

  • A nit value or a pitch from another project

    Distance and environment are this aisle and this sheet. Outdoor is not the inside of the hall. This page gives no nit value.

  • Ignoring strike hours

    The build succeeded and the strike needs twice the window. The design includes both.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

Joinery for one showA form specific to a known hall, with hours enough for its joints.When strike breaks it, or the window is shorter than the part count. It will not return, even if the render calls it a system.
A reusable systemRepeat shows, a fast strike, and joints the crew already knows.When the brand form is not what the system can stand, or when it is treated as a rated building because it is metal.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is a booth only scenery?

In behaviour it is a short-life building: stability, power, an exit if the plot is large enough under the code, and sometimes a materials class from the report of the build-up. The manual and the local code state the limit.

Where do I take screen brightness?

From the manufacturer sheet at the content and the hall environment. This page gives no nit value. An outdoor panel in an indoor booth glares and heats.

How do I estimate LED distance?

Metres are about 3.4 times pitch in millimetres for the limit at which a pixel shows at one arcminute. For text, move closer or refine the pitch. The estimate is not DISCAS and is not a manufacturer rule.

Do I perform AVIXA DISCAS from this page?

No. The standard is a procedure for image size and the viewing task. This page does not calculate it and does not output a screen height. The hall manual still governs the physical height of the booth.

Where do the crates go?

Inside the booth store during the day, then with their owner after closing. The aisle is not a store. Strike hours in the manual are part of that path.

Technical sources

  • The organiser’s hall manual

    Height, hours, power, rigging, and the aisle. This page does not replace the contract conditions with the hall.

  • AVIXA DISCAS and the display sheet

    Image size from the procedure when it is required, and pitch, luminance, and throw from the manufacturer. This page neither performs the calculation nor publishes a nit value or a screen height.

  • The material report and the local code

    Fire class of the build-up as installed, and the stability of a freestanding element with the engineer responsible for this erection. No certificate, no kilogram, and no safety factor on this page.
