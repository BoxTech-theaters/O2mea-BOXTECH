Fixed grandstands

A fixed stand stays, so it carries a repeated crowd and wind if it is exposed, and it takes that load to a foundation or into the building frame. The seat is the last link. The barrier and the aisle are exit width, not decor. This page gives neither a kilonewton nor a barrier height as if it were the code.

Definition

The geometry: a raker, treads, risers, and barriers at aisles and voids. Dead load is the weight of the structure and the seats. Live load is a crowd from the adopted code, reduced or not according to that code’s area rules. Wind and seismic, where they apply, are structural items. This page does not choose the value. Coordination with the building frame means a movement joint, a connection point, and a slab designed for the load, not an architectural slab surprised by the stand.

Aisles and exits are a width calculated from capacity, not from the space left after the chairs. Barrier height and load come from the code, measured as that code defines from the nosing or the standing surface. Inspection needs access to hidden connections. Sightline and C-value constrain riser height: a comfortable riser can hide the row behind. No EN is declared from here.

When is this service needed?

  • Repeated use

    The ground or the hall is seated permanently. A telescopic stand with no operating crew is less honest.

  • Before the slab

    The load enters the structural design. Adding it later is strengthening or refusal.

  • Barrier and exit

    When capacity is drawn. The chair does not set whatever exit width is left over.

  • Periodic inspection is required

    A permanent structure. Connections that cannot be seen are not designed as if they will never be inspected.

Where it is used

  • Stadiums

    Wind, crowd, and an external riser. The seat is another page. The structure is here.

  • Sports halls

    A fixed stand along the hall. Slab, barrier, and coordination with the playing floor.

  • Theatres with permanent tiers

    Architectural concrete or steel. A tip-up seat is mounted on it and does not replace it.

  • Not a passing event

    A single day may be demountable. Fixed means a foundation and a longer life.

Parts of the solution

  • Raker and treads

    The main load path. The section comes from calculation, not from the look of a stand in a photograph.

  • Barriers

    At the edge, the aisle, and the void. Height and load come from the adopted code.

  • Aisles and exits

    A continuous width from the seat to the exit. The platform is not cut by an extra chair.

  • Foundation or connection

    An independent base, or a connection to the building frame with a calculated movement joint.

  • Inspection access

    An opening or a route that sees the connection and the bolt. Hiding everything prevents maintenance.

Engineering considerations

  • Live load from the code

    The crowd value, and any area reduction that exists in the adopted code. No kilonewton here.

  • Wind and vibration

    An exposed stand carries wind. Human rhythm may impose a vibration limit from the structural standard, not from this page.

  • The barrier

    Measured as the code defines. Copying a height from another project is not a design.

  • Sightline with the riser

    C-value is discussed around 90–120 mm, then geometry fixes it. A higher riser with no clearance hides the row behind.

  • No quoted safety factor

    If a standard states a factor, it belongs to the standard and the engineer of record. This page does not quote it as law.

Materials

  • Structural steel

    Grade and connections from the design. Galvanizing is a coating, not a section. See the galvanizing page for a wet environment.

  • Concrete

    A cast riser with cover and reinforcement as drawn. The seat hole does not eat the cover.

  • The tread

    Metal or concrete with slip resistance, and drainage if exposed. This page gives no friction coefficient.

Related systems

  • Seats

    Mounted on the finished riser. They do not reverse the load or the aisle width.

  • Lighting and numbering

    Tread and aisle lit enough to read the letter as the code requires. No lux here.

  • Drainage

    Water from an open stand does not pour into a closed aisle with no path.

  • The building structure

    The connection, the joint, and the shared load. The stand is not a guest on the slab.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Fix capacity and whether it is open or enclosed before the section.

  2. 02

    Enter live load and wind from the adopted code into the structural model.

  3. 03

    Draw the aisle and the barrier with the capacity, not after the chairs are packed.

  4. 04

    Check the sightline against riser height.

  5. 05

    Provide an inspection route to the connections before finishes close them.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The load path is built as drawn. A section change in the shop restarts the calculation.

  2. 02

    Connection bolts are checked before they are hidden.

  3. 03

    The barrier is measured from the reference the code names.

  4. 04

    Seat holes are made at the approved edge distance.

  5. 05

    An as-built of the hidden connections is handed over.

Testing

  • Connections

    Torque or inspection as the execution specification says. This page gives no torque value.

  • Critical dimensions

    Aisle width and barrier height as the code defines them. Measured on site.

  • No invented load test

    If a load test is required, its protocol comes from the engineer, not from a figure on this page.

Commissioning

  • A structural file

    Calculation, as-built drawings, and a use limit. Without them the stand is a structure with no memory.

  • Exit operation

    Doors and aisles clear. An extra chair is removed before the public.

  • No standard conformity from the page

    The declaration, if one is required, is from the engineer of record against the adopted code.

Maintenance

  • Periodic inspection

    Connections, corrosion, and vibration according to the building procedure. This page does not set the months.

  • Seat alterations

    A new hole reopens the edge question. No row is added without a load review.

  • Rust where it is exposed

    Treat it before the section is reduced. Paint is not a structural design.

Common mistakes

  • Furniture on a slab

    A stand ordered after an ordinary slab is built. The load was not designed.

  • Chairs, then an aisle

    Capacity is drawn first. What remains, if anything, is not an exit width.

  • A barrier from another project

    A copied height. The local code and the measurement method may differ.

  • Hiding every connection

    No inspection. The defect stays until failure.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

PathFits whenWeakens when
FixedPermanent use, a slab or foundation designed for it, and inspection possible.When the floor must be cleared regularly, and storage depth plus a crew to operate the movement exist. Then telescopic is more honest.
TelescopicThe floor is really cleared, and storage, operation, and the slab are acceptable.Daily use with no movement crew. Fixed is more honest.
DemountableA temporary event and an inspection at every erection.A stand that stays for years. Temporary is not a substitute for a foundation.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is a grandstand furniture?

No. It is a load path: crowd, barrier, and a foundation or a connection to the frame. The chair is mounted after that.

Who gives the crowd load?

The code adopted for the project and the engineer. This page writes no kilonewton.

Why not put the stand on any slab?

Because the load is concentrated and repeated. A slab not designed for it deflects or cracks. Coordinate before the pour.

Is barrier height a universal constant?

No. Codes and measurement methods differ. There is no millimetre on this page.

When do I refuse fixed?

When the floor must be cleared daily and there is honest storage and operation for a telescopic stand, or when the event is one day and comes apart.

Technical sources
