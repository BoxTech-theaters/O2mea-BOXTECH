Telescopic grandstands

A telescopic stand is units that slide. Closed, it remains a depth in the hall even on the day you call the floor empty. Open, it is a grandstand with a crowd, so the slab is loaded twice: a concentrated stack, then a distributed load. The movement itself is a procedure: a clear zone, an operator who can see the path, and a stop when something is in the way.

Definition

Storage depth is the stack thickness for the row count, from that system’s document, not from this page. If the architectural pocket does not fit the depth, the stand will not disappear. Floor flatness and track tolerance come from the manufacturer document, and slab deflection under the stack and under movement comes from the structural engineer. Neither figure is invented here. A joint in the floor or a drainage slope can stop a wheel or skew a row.

During movement there is no public in the path. Interlocks, an alarm, and a person who can see the whole travel are part of handover, not an accessory. Fixed is more honest when use is daily with no crew, or the slab cannot take the track, or there is no depth. A telescopic stand does not become fixed because someone forgot to close it. Each state — open or closed — is a use condition with its own limit.

When is this service needed?

  • The floor changes job

    A match and then a floor event, and a crew will actually move the stand.

  • The depth exists

    A pocket or a wall that accepts the stack. Without depth, the promise of an empty floor is false.

  • The slab takes both conditions

    A closed stack and an open load. If the calculation rejects either, the system is not bought.

  • Not permanent use with no operator

    A hall seated every day with nobody trained. Fixed is more honest and moves less.

Where it is used

  • Multipurpose sports halls

    A stand on the long wall that opens for a match and closes for an exam or an exhibition.

  • School halls

    Provided there is training and inspection. Movement without a procedure is an incident.

  • Flexible theatres, carefully

    The seat may be a hall seat, not a stadium shell. A stage floor and a slope may not suit the track.

  • Where it should not go

    A floor sloped for drainage, a weak slab, or a hall with no storage pocket.

Parts of the solution

  • The sliding units

    Rows that nest. Count and depth come from the system document for that height.

  • Wheels or track

    On the floor or in a trench. Debris and a tile joint stop them.

  • Locks

    They hold the open and closed positions. A half-open state is not a use.

  • Interlock and alarm

    Stops movement with an obstruction or with the public present, or alarms throughout the travel.

  • Seats on the row

    Chosen for the hall type. An open-stadium seat is not automatically a hall seat.

Engineering considerations

  • Two loads on the slab

    The concentrated stack and the open load. Both are in the structural calculation. This page has no kilonewton.

  • Flatness

    The manufacturer tolerance plus slab deflection. Adding them is a joint decision, not a figure here.

  • The travel zone

    Clear of a platform, a cable, and a chair. The width comes from the system document and is not guessed.

  • Safety while moving

    A trained operator, a view, and a stop. Speed comes from the document. This page gives no metres per second.

  • When fixed is more honest

    No depth, or no crew, or a slab that rejects the track, or a use that does not need the floor cleared.

Materials

  • The unit frame

    From one system. A row from another product changes the nesting and the load.

  • Wheels

    A material that suits the floor. A hard wheel on a stage timber, or a soft one in debris, suffers.

  • Paint or galvanizing

    A wet hall or a pool is the galvanizing page’s question. Coating cracked at a joint rusts first.

Related systems

  • The slab

    Designed for both conditions. A movement joint does not sit under a wheel without a detail.

  • Power

    A motor, if fitted, is a circuit and a lock. Manual pulling is also a procedure, not an assumption that it is easier without training.

  • Exits in both positions

    Open, it is a stand with its aisles. Closed, the stack does not block an emergency door.

  • Seats and sightline

    A moving riser is still a sightline. C-value does not disappear because the system folds.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Prove the floor must be cleared and that someone will move the stand.

  2. 02

    Ask the stack depth for that row count and compare it with the architectural pocket.

  3. 03

    Give the structural engineer the stack load, the open load, and the flatness tolerance.

  4. 04

    Draw the travel zone and the emergency doors in both positions.

  5. 05

    If depth, crew, or slab is missing, move to fixed instead of editing the promise.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The floor is levelled or the track is installed before the units. The telescopic stand does not repair a random slope.

  2. 02

    Units are installed from one system and the locks are proven in both positions.

  3. 03

    Interlocks are tested with a trial obstruction, not with the public.

  4. 04

    The operator is trained on sight and stop before handover.

  5. 05

    The actual stack depth is handed over against the pocket. The difference is resolved before opening.

Testing

  • A full cycle

    Open and close with no unintended stop. Noise and skew are recorded.

  • The obstruction

    Movement stops or alarms as designed. A test with nothing in the way is not enough.

  • The slab

    No visible deflection that leaves the manufacturer tolerance under the stack. The verdict is shared with the structural engineer.

Commissioning

  • A written procedure

    Who operates, when the zone is cleared, and where they stand. Without a procedure the system is a machine with no owner.

  • Both positions documented

    The open-position aisles and the closed-position path relative to the doors.

  • No speed from this page

    Movement limits come from the manufacturer document and the machinery standard if one is adopted.

Maintenance

  • Wheels and locks

    Inspected because travel repeats. A damaged wheel skews the row and can stop movement in front of the public if ignored.

  • Track cleanliness

    Grit and adhesive tape stop a unit. Clean before each cycle of use, not only once a year.

  • Later training

    A new operator repeats the procedure. Oral memory is not a handover.

Common mistakes

  • An empty floor on paper

    The stack stayed in the section. The empty day is not empty.

  • An ordinary slab

    Designed for a room, not for a stack. Deflection stops the wheels or opens the row.

  • Movement with no operator

    A button with no view. The obstruction is a person.

  • Telescopic treated as fixed

    Left open permanently with no track maintenance. You bought a mechanism you neither use nor inspect.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

PathFits whenWeakens when
TelescopicThe floor changes job, and depth, crew, and slab agree.Daily use with no operator or no pocket. The movement becomes an abandoned hazard.
FixedNo need to clear the floor, or the telescopic option is not honest about depth or operation.When the hall is truly multipurpose and the conditions exist. Fixed steals the floor.
DemountableThe event is days long and the stand leaves the building.When the need is weekly inside the same hall. A full strike is slower than sliding.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Does the stack vanish on the empty-floor day?

No. It remains a depth in the hall. If the pocket is not drawn, the floor is not empty.

Does any slab work?

No. The concentrated stack and the open load are calculated. Deflection stops the movement.

Who moves the stand?

A trained person who can see the path, with a procedure. A button with no view is not enough.

When do I choose fixed instead?

If seating is daily, or there is no depth, no crew, or the slab rejects the track.

Is safety during movement a detail?

No. Interlocks and the clear zone are part of the design. An opening test in an empty hall does not replace an obstruction test.

Technical sources

  • The manufacturer system document

    Depth, floor tolerance, travel speed, and stack load. This page does not carry the figures.

  • The structural slab calculation

    Deflection under the stack and under the open stand. This page gives no kilonewton.

  • The applicable machinery standard if adopted

    Interlock and inspection. This page declares no conformity and sets no inspection interval.
