Demountable grandstands

Each erection is a new structure until it is inspected. The ground differs, the wind differs, and pieces are swapped or lost. Ballast or a bolt is calculated for that condition, not for the truck’s memory. The public does not climb before the checklist is closed. This is not the fixed stand with a permanent foundation.

Definition

Demountable work is designed to be assembled repeatedly: posts, decks, braces, and pins from one system. Mixing two products changes the nesting and the load. Holding-down is either ballast that prevents overturning and sliding, or a mechanical fixing into a foundation that can take it. The calculation includes the crowd as the adopted code defines it, wind if it is exposed, and the ground that carries it: soft soil is not a hall slab. This page gives neither a ballast kilogram nor a kilonewton.

Inspection before every use counts again: a missing pin, a cracked deck, a lock left open, ballast moved to clear an aisle. The previous erection file is evidence of that day and that site, not of today. After strike, damaged pieces are reviewed before they return to the store as if sound. The design life of a component is not the life of a fixed building. This page does not name it in years.

When is this service needed?

  • An event, then the stand leaves

    Days on a site, then strike. A permanent foundation is not justified.

  • The ground changes

    A park, then a street, then a hall. Each ground is a new holding-down calculation.

  • Repeated assembly

    Pieces wear. Checking every time is a condition, not a first-year procedure.

  • Not the stand for the whole season

    If it stays for months in the same place, the fixed question or the long temporary permit is reopened. This page does not set the legal duration.

Where it is used

  • Outdoor events

    Wind, crowd, and ground unknown until the survey. Ballast or a stake according to the calculation.

  • Halls with no permanent stand

    Floor load and height limit from the hall manual. The truck does not cancel the manual.

  • School events and temporary grounds

    A crowd not trained to the aisle. Marks and barriers are clearer than on a familiar permanent stand.

  • Not a substitute for a fixed stadium

    A full season and repeated thousands need a foundation and a facility inspection, not a weekly erection from memory.

Parts of the solution

  • Frame and decks

    From one engineered system. A piece borrowed from another system is not fitted.

  • Pins and locks

    Counted and locked. A missing one stops the erection. It is not replaced with a workshop bolt.

  • Ballast or anchor

    A mass or a fixing calculated for the condition. An empty barrel is not ballast.

  • Barriers and stairs

    Part of the system, not an added rope. Load and width come from the adopted code.

  • The checklist

    For this ground and this day. It is closed before climbing.

Engineering considerations

  • Overturning and sliding

    A crowd on one side and wind. Holding-down covers both with the factor method the standard and the engineer of record state. This page does not quote the factor.

  • The ground

    Its capacity is surveyed. Wet grass is not a concrete hall. The calculation changes.

  • Repeated assembly

    The hole ovalises and the pin wears. The rejection limit comes from the system manual, not from visual guesswork alone.

  • Fire and exit

    Even the temporary stand has an exit and a width. The local code or the event guide names the limit. This page gives no metre.

  • Not the fixed stand

    No permanent foundation, less certainty in the support, and a clearer, shorter responsibility on erection day.

Materials

  • System pieces

    Marked and traceable. What is broken is quarantined in the store.

  • Ballast

    A known mass, not a guess that it is “heavy”. Water in a tank is calculated full and empty so it is not drained in secret.

  • The deck

    A slip-resistant surface with no rotten board from a previous wet erection. What is damaged leaves the set.

Related systems

  • Seats

    Fixed to the system. A fabric theatre seat on an exposed temporary deck is the wrong assumption.

  • Temporary power

    A cable does not cross the walking tread. A trip is part of the incident.

  • Emergency

    An aisle stays open at erection and is not closed by an equipment case.

  • Weather

    A wind limit for stopping, from the calculation or the system manual. This page gives no speed.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Fix the duration, the ground, the crowd, and the expected wind.

  2. 02

    Choose one engineered system and obtain the assembly manual and the component limits.

  3. 03

    Calculate holding-down for this ground. Do not copy ballast from a previous site.

  4. 04

    Place the inspection before the public-entry schedule with enough margin to repair.

  5. 05

    Plan the strike: where damaged pieces go so they are not returned as sound by a lie.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The ground is surveyed and compared with the calculation’s assumption. Mud is not the drawing.

  2. 02

    Assemble to the manual. A missing pin stops the row.

  3. 03

    Ballast is placed or anchors are tightened, and the mass or the torque the manual requires is recorded.

  4. 04

    Barriers and stairs are locked before any trial climb.

  5. 05

    The checklist is closed with a signature from the person who may stop the erection.

Testing

  • Completeness of pieces

    Count pins and braces against the drawing. A shortage is a failure.

  • Holding-down

    Ballast is in place and not casually movable, or the anchor is tight. No invented load test on this page.

  • Aisles

    The width is clear of cases. Measure to the event guide or the code.

Commissioning

  • Permission to climb

    After inspection only. The rehearsal does not come before the signature.

  • A weather limit is announced

    Who stops use if the wind rises. The name is written.

  • This erection’s file

    Ground, holding-down, and inspection. Last month’s file is attached as history, not as permission.

Maintenance

  • Between erections

    Quarantine what is damaged and replace the stretched pin. The store is not a graveyard of pieces.

  • During the event

    A short look after a dense crowd or rain: locks and ballast that have not been moved.

  • After strike

    Mud is cleaned and the deck is checked. A doubtful piece does not travel to the next city.

Common mistakes

  • An old certificate

    Another site and another day. It is not today’s permission.

  • Mixing two systems

    A pin that looks close enough. Load and nesting do not match.

  • Ballast that gets moved

    To clear a door or to pass a truck, then forgotten. Overturning returns.

  • Treating it as fixed

    No inspection because “it looks the same”. The same shape on other ground is another structure.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

PathFits whenWeakens when
DemountableDays on the site, then it leaves, with an inspection at every erection.When it stays a full season. A foundation and permanent inspection are more honest.
FixedContinuous use and a designed foundation.For a passing event. You build a foundation you do not need and then leave it.
TelescopicThe same hall every week and a floor designed to slide.When the stand travels. A telescopic unit does not strike onto a truck easily.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is last year’s inspection enough?

No. Each erection is inspected on its ground. The old file is history, not permission.

Is ballast any heavy weight?

No. A calculated mass in the calculated place, and it is not drained or moved. An empty barrel weighs empty.

May I mix pieces from two similar systems?

No. Nesting and load are not shared. A visual likeness is not a manual.

When is demountable the wrong choice?

When the stand stays and is used continuously. Fixed, with its foundation and inspection, is more honest than an erection repeated from memory.

Is the safety factor written here?

No. The applicable standard and the engineer of record define it. This page does not quote a figure as if it were the law.

Technical sources

  • The temporary-stand system manual

    Assembly, component limits, and typical holding-down under its conditions. This page carries no ballast mass.

  • The local code and the event guide

    Crowd, exit, and a procedural wind limit if one exists. This page does not replace them.

  • The engineer of record for this erection

    The calculation of ground and overturning, and the safety factor if a standard states one. This page does not quote the factor.
