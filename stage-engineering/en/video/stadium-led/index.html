Stadium LED

The furthest seat owns the picture, not the seat at the touchline. The ribbon and the large board are two questions: distance, luminance from the outdoor-face sheet, and maintenance that does not close the stand. The indoor page is not copied here.

Definition

A stand ribbon is a long narrow board read during play. A scoreboard is larger and may carry replay. Both are exposed or nearly exposed: water, dust, heat, and a sun angle that moves. A coarser pitch is acceptable when the last row is far, and rejected when the text is small.

Height on the section is drawn from the last-row eye over the heads, not from a reference photograph. The theatre C-value is not the millimetres of this bowl. The code owns barriers and exits.

When is this service needed?

  • Before the last tier is frozen

    Distance changes with height. The screen is not stuck on after the stand is poured, like a sticker.

  • Direct sun for part of the day

    An indoor face visually dies. The sheet has to say outdoor.

  • A season that does not stop

    A module is swapped at night or it is not swapped. The plan is written.

  • A score, not only a film

    Small numerals want a finer pitch than a wide view of the pitch.

Where it is used

  • An open stand

    Dust and water. Drainage is also a seat question, and the exposed-seat page is separate.

  • A roofed stand without walls

    Sun enters at an angle. Do not assume indoor.

  • A board at the end of the field

    It can cut a sightline from the end. Draw it.

  • Not a closed box

    The box is the indoor LED page if the distance is a few metres.

Parts of the solution

  • An outdoor face

    From a sheet that names weather, not from a conference sheet.

  • A wind frame

    The load is the engineer’s. This page gives no wind speed.

  • Protected power

    Water and dust are a fault path. The detail is from the specification, not from a picture.

  • Maintenance access

    A ladder or a gangway that does not steal the public exit.

Engineering considerations

  • The last row

    It is the design distance for small text. Midfield is a deception.

  • Pitch estimate

    3.4 m per mm remains an acuity estimate. Sun is a separate luminance question, with no nit value here.

  • Glare

    The sun angle on the face is reviewed on the section, not on a night render.

  • Sound

    The screen does not correct stand echo. Isolation and treatment are other questions.

  • The exposed seat

    ES-500 and its relatives are a seat sheet. Do not mix them with the panel sheet.

Materials

  • Sealed cabinets

    The sealing grade comes from the product sheet, not from this page.

  • Frame coating

    Galvanizing is a stand page. Here the talk is only the screen support.

  • A protective face if there is one

    It can change glare. It is named on the sheet.

Related systems

  • Replay

    Picture delay against sound is reviewed if commentary is live.

  • Score control

    A named operator. It is not only an advertising screen if the contract is a score.

  • Emergency

    An evacuation message belongs to the code’s system, not to the advertising operator.

  • Floodlights

    They can wash the face. The aim is drawn.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Fix the last-row eye and the first-row eye on the section.

  2. 02

    Separate the ribbon from the scoreboard: distance and reading differ.

  3. 03

    Ask for an outdoor-face sheet. Reject an indoor sheet.

  4. 04

    Draw maintenance access without cutting the exit.

  5. 05

    Do not write a nit value into the specification from this page.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The frame is given to the engineer before the panels.

  2. 02

    Cabinets are jointed and water is tested where the sheet asks for it.

  3. 03

    The real score text is shown from the last row in daylight.

  4. 04

    A window for swapping a module inside the season is scheduled.

  5. 05

    The cooling face is not covered with solid advertising boards.

Testing

  • Daylight

    Reading under the expected sun, not only at night.

  • Rain or a wash

    Where the face claims it on its sheet. This page gives no IP rating.

  • The last row

    A small numeral is read or rejected.

Commissioning

  • Match content

    A score and a replay, not the factory film.

  • The operator

    A name and a procedure. A screen without a procedure is a dead board at half-time.

  • Spares

    A store on site, not in another city.

Maintenance

  • Season dust

    A cleaning schedule that does not wait for summer.

  • A dead module on match day

    Either a written swap or an accepted blot. Settled before the season.

  • A sponsor change

    It does not close the ventilation and does not change the pitch.

Common mistakes

  • A conference pitch on the stand

    Cost without benefit if the last row reads a large numeral, or the reverse if the text is small and the distance was not calculated.

  • An indoor face in the sun

    It goes dark. The sheet is the judge.

  • Maintenance without access

    The season leaves the module dead.

  • Treating the screen as a sufficient sightline

    The spectator came for the field. The board does not repair a column that cuts the field.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Do I copy the hall pitch to the stadium?

No. Distance and sun change. 1.5–2.5 mm is the nearby-indoor band, not the stand.

Is there a recommended nit value?

Not on this page. The outdoor-face sheet at the content is the source.

Does the ribbon replace the scoreboard?

Not if the contract is replay and large numerals. Both displays are named.

Who calculates wind?

The engineer of record. This page gives no speed.

Where is the stand seat?

In the seat catalogue and on the stadium-seat page. ES-500 is a dimension sheet, not a screen sheet.

Technical sources

  • The last-row section

    The reading distance. This page does not replace it with a general figure.

  • The outdoor-face sheet

    Weather, luminance and power. No nit value is copied here.

  • The adopted stand code

    Barriers, exits and access. The screen does not cancel them.
