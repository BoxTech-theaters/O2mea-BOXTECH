Laser projectors

Laser cancels neither throw ratio, nor screen gain, nor house light. It usually changes source life and the kind of maintenance as the device sheet says, not as this page promising hours. The seat near the booth hears the fan. That is a brief item.

Definition

Throw ratio is projector-to-screen distance divided by image width. The figure comes from the lens table at the shift required. Short and long are two geometry pages, and a laser may sit on either.

Light at the eye depends on lumens stated on a sheet measured by a named method (such as the ISO 21118 family for projectors where it applies), and on image size, screen gain, and ambient light. This page turns that neither into nits nor into a lumen band.

When is this service needed?

  • Lamp maintenance is the complaint

    Laser is a discussion about replacing the source, not about image geometry alone.

  • A booth exists or is required

    The device is heard and it heats. The seat stuck to the port pays.

  • A large picture without an LED wall

    The optical path has to stay clear of heads and rigging.

  • Not a substitute for measuring contrast

    A lit hall can stay washed out. Laser is not a curtain.

Where it is used

  • A lecture hall

    Text on a screen. The lights dim or the contrast fails.

  • A theatre

    A rear booth or a device in the house. The noise differs.

  • Home cinema and a box

    Short throw may fit better. A separate page.

  • Not a sunny stadium

    Projection there loses to the sun unless a sheet proves otherwise, and this page is not that sheet.

Parts of the solution

  • The device and its lens

    The table ties distance to width and to shift.

  • The screen

    Gain, direction, and acoustic transparency if the sound is behind it.

  • The path

    Clear of heads, chandeliers, and rigging.

  • Thermal and acoustic isolation of the booth

    If there is one. The door and the duct, not the glass alone.

Engineering considerations

  • No lumen band here

    Figures such as 6 to 20 thousand circulate in the market. This page rejects them because they have no image size and no ambient.

  • ISO 21118 where it applies

    A method for measuring device output, not seat brightness.

  • Noise

    The device sheet in decibels under its conditions. Acceptance is a brief criterion, not a number we pick.

  • Keystone

    Optical shift before digital correction. Correction eats pixels.

  • Safety

    The laser class comes from the device sheet and the code. This page classifies no device.

Materials

  • The screen surface

    Higher gain is not always clearer. The angle narrows.

  • Booth glass

    It absorbs light and reflects. It is counted in the path.

  • The device mount

    Roof vibration shows on a large picture.

Related systems

  • Control

    On, off, and a source. Not DMX unless the device says so.

  • Audio

    Picture delay against the array is reviewed in cinema and lectures.

  • Cooling air

    It is not dumped on the nearest seat.

  • Emergency light

    From the code. The projector is not escape lighting.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Fix the image width and the device position before the name of the source.

  2. 02

    Read the lens table. Reject a throw with no shift.

  3. 03

    Put noise and heat on the booth seat, not in the general specification.

  4. 04

    Name the ambient: a lit lecture is not a dark film.

  5. 05

    Do not write lumens from this page.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The path is built and confirmed clear before the device is hung.

  2. 02

    The lens is the one in the table, not the nearest in the box.

  3. 03

    Shift is set optically before any digital correction.

  4. 04

    Noise is measured at the agreed seat if it is an acceptance item.

  5. 05

    The source sheet is handed over as the manufacturer printed it, not as a marketing summary.

Testing

  • From the seat

    Text at the true size under show lighting.

  • The path

    No head shadow and no rigging shadow.

  • Noise

    If it is written in the brief. The method is named.

Commissioning

  • The named source

    Laser or lamp as installed. It is not swapped in silence.

  • Focus and edges

    From the edges, not only from the middle of the screen.

  • What is not accepted

    A factory picture on a slide, or lumens kept in memory.

Maintenance

  • Filters and the thermal path

    From the device manual. This page gives no months.

  • Dust on the lens

    It shows on the large picture before it is seen on the device.

  • Source replacement

    The manufacturer’s procedure and the laser class. Not necessarily the old lamp routine.

Common mistakes

  • Laser means a daytime picture

    Ambient still wins if the hall is bright and the screen gain is small.

  • Copying lumens from another job

    Size and distance differ.

  • A device over the heads with no noise in the brief

    The seat complains after opening.

  • Heavy digital keystone

    Edges break and the text is lost.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

How many lumens do I need?

No answer from this page. Size, gain, ambient, and the device sheet. Copied market bands are rejected here.

Is a laser silent?

No. The fan remains. The nearby seat hears it if the booth is not isolated.

Does it replace LED?

When the path is clean and the light is controlled. Not when sun or a bright hall beats the screen.

Where is short throw?

On its own page. A laser may sit on a short or a long lens. Geometry is not the source.

Is this the commercial cinema page?

No. The auditorium and isolation between houses are on the cinema design page. Here the light source and its maintenance.
