Reverberation time RT60

RT60 is the time for energy to fall 60 dB after the source stops, and it is not one number for the hall. A serious measurement reads it in bands, and T20 or T30 are extrapolated because noise hides the full fall. ISO 3382-1 describes that for performance spaces.

Definition

The definition ties the time to a fall, not to the operating level. A speech room and an unamplified music room of the same volume are not discussed with the same tail: speech wants the syllable to die, and music may want the energy to remain. This page sets no universal target in seconds for any hall type. A mid-band figure opened in a meeting is a discussion target until the brief and the measurement fix it.

The metric Sabine equation RT = 0.161 V / A ties the time to volume V and to total absorption A. The picture is sound: a larger volume lengthens the time if absorption per square metre stays put. The formula assumes a diffuse field and modest, distributed absorption. When panels cover a large area, or absorber gathers in the ceiling, it drifts from measurement. Eyring handles a higher mean and is still not the room.

When is this service needed?

  • Before volume is frozen

    Added height lengthens RT60 if absorption does not rise. The architectural decision comes before the panels.

  • When the programme differs

    A lecture by day and music at night in the same hall. One figure does not serve both settings, and variable acoustics may be the discussion.

  • After a finish change

    A mineral tile, a curtain, or a carpet changes A in a band, not in every band. Recalculate, then measure.

  • Before judging the sound system

    STI is affected by the tail and by noise. Raising the gain does not shorten RT60. IEC 60268-16 is a separate question.

Where it is used

  • Performance spaces

    Theatres and music rooms, where ISO 3382-1 is the reference for measuring parameters, including decay.

  • Speech rooms

    Lecture and conference. Discussion tends toward a shorter mid-band tail so syllables separate. The value lives in the brief, not here.

  • Multipurpose halls

    A compromise, or curtains and movable absorption. Each setting is measured. No setting is perfect for both programmes.

  • Small rooms

    Modes show, and Sabine is weaker. Measurement and a geometric model matter more than one second.

Parts of the solution

  • Volume

    V in the formula. More seats in the same volume tend toward a shorter time because the audience is absorption, if the surfaces stay.

  • Absorption A

    Areas times frequency coefficients from ISO 354 reports. Without those data A is unknown. This page does not fill the table.

  • Distribution

    Scattered absorption is closer to the Sabine assumption. Concentrated absorption leaves live surfaces and late paths.

  • Audience and seats

    Occupancy is part of the criterion. Empty and full are two figures. The report states which was measured.

  • Noise that hides the fall

    T20 and T30 exist because a full 60 dB may not be visible above HVAC. The method is named.

Engineering considerations

  • Frequency dependence

    Bass longer than the mid band is common in rooms untreated at low frequency. An average of 500–1000 Hz hides it.

  • Sabine at high absorption

    When mean α is large or absorber covers most of the surface, the field is not diffuse and the result drifts from 0.161 V / A.

  • Volume and use together

    A larger hall tends toward a longer time for the same finish. Speech and music do not share one target because of that. There is no universal table here.

  • Clarity is not the time

    C50 and STI describe speech in other ways. A short RT60 with high noise can still fail understanding.

  • Prediction then measurement

    The calculator on this site is instructional. ISO 3382-1 is the measurement in a performance space. This page does not perform the standard.

Materials

  • Live surfaces

    Solid plaster, dense wood, and glass reflect. Keeping them is a decision about the tail, not a forgotten treatment.

  • The absorber and its build-up

    Thickness, cavity, and face change the band. Thin PET felt is not bass treatment. The coefficient comes from the report.

  • Variable elements

    Heavy curtains and rotating panels. Measure them open and closed. The mechanism’s name is not two figures.

Related systems

  • Treatment

    Panels are one way to change A. Coverage on the drawing is not the result.

  • Isolation

    More isolating walls can reflect more inside. Isolating the neighbour does not shorten the room’s tail.

  • The sound system

    A Line Array or a Point Source lights surfaces. Poor aim asks absorption to hide a mistake.

  • HVAC

    The noise floor decides whether T30 is valid. Lowering noise is part of being able to measure, not of elegance.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Name the use, the volume, and the occupancy before any second.

  2. 02

    Ask for a discussion target curve in bands, not one figure copied to every space.

  3. 03

    Build A from reports of the actual build-ups. Reject a coefficient with no frequency.

  4. 04

    Use Sabine or Eyring as a guess and state its limit. If absorption is high, move to a model that does not assume a diffuse field.

  5. 05

    Fix the measurement method: ISO 3382-1 where it applies, the positions, and the condition. The calculator is not acceptance.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Do not swap one absorber for another because the colour is closer. The band changes.

  2. 02

    Cavities are built as in the report the α came from.

  3. 03

    Seats are installed before an occupied measurement, or simulated as agreed, and the difference is stated.

  4. 04

    Doors are closed and HVAC is set to show mode before the decay is recorded.

  5. 05

    The calculation model is kept with the material revision so the measurement is compared with the hypothesis, not with memory.

Testing

  • ISO 3382-1

    The performance-space method: source, positions, and bands. This page does not run the measurement.

  • T20 and T30

    A partial fall scaled to 60 dB. The report states which was used and why noise prevented the full fall.

  • More than one seat

    A position at the source or under a balcony does not represent the hall. The spread between positions is part of the result.

Commissioning

  • Comparison with the brief

    Each band on its own. Success in the mid band with a failure at 125 Hz is not a silent pass.

  • The named condition

    Empty, full, or curtains open. A figure without a condition is not handed over.

  • What is not accepted

    A phone application, a single clap, or a printed Sabine calculation. None of these is an ISO 3382-1 handover.

Maintenance

  • Later finishes

    A carpet, a curtain, or a new ceiling changes A. Review again before the sound system is blamed.

  • A different audience

    A standing event is not upholstered seats. Absorption changed even if volume did not.

  • Damaged panels

    A painted face or a closed cavity changes the band that was designed. Repair matches the report.

Common mistakes

  • One second for the whole project

    A lecture, a sports hall, and music do not share a target. One figure deletes use and volume.

  • Sabine after dense coverage

    The formula is used where its assumption is weak, then the team is surprised by measurement. The formula’s limit is written with the result.

  • Reading only the mid band

    The bass stays long and speech sounds coloured despite a neat mid-band figure.

  • Mixing RT60 with STI

    A shorter time does not guarantee understanding if noise is high. The index is in IEC 60268-16.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is there a correct value for every theatre?

No. Volume and programme change the discussion. This page rejects a universal target. The brief writes the band curve.

Why not trust Sabine?

Because it assumes low, distributed absorption. A hall covered in panels breaks the assumption. Measurement shows the gap.

Is T30 the same as RT60?

T30 estimates the full fall from part of the curve, inside the standard’s method. The report names the symbol. Do not mix the result with a Sabine calculation without saying so.

Is the calculator on the site enough?

No. It applies the formula to numbers you type. Replace the coefficients with laboratory reports, then with an ISO 3382-1 measurement.

Does a short RT60 mean a high STI?

Not always. Noise lowers the index. STI is in IEC 60268-16. The time is part of the story.
