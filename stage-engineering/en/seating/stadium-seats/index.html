Stadium seats

An outdoor seat is a shell that takes sun, water, and sometimes standing, and it is fixed to the riser, not to a hall’s timber floor. The row letter and seat number stay readable after a season. A theatre chair, with its fabric, tip mechanism, and inner foam, assumes a closed building. This page declares conformity to no part of an EN.

Definition

The difference from a theatre seat is exposure, fixing, vandalism, and drainage. An exposed polymer or metal fades and embrittles unless UV resistance is named on the material sheet for the site, not as a sentence on this page. Rain needs a path off the tread — a hole or a gap — so water does not pool. On a covered stand the same hole can drip onto the row below, and that is coordinated.

Riser mounting: the hole pattern from the manufacturer, with edge distance and bolt diameter from the structural drawing. The seat is not drilled into a weak concrete edge because the picture wants alignment. The row letter is engraved or fixed so it resists cleaning and sun, and it matches the tickets. Vandal resistance means parts that do not come off by hand in general admission, not a fabric that can be slit. If EN 13200 or another standard is adopted, that is a project declaration against the edition, not a certificate here.

When is this service needed?

  • An open or partly open stand

    Sun and rain directly. An indoor hall seat is the wrong assumption.

  • Fixing to a concrete or steel riser

    Before holes are cast or the edge is finished. Redrilling weakens the concrete cover.

  • General admission that sometimes stands

    Load and vandalism are higher than a roofed hospitality seat. The specification differs.

  • A clash with a theatre seat

    When a fabric tip-up is offered for the bowl. Reject it, or move it to a closed club with its reports.

Where it is used

  • Open stadiums

    Sun, surface temperature, and rain. Drainage and the row letter are part of the seat.

  • Covered stands

    There is still diffuse light and humidity. A hole that drains may drip. Check the row below.

  • Enclosed hospitality

    The seat may move toward an indoor specification, but fixing and fire remain report items, not a sentence here.

  • A temporary stand

    The seat is fitted and removed. Loss and vandalism are higher. The demountable page continues the question.

Parts of the solution

  • The shell

    An exposed material with weathering data from the sheet. This page gives no fade years.

  • The fixing

    Bolts or clips to the manufacturer pattern and an edge distance from the structural engineer.

  • Drainage

    A hole or a gap in the tread. Drawn with the water direction.

  • Numbering

    A row letter and a number that resist UV and cleaning and can be read from the aisle.

  • Anti-vandal parts

    Parts that do not come off without a tool in general admission. Loose fabric is not the assumption.

Engineering considerations

  • UV and heat

    The material sheet for the site conditions. The sentence “UV resistant” without an exposure report is not a specification.

  • Water

    No ponding on the sitting surface or the walking tread. The hole is not deleted at finishing.

  • The riser edge

    Edge distance and embedment come from the structural drawing. The seat does not choose them.

  • Standing

    A crowd may stand on the seat. The load comes from the adopted code, not from this page.

  • No EN from here

    The EN 13200 family is spectator documentation where it is adopted. This page does not certify conformity.

Materials

  • Exposed polymer

    UV stabilisers from the material sheet. Replacing with a closer colour and no data brings the fade back.

  • Exposed metal

    Galvanizing or paint is the grandstand page’s strategy, not only a colour. Rust at the hole is common if the coating is broken.

  • No theatre fabric

    Fabric and foam assume indoors. In open general admission they are slit and they hold water.

Related systems

  • The grandstand

    The riser, the aisle, and the barrier come before the seat. The chair does not design the steel.

  • Ticketing

    The letter and the number match the gate. Two different systems lose the crowd.

  • Sightline

    Riser height and C-value. A tall seat with no clearance hides the row behind.

  • Fire in the enclosed zone

    If the seat enters an enclosed hall, the report returns. This page does not give it.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Separate open from enclosed. Do not copy the theatre specification.

  2. 02

    Take the hole pattern from the manufacturer and the edge distance from the structural engineer before the riser is approved.

  3. 03

    Draw the water path off the tread, including the effect of a hole on the row below.

  4. 04

    Name numbering with the tickets, and the letter height readable from the aisle if the code states one.

  5. 05

    Write vandal resistance for general admission with no claim of a standard that is not adopted.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Risers are drilled at the approved positions, not by guessing alignment on site.

  2. 02

    Coating or galvanizing is repaired at the hole by the named method. A bare hole rusts.

  3. 03

    Shells are fitted and holes are checked so mortar has not blocked them.

  4. 04

    Letters are fixed after the final clean so it does not erase them.

  5. 05

    A whole row is checked from the aisle: number legibility and line straightness.

Testing

  • The fixing

    A torque sample or a visual check as the project specification says. This page gives no torque.

  • Drainage

    Water on the tread leaves and does not pool. If it drips on the row below, that is recorded and treated.

  • Numbering

    It is read from the aisle and matches the plan. No UV test is invented here.

Commissioning

  • A hole drawing handed over

    Pattern and edge as built. A difference from the manufacturer drawing is explained.

  • No EN certificate

    This page does not hand over conformity. If a declaration exists, it is from the responsible party against the adopted edition.

  • Spares

    A shell, a number, and a fixing from the same system. Mixing another product changes the hole and the colour.

Maintenance

  • Uneven fade

    Compare with the material sheet, not with the catalogue colour expected years later. Replace in a consistent batch.

  • Blocked holes

    Cleaning restores drainage. A hole closed by paint spray brings ponding back.

  • Missing numbers

    Replace them from the system. Handwriting does not match the gate.

Common mistakes

  • A theatre chair in the stadium

    Fabric, foam, and a tip-up under sun, rain, and standing. Exposure, drainage, and vandalism fail.

  • A hole on the edge

    A pretty alignment breaks the concrete cover. Distance comes from the structural engineer.

  • An EN claim

    A conformity sentence with no edition and no party’s declaration. This page rejects it.

  • A hole sealed for looks

    Water pools on the tread. Drainage is not a visual defect.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

An open-bowl shellSun, rain, general admission, and a fixing to the riser.In a closed hall that needs fabric and a tip-up. A plastic shell is the wrong seat there.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why not use the theatre seat?

Because the fabric, foam, and tip mechanism were not designed for sun, rain, standing, and vandalism. The assumption is a closed building.

Is the hole a defect?

Not if it is for drainage. Sealing it pools water. On a covered stand, confirm it does not drip on the head below without a treatment.

Who sets the bolt position?

The manufacturer pattern with edge distance from the structural engineer. Visual alignment does not move closer to the concrete edge on its own.

Are you conformant to an EN?

This page claims no conformity. If a spectator standard is adopted, the declaration is a project document against its edition.

The letters vanished after a season. What does that mean?

The numbering was not for the stand’s climate, or cleaning removed it. Replace it with a system that resists exposure and matches the tickets.
