Auditorium seats

A lecture is longer sitting than a short scene, a hand that writes, and an eye that sees the speaker and the written surface together. The seat is relatively upright, and its tablet folds inside the width already counted. A deep recliner is less capacity, a harder aisle, and one viewing target: the screen.

Definition

An auditorium seat differs from a theatre tip-up if a writing tablet, sometimes a power outlet, and a double sightline target are added. The open tablet must not become the exit width. Its folded envelope is measured with the tipped seat, or as the code asks. This page gives no code millimetre. Fire is still the build-up’s report, and the tablet is part of that build-up if it is fixed to the tested seat.

Long sitting means a back and a seat front reviewed for hours, not only for a short turn, without turning the seat into a sofa that swallows the row. The speech sightline sees the mouth, and the board or screen sightline may be to the side or higher. A seat aimed only at centre stage can miss a side board. A cinema recliner solves another target: a film, no writing, a larger row depth, and fewer seats per square metre.

When is this service needed?

  • The lecture is the programme

    Writing, duration, and a speaker. The seat is drawn for that, not for a cinema picture.

  • A tablet per seat or per row

    Before pitch is frozen. An open tablet consumes depth if it was not reserved.

  • Side screens or boards

    A second sightline. Aiming the seat at one target leaves the other outside the eye.

  • The recliner temptation

    When “cinema comfort” is asked in a teaching hall. Capacity, aisle, and tablet fall back. The decision is explicit.

Where it is used

  • University lecture rooms

    Long rows and boards. Regular access matters more than one-night plushness.

  • Conference halls with a seated audience

    A full-day session. The back, and a tablet if notes are taken. Not a standing exhibition seat.

  • School halls

    Sometimes a smaller body size, and harsher daily use and fire. The report is still for the build-up.

  • Not a pure cinema

    Cinema has its own page. The recliner is that room’s decision, not the assumption here.

Parts of the solution

  • Seat and back

    Upright enough to write. Too much recline moves the eye off the board and shortens the row.

  • Writing tablet

    A stated side, and it folds without staying in the aisle. Its surface does not cut the knee behind if the design can avoid it.

  • Centre and row pitch

    Wider than a short-stay seat if writing is required, and narrower than a recliner. No code figure here.

  • Power if it is required

    In the riser, not across the tread. A loose cable is an obstruction and a trip.

  • Numbering and access

    As in a theatre: tickets, a companion, and a sightline. A lecture does not waive the code.

Engineering considerations

  • The tablet envelope

    Open and folded. Egress uses the condition the code names, often folded with the seat tipped.

  • Two targets for the eye

    The speaker and the surface. A seat that sees only one is half a hall.

  • Duration

    Hours of sitting change what the back is expected to do. This page has no standard hour rating.

  • Fire

    Tablet, plastic, and fabric are one build-up. The report, not the picture. This page has no class.

  • Sound

    A speech room: RT60 and STI after the seats exist, not before. An occupied seat absorbs.

Materials

  • Tablet surface

    Stiffness and scratch resistance from the product sheet. A soft surface waves under writing and fails in a week.

  • Fabric or shell

    Daily use is harsher than a night theatre. The report is still the fire condition.

  • The fold mechanism

    Cycles from the manufacturer sheet. This page does not invent a count.

Related systems

  • Board or screen

    The bottom of the image above the front sightline. The display page does not compensate for a badly aimed seat.

  • Sound and speech

    STI to IEC 60268-16 if understanding is the criterion. The seat does not fix HVAC noise.

  • Light on the tablet

    Writing needs light on the lap without glaring the speaker. Coordinate with lighting.

  • The row exit

    An open tablet on an exam day narrows passing. The fold procedure is part of operation.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Name the tasks: listening, writing, seeing a side screen. Each task changes the aim.

  2. 02

    Reserve the tablet envelope inside the row pitch before publishing the count.

  3. 03

    Draw two sightlines: the mouth, and the bottom of the board or screen.

  4. 04

    Reject the recliner if count, aisle, and writing are constraints. Or state the waiver.

  5. 05

    Tie the fire report to the build-up, including the tablet if it is attached.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Tablets are fitted on the drawn side. A reversed row collides with the aisle.

  2. 02

    The fold is checked with the seat tipped before clear width is measured.

  3. 03

    Power in the riser is tested with no cable on the tread.

  4. 04

    Aim is checked from an end seat toward the board, not only from mid-hall.

  5. 05

    Numbering matches the timetable or the tickets if there are any.

Testing

  • The tablet in the aisle

    Folded, it does not stay inside the clear width. Open, it is not counted as exit width if the code forbids that.

  • The double view

    A sample of seats sees the speaker and the surface. Failure at the end is common and is corrected by aim, not by hope.

  • The report

    The installed build-up is the written build-up. No class is invented.

Commissioning

  • A fold procedure

    Handed to operations: when the tablet is folded before a mass exit.

  • Occupancy condition for sound

    If STI or RT60 is measured, the condition is stated. An empty seat is not lecture day.

  • No standard comfort hours here

    Duration is a brief discussion. This page does not rate the seat in hours.

Maintenance

  • The tablet hinge

    It loosens and stays in the aisle. Tighten or replace it before the width is counted wrong.

  • A scratched or broken surface

    Replace it with the report’s part, not with a harder untested board, if fire includes the surface.

  • A damaged outlet

    Isolate it. A substitute cable on the floor is not a repair.

Common mistakes

  • A cinema chair in a classroom

    Row depth jumps, the count collapses, and the tablet has nowhere to go. The comfort belonged to another programme.

  • A tablet with no envelope

    It is added after the drawing and eats the knee or the aisle. Pitch is redone.

  • Aim at the screen only

    The speaker at the side is outside the eye. A lecture sees the mouth and the surface.

  • Fire from the chair colour

    The class is the build-up’s report. This page does not give it.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why is a cinema chair not always more comfortable in the useful sense?

Because it consumes row depth, blocks the tablet, and cuts the count. Comfort that cancels writing is not lecture comfort.

Where does the tablet go at exit?

It folds out of the clear width the code defines. A tablet left open in an emergency aisle is an operations error and a design error.

Does the seat see the board and the speaker?

Both lines have to be drawn. A seat aimed at a centre screen can miss a side board. Do not assume.

Does fire include the tablet?

If it is fixed to the seat it is part of the tested build-up. A fabric report alone does not cover an added tablet. This page gives no class.

Do I write the long duration as a number in the specification?

Describe the day in the brief. There are no standard hours on this page that turn the seat into a time-rated product.
