Wall cladding

The face of the wall is not its behaviour. Solid wood of enough mass reflects in the speech range. The same wood, perforated, with an absorber and a cavity, absorbs in a band set by hole diameter, open area, and depth. Without those three the hole is ornament. Thin PET felt on a hard wall is weak at low frequency.

Definition

Cladding is a visible layer on a support. Acoustics ask whether the face is open to air, how thick it is, and what is behind it. The coefficient α comes from ISO 354 for a named mounting, with its frequencies. An open-area percentage on a drawing with no diameter and no cavity is not a specification. This page publishes no coefficient for any product and none for PET felt. At 100 Hz the wavelength is about 3.4 m, so a thin layer hard against the wall is not a bass tool.

Fire is the report of the assembly: face, adhesive, core, and substrate. A handsome veneer on an untested core inherits no class. The lower wall in an aisle is hit by a trolley and a chair. The impact layer may be acoustically harder than the upper wall, and the difference is calculated instead of assuming the whole wall absorbs. This page gives neither a fire class nor an impact joule.

When is this service needed?

  • Before the face is approved

    The cavity and the absorber can still be drawn. The picture alone is late.

  • A rear wall that echoes

    Cladding there is absorbent or tilted. On the side it may stay reflective to support speech.

  • An aisle that gets hit

    The lower wall is a layer that takes impact. The fragile acoustic face sits above hand height.

  • A fabric substitution

    It seals the pores or changes the fire behaviour. The report is tied again.

Where it is used

  • Hall walls

    Side, rear, and a low ceiling. The acoustic role differs in each place.

  • Public corridors

    Impact, cleaning, and exit fire. Absorption is secondary if it conflicts with those.

  • Rear theatre walls

    A late echo. Perforation without an absorber is not enough.

  • Short-life exhibition walls

    A visual skin may be enough. Do not call it acoustic without a build-up.

Parts of the solution

  • The face

    Wood, perforated metal, fabric, or PET. Open area and diameter if there is a hole.

  • The cavity

    A spacing off the hard surface. Changing it changes the band. It is written in millimetres on the detail, not here as a product figure.

  • The inner absorber

    Fibre or felt at the tested thickness. Its absence makes the hole a texture.

  • Support and fixing

    A frame that does not close the cavity and does not form a hard bridge over the absorber.

  • The impact layer

    At the bottom of the aisle, denser, to a height above the line a trolley hits.

Engineering considerations

  • The hole is not absorption

    Without a cavity and an absorber, energy returns from the hard back.

  • Low frequency

    It needs thickness or spacing. Thin PET on the wall does not treat 100 Hz because the wave is long.

  • Distribution

    A fully absorbent wall can kill a useful early reflection. Position comes from the section.

  • Fire for the assembly

    Face, adhesive, and core. This page has no class.

  • Impact

    The lower zone is separated on the detail. Assuming the same face from floor to ceiling breaks the bottom and changes the sound.

Materials

  • PET

    A common porous felt. α comes from the report of its thickness and mounting. The name is not enough.

  • Wood

    Solid and reflective, or perforated on condition of a cavity. A heavy varnish can seal the pores.

  • Fabric

    Open or closed. A closed weave kills the absorber behind it. The tested one is the one installed.

Related systems

  • Treatment and RT60

    Cladding is one way to change A. Coverage is not the time.

  • Isolation

    Internal cladding does not close flanking. Do not mix the two pages.

  • Lighting

    A spotlight punches the cavity or closes it. Its place is coordinated.

  • Furniture

    A cupboard in front of the absorber removes it from the calculation. The free face is the area.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Decide the position: early support, killing an echo, or protecting an aisle.

  2. 02

    If it is absorbent, write open area, diameter, cavity, and absorber, then ask ISO 354 for that.

  3. 03

    Separate the lower impact metre from the upper face.

  4. 04

    Tie fire to the report of the full assembly.

  5. 05

    Do not turn an open-area percentage on the plan into an RT60.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The cavity is kept as drawn. Felt stuck flat is another build-up.

  2. 02

    The tested face is installed, not a fabric that is “close”.

  3. 03

    The impact layer is finished at its height. It is not replaced by an extension of the fragile face to save an item.

  4. 04

    The fixing does not compress the absorber until it becomes solid.

  5. 05

    The build-up is photographed before the face is closed, so it can be checked against the report.

Testing

  • Build-up match

    Cavity, absorber, and face as in the report. A difference cancels the transfer of α.

  • Impact

    The lower sample is inspected visually after early use. This page gives no impact energy.

  • No on-site α as a certificate

    Inferring the coefficient from one room is an estimate. The certificate remains ISO 354.

Commissioning

  • A handed-over detail

    Open area, depth, and material. Without them the wall is decor.

  • Fire and absorption reports

    For the installed assembly only. This page adds no figure.

  • A free zone

    What furniture covers is deducted from the acoustic area in the final calculation.

Maintenance

  • Painting the holes

    It fills the opening and kills the role. Forbidden unless the design is redone.

  • Replacing PET or fabric

    The same thickness and openness, or a new report.

  • Aisle impacts

    Repair the lower layer before the face tears and exposes an untested core.

Common mistakes

  • Perforation with no absorber

    A texture. Energy returns from the back.

  • PET as bass treatment

    A thin layer is not enough against the wavelength at 100 Hz. No coefficient on this page proves otherwise.

  • A class from a picture of the veneer

    Fire belongs to the assembly. The picture has no report.

  • An absorbent wall where a reflector is needed

    Speech goes dry. Position matters more than covering every metre.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why does the perforated wall not absorb?

Because behind it is hard, or there is no absorber in the cavity. The hole alone is a texture. The report names the build-up that absorbs.

Does PET treat bass?

Thin felt hard against the wall does not. Low frequency needs thickness or spacing. The coefficient comes from ISO 354, not from the name.

Where do I take the fire class?

From the report of face, adhesive, and core together. This page does not give it.

Why is the bottom of the wall different?

Because it is hit. A harder layer protects it and may reflect more. Do not assume the whole wall absorbs.

Does cladding isolate the neighbour?

No. That is isolation and paths. Cladding is inside the room.

Technical sources

  • ISO 354

    Absorption of the named mounting. This page carries no coefficient.

  • The assembly reaction-to-fire report

    The class for face and core together. This page has no certificate.

  • ISO 3382-1 after installation

    The wall’s effect on the room. Open area is not that measurement.
