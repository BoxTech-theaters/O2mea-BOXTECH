Acoustic ceilings

A hall ceiling either reflects, absorbs, disappears visually, or leaves a void for the duct, the spotlight, and the rigging. A cloud is a hung panel that absorbs from one face or two and does not close the room. A baffle is a vertical fin that adds area. Black paint on a hard soffit is not an absorber. A mineral-fibre tile is an acoustic decision, not a theatre default.

Definition

A cloud leaves the structural soffit visible or open, so services and rigging pass between panels. If both faces absorb, both areas are counted if the report allows, and that is still not an RT60: volume and the rest of the room are in the calculation, then ISO 3382-1. Hang height changes the low-frequency behaviour of a porous panel. This page gives no α. A baffle adds area when there is not enough horizontal area, and it collides with a sprinkler and a spotlight if it is packed with no coordination.

A black ceiling over the stage hides the tower. If it is hard, it is a dark reflector. If it is a drape, its absorption is that drape’s report, and it moves in the air. A mineral tile in an office absorbs mid and high easily and opens for services; in an unamplified theatre it can kill brilliance and remove a shaped reflector. In a speech room it may be the right tool. The choice is written with its consequences, not as a building habit.

When is this service needed?

  • Services fill the soffit

    Duct, sprinkler, and rigging. A closed ceiling hides them or is punctured until it loses its role.

  • Absorption is wanted without closing the room

    A cloud or a baffle. A full tile is another option to compare, not to assume.

  • The tower must disappear

    Black paint or a drape. Decide whether the surface reflects before approving the colour.

  • Speech room versus music

    Tile may suit the first and hurt the second. The programme comes before the catalogue.

Where it is used

  • Lecture halls

    Distributed absorption and a ceiling that opens for maintenance. Tile is an intelligible option if speech remains the target.

  • Theatres

    A reflector in some places and a cloud in others. A blanket of tile rarely suits unamplified music.

  • Over the stage

    A visually black soffit, and rigging that moves. Absorption, if needed, comes from a drape or a panel, not from paint.

  • Booths and corridors

    A short-life baffle or a tile. Do not call the office a theatre or the reverse.

Parts of the solution

  • The cloud

    A hung panel at a written height, one face or two according to the report.

  • The baffle

    A vertical fin. The spacing between fins is part of the calculated coverage.

  • The mineral tile

    A grid and a tile. NRC, if the sheet mentions it, is a laboratory average, not a theatre curve. This page does not quote it.

  • The black soffit

    Paint, drape, or panel. Each behaves differently.

  • The penetrations

    Sprinkler, loudspeaker, spotlight, and access hatch. Every hole is coordinated with the acoustic role.

Engineering considerations

  • Coverage is not RT60

    A cloud’s square metres, with no volume and no other surfaces, do not produce a second.

  • Hang height

    It spaces the porous panel off the hard soffit and changes the low band. The figure comes from the detail, not from here.

  • Two faces or one

    Count only what the report allows. Assuming the back face absorbs without a test is a fictional gain.

  • Black reflects if it is hard

    Visual colour. α comes from the build-up. Paint is not an absorber.

  • Tile is a decision

    It absorbs highs easily. In unamplified music that can be a loss of brilliance, not a gain in comfort.

Materials

  • Fibre or PET in the cloud

    Thickness and mounting from ISO 354. This page has no coefficient.

  • Mineral tile

    Easy to replace and cheap to maintain. Its office look may be unwanted in the house, and that is a reason separate from sound.

  • Black paint or drape

    Paint reflects if the surface is hard. The drape has its own report, and it moves.

Related systems

  • Sprinklers and detection

    The ceiling cover does not delay a sprinkler. Coordination with fire protection is mandatory.

  • Stage rigging

    Baffle and cloud stay out of the bar’s path. A collision drops both.

  • Sound

    The loudspeaker is not buried behind a tile with no calculated opening. The grid blocks or it does not, by design.

  • Lighting

    The spotlight needs an angle the baffle does not cut. The ceiling serves the light and does not sever it.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Name the programme: speech, unamplified music, or a stage that must disappear.

  2. 02

    Draw services and rigging first, then place the cloud or baffle in the space that remains.

  3. 03

    If you choose tile, write its effect on the highs and on reflector shape, not NRC alone.

  4. 04

    Decide the stage soffit: hard black and reflective, a drape, or nothing. Do not mix colour with absorption.

  5. 05

    Leave a room-measurement criterion. Coverage is a drawing target.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Clouds are hung at the written height and at spacings that do not close the sprinkler path.

  2. 02

    Baffles are fixed outside the path of rigging and spotlights.

  3. 03

    Tile is opened where the loudspeaker and the access are, not cut at random after installation.

  4. 04

    Black paint on a hard soffit is recorded as reflective in the acoustic handover.

  5. 05

    The drape is weighted so it does not move with every door opening.

Testing

  • Room measurement

    ISO 3382-1 if it is a performance house. The cloud is not accepted by the square metre.

  • Services clearance

    Sprinkler, rigging, and spotlight with no collision. A visual check from the walk.

  • No NRC from this page

    If the sheet uses it, it is their average, not our target. It is not copied into a theatre specification as if it were an RT60.

Commissioning

  • A final reflected ceiling

    Cloud, baffle, tile, and opening, each with its acoustic role.

  • The stage condition

    Hard black or drape, written down. No sentence “the ceiling is treated” without a type.

  • Material reports

    ISO 354 for what claims absorption, and fire for the assembly. This page does not hold them.

Maintenance

  • A stained or missing tile

    Replacement is the same type. A hole left open changes the ceiling.

  • A tilted cloud

    After duct work it is returned to its height. Tilt changes the cavity.

  • The stage drape

    Dust weights it, and cleaning follows the fabric instruction, not a random wash that seals the pores.

Common mistakes

  • Office tile in a music theatre

    It absorbs highs and removes shape. Speech may accept it and unamplified music may not.

  • Black assumed absorbent

    Paint on concrete is reflective. Colour is visual concealment.

  • A baffle in the bar’s path

    The first rigging move tears the acoustic ceiling. Coordination comes first.

  • A square metre equals a second

    No conversion. RT60 is a calculation or a measurement.

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

What this page does not claim

Questions designers and owners ask

Do I fill a theatre ceiling with tile?

Rarely for unamplified music. It may suit a speech room. The decision is written for its effect on the highs and on the reflector, not as an office habit.

Does a black ceiling treat sound?

Not if it is painted and hard. It treats sound if it is a drape or a panel with a report. Colour hides and does not absorb.

Does the cloud area equal an RT60?

No. The time needs the volume and the other surfaces, then a measurement. The square metre is coverage.

Where does the sprinkler go?

On a clear path the cloud or baffle does not block. Fire protection is coordination, not a later detail.

Is NRC enough to choose the tile?

No. It is an average if it is on the sheet. A theatre needs the bands and the shape. This page does not quote an NRC.

Technical sources

  • ISO 354

    Absorption of the cloud or tile for its mounting. This page carries neither a coefficient nor an NRC.

  • ISO 3382-1

    Measurement of the house after the ceiling. Coverage does not replace it.

  • Fire-protection coordination

    Sprinkler and detector clearance. This page gives neither an exemption nor a ceiling class.
