Theatre interiors

A wall in a theatre is a tool. It may reflect early speech, absorb a late echo, hide a row with a low balcony, or block a lamp change. Colour is a last decision. The cavity behind the timber, the rail height, the catwalk route, and light reflecting toward the stage are drawn first.

Definition

The interior serves four things at once: acoustics, sightline, maintenance access, and light. A hard surface near the source can be a useful reflector. The same surface at the rear can be an echo. Hatched coverage is not an RT60. A balcony front’s height changes the view of the row behind it and an early reflection. The maintenance walk above the ceiling or behind the wall is what lets a lamp and a filter be changed without closing the season.

Light: surfaces near the proscenium, if they are light, spill house light onto the stage. Black surfaces over the tower hide the rigging visually and do not mean they absorb. Fire comes from the report of the visible build-up — face, fabric, and core — not from the wood’s name. This page gives neither an absorption coefficient nor a class.

When is this service needed?

  • Before the material board

    A cavity, an absorber, and a reflector can still be named. After installation, change is demolition.

  • When the balcony is designed

    Its front is sightline and sound together. Postponing it to “decor” misses both.

  • When the maintenance walk is missing

    A lamp above the audience with no access. The interior has closed the technician’s path.

  • When light washes the stage

    Light walls around the opening. Colour is an operational decision, not only taste.

Where it is used

  • The auditorium

    Side walls, rear wall, and ceiling. Each face is classed as reflector, absorber, or diffuser.

  • Balconies and boxes

    A rail and a front. Their height serves both the view and safety.

  • Around the proscenium

    Surfaces that control house-light spill onto the stage.

  • The back of the house and entries

    Door noise and sudden light. The interior does not end at the last seat.

Parts of the solution

  • The early reflector

    A nearby hard surface, its shape from the acoustic section, not from ornament.

  • Absorber at the echo

    Where the path is late. Its build-up, cavity, and face come from a report, not from a photograph.

  • Rails and fronts

    Height from the code and the sightline together. This page gives no millimetre.

  • Maintenance walks

    A safe width and guard to reach the lamp and the filter. Drawn with the structural engineer.

  • Light-controlling surfaces

    Dark where they should disappear, and cleanable. Gloss black also reflects a spotlight.

Engineering considerations

  • Classify every surface

    Reflector, absorber, diffuser, or a cover with no role. An unclassified surface does not enter the calculation and is not drawn as if it were a solution.

  • Sightline before ornament

    A balcony front and a decorative column can obstruct. C-value is discussed, then geometry fixes it.

  • Access

    A hatch behind the panel or a walk. A lamp with no maintenance path is a decision to close.

  • Light reflectance

    A value from the paint specification, not from the colour’s name. This page gives no percentage.

  • Fire

    The visible assembly is tested as an assembly. Natural wood is not a class.

Materials

  • Solid or perforated wood

    Solid wood usually reflects in the speech range. Perforated wood needs a cavity and an absorber, or it is a texture.

  • Fabric and PET

    A porous face, its thickness and mounting from an ISO 354 report. The word is not a coefficient.

  • Paint

    Sheen and cleanability. High sheen on a side wall sends a patch of light.

Related systems

  • Acoustics

    RT60 and treatment are separate pages. The interior carries them out and does not shorten them to a colour board.

  • Lighting

    Spotlight positions and their walks come before the final skin.

  • Seats

    The last row and the view under the balcony. Seats are not packed and the rail then discovered.

  • HVAC

    Diffusers do not whistle in the house and do not cut a reflector. The route is coordinated.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Draw the acoustic section and the sightline before the material board.

  2. 02

    Classify every large surface: reflector, absorber, diffuser, or visual only.

  3. 03

    Place lamp walks and filter access in the section.

  4. 04

    Review house-light reflection onto the stage from light and glossy surfaces.

  5. 05

    Tie every cladding to a fire report and to absorption data if it claims an acoustic role.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Cavities behind the timber are built as drawn, not as the leftover structure allowed.

  2. 02

    A panel that comes off for maintenance is fixed with a fastener the crew can see, not with permanent glue.

  3. 03

    The balcony front is measured after installation against the sightline.

  4. 04

    Surfaces near the opening are painted in the approved sheen, not in a glossier remainder left on site.

  5. 05

    The key to the walks is handed over with a ban on storage inside them.

Testing

  • Echo and reflector

    A room measurement to ISO 3382-1 in a performance space, not one clap.

  • Sightline

    From the critical seats: last row, end seat, and under the balcony.

  • Lamp access

    A technician reaches and replaces a part without demolishing a finish. The trial is before opening.

Commissioning

  • A surface schedule

    Each face with its role: reflector, absorber, or visual, with the report if one exists.

  • House-light mode

    Reviewed with the stage dark. Spill is recorded.

  • No coefficient from the board

    α, if needed, comes from ISO 354 for the build-up. This page does not have it.

Maintenance

  • Panel removal

    The fastener stays removable. Paint over the screw cancels access.

  • Cleaning gloss

    A method that does not scratch. A scratch scatters light and dirties the picture.

  • Adding signs

    On an absorber or a reflector they change the area. They are reviewed, not stuck on show night.

Common mistakes

  • A finish schedule only

    A wood name and a colour with no cavity, no acoustic role, and no access.

  • A low balcony for the look

    It hides the row behind. The view comes before the handsome line.

  • Gloss black over the tower

    It is assumed to absorb and still reflects the spotlight. Colour is not an α.

  • Closing the lamp path

    A final skin with no hatch. The first burned-out lamp opens the wall with a saw.

Short comparison

The comparison is a way to choose a path. It is not a product recommendation and it is not a code.

PathFits whenWeakens when
An interior that serves the hallThe surface is classified, the view is kept, and the lamp can be replaced.When it is reduced to a colour board after the section is frozen. Nothing left can change.
A simpler lecture roomSpeech is amplified and access matters more than a music reflector.When the programme is unamplified music. Simplicity absorbs what should have reflected.
An office fit-outIt does not apply. A generic mineral ceiling and gypsum walls with no role may suit an office, not a theatre.Always in the house. The consequences for RT60 and sightline are documented on the acoustics pages.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why not start with the material board?

Because wood may reflect or absorb depending on what is behind it. A board with no cavity and no acoustic role is a fit-out, not a theatre interior.

Is a black ceiling absorbent?

Not merely because of its colour. A hard surface reflects. Absorption is a build-up that is measured. Colour hides equipment visually.

Who sets the balcony-front height?

The code together with the sightline. This page gives no millimetre. The literature C-value is only the start of a discussion.

How is a lamp replaced?

From a walk or a hatch drawn before the skin. If it does not exist, replacement is demolition.

Is fire in the name of the stone or the wood?

No. It is in the report of the visible assembly. This page carries no class.

Technical sources

  • ISO 3382-1

    Measurement of the house after finishes. The material board does not replace it.

  • ISO 354 when absorption is claimed

    The coefficient of the named build-up. This page does not carry an α.

  • The local code for rails and walks

    Safe height and width. This page fixes no figure.
