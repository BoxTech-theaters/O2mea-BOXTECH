Home cinema

A living room does not become a cinema because a screen was hung. If a bedroom shares the slab, or HVAC is the noise floor, the work is construction: isolation, ducts, and bass. A product list does not do that work.

Definition

A home cinema is a dwelling room with internal neighbours: sleep above, below, or behind the wall, shared air ducts, and a door to the corridor. Dialogue wants a shorter tail than what is discussed for a concert hall, but RT60 is a project criterion, not a figure from this page. ISO 3382-1 is written for performance spaces; using it in a house is stated explicitly, with its limits. Seating distance follows image width, not the habit of a sofa facing a television.

Bass crosses structure. An isolation figure for a product — and this page does not write an STC anyway — does not describe the neighbour's floor. If construction is refused — door, seal, an independent duct, and separation where it is required — the room stays a living room at an honest accepted level. This page is not an amplifier shop, not a commercial screen, grants no certificate, and states no fire class for a lining.

When is this service needed?

  • A dedicated room is being built

    The door, wall, ceiling, and ducts can be separated before finishes.

  • Sleep is on the same slab

    Bass and speech will arrive unless the path is treated. That is a construction question.

  • A living room that will stay a living room

    The honest scope is a limited screen and an acceptance of shared noise, not the name cinema.

  • Wrong scope

    A shopping list of loudspeakers for a commercial screen, or a promise of cinema isolation with no door and no duct work. Both sit outside the page.

Where it is used

  • A room at the end of the house

    An external wall may help, but the neighbouring bedroom and the slab remain.

  • A basement or an annex

    Sometimes further from sleep, and it still carries bass vertically if someone lives above.

  • A living room open to the kitchen

    No door means no envelope. Calling it a cinema here is the wrong scope.

  • A flat with a neighbour's wall

    The path is not only inside the family. The local code may already limit sound.

Parts of the solution

  • Door and seal

    The gap under the door is enough to leak dialogue. A heavy door with no edge seal is an ordinary door.

  • Ceiling or floor toward sleep

    Separation where bass travels. An absorbent panel inside the room does not stop the slab.

  • Independent or treated HVAC

    A shared branch carries sound to the bedroom and returns fan noise to the film.

  • Seat distance and the image

    They follow image width and the intended viewing angle. The page sets no ready-made metre.

  • Dialogue absorption

    So the sentence does not return from bare glass. This is inside the room, not isolation from it.

Engineering considerations

  • Isolation is not treatment

    Panels inside the room control RT60. The neighbour needs mass, a break, and a sealed duct.

  • Bass is a structural path

    Mid-band RT60 does not describe it, nor does a loudspeaker product figure. The page writes no STC.

  • Fan noise is the floor under dialogue

    A curve in the brief. There is no NC on this page.

  • RT60 is a criterion, not a domestic table

    Directionally shorter than a concert hall, then measured by a stated method. ISO 3382-1 may not automatically cover a house.

  • Honesty in an open room

    If construction is not accepted, write that the level is a living-room level. Do not call it an isolated cinema.

Materials

  • The door, not the loudspeaker

    Door mass and its seal decide speech leakage more than the amplifier's name.

  • Inner lining

    To control how long dialogue remains. Mounting data is not shortened to a coefficient from this page, and there is no fire class.

  • Fixings that do not bridge the break

    Gypsum screwed to the shared structure can restore the bridge you meant to open.

Related systems

  • The image

    Projection or LED after seating distance and glare. The page does not sell the size.

  • Loudspeakers as a load, not a catalogue

    Their position excites the room and the structure. Product choice sits outside this page.

  • HVAC

    A quiet outlet and a duct that does not open onto the bedroom. That is construction.

  • Power and heat

    A closed rack with no ventilation raises noise when its fan opens. It is drawn with the room.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Draw who sleeps above, below, and behind the room, and where the air runs.

  2. 02

    Decide: isolating construction, or an explicit acceptance of a living room. Do not mix the two names.

  3. 03

    Tie image width to seating distance. Do not start from an old television diagonal.

  4. 04

    Write the noise criterion and the bass target toward other rooms as project limits, with no figures invented here.

  5. 05

    If RT60 stays in the brief, name the measurement method and say that ISO 3382-1 is for performance spaces.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    The door and the seal before hanging the screen. Sequence shows what matters.

  2. 02

    Separate or treat the duct toward sleep before the ceiling closes.

  3. 03

    Build any required structural break before the inner lining.

  4. 04

    Place the image at the agreed distance and check glare at night, as in a showing.

  5. 05

    Listen from the bedroom with bass running before you call the room finished.

Testing

  • Listening from the bedroom

    Speech and bass in operating mode. If the brief asked for a measurement, the method is named and not invented here.

  • Noise with the fan running

    HVAC and the equipment rack in film mode. No NC figure from the page.

  • Dialogue inside the room

    If RT60 is measured, it is by the stated method. The result is not a universal house number.

Commissioning

  • What was promised is what is handed over

    Either isolation was built, or there is a written acceptance of living-room limits. No marketing name in between.

  • Bass is part of handover

    Check the neighbouring room at night as it will be used, not at noon with everyone awake.

  • No home-cinema certificate

    The page does not accredit a room and does not grant a brand mark.

Maintenance

  • A renovation that opens the ceiling

    It restores the shared duct. Close the break before the boards go back.

  • New carpet or new glass

    It changes how long dialogue remains inside the room. If there is a criterion, it is reconsidered.

  • A stronger amplifier in the same room

    It adds no isolation. It may increase what reaches sleep. Power is not construction.

Common mistakes

  • A shopping list instead of a room

    The most common wrong scope. A loudspeaker does not isolate a slab and does not silence a duct.

  • Calling the living room an isolated cinema

    With no door and no air separation, the name is false. Honesty is a living-room scope.

  • Absorbent panels against the neighbour

    This mixes treatment with isolation. Bass crosses the structure despite the fabric.

  • Copying a commercial screen into a flat

    Occupancy, code, and adjacency differ. The commercial screen page does not stick onto a bedroom.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Do I buy the equipment first?

No. That is the wrong scope. If the slab is shared with sleep, construction comes before the loudspeaker's name. A list does not isolate.

When can a living room not become a cinema?

When no door closes, there is no independent duct, sleep is on the same structure, and construction is refused. A living-room level can be accepted. It cannot be called isolation.

Do absorbent panels protect the bedroom?

No. They treat how long sound remains inside the room. Isolation is mass, a break, and the duct. Mixing them is a common error, and the page does not offer an STC as a cure.

What RT60 do I target at home?

No seconds from this page. The direction shorter than a concert hall is discussion. The criterion belongs to the project, and ISO 3382-1 is not assumed to cover a dwelling without saying so.

Is this the commercial screen page?

No. A multiplex and isolation between two public films are on the cinema design page. Here the neighbour is a bedroom and the code is a dwelling code.

Technical sources

  • ISO 3382-1

    A measurement standard for performance spaces. It is not moved into a dwelling silently. If it is used, that is stated in the brief. The page does not replace it.

  • The building and fire code adopted by the project

    It may limit sound between dwellings and the performance of finishes and doors. The page names no city and grants no fire class.

  • The difference between isolation and treatment

    Absorption inside the room does not describe transmission through the slab and the duct. This page does not replace a laboratory report for a stated build-up and writes no STC.
