Auditorium design

Counting chairs from floor area is a sketch. Legal capacity comes from the exit, the row length, and the adopted code. Auditorium design separates comfort from that number, and builds the rake for sightline and speech.

Definition

A lecture or assembly room is designed so the speaker is seen and the sentence is understood. The rake comes from C-value, not from a stock slope. The aisle comes from the code, not from the carpet width. A wheelchair position sits on a sightline, not in a dead corner. A shorter RT60 is discussed for speech than for music, and STI under IEC 60268-16 is a way to write intelligibility. Both are project criteria.

This page is not a theatre with a fly tower and not a concert hall. If the programme is unamplified music, the scope is wrong here. A capacity that comes from dividing the floor area is not a legal capacity, however often the number appears in a space programme. The page replaces neither ISO 3382-1, nor IEC 60268-16, nor the egress code, and it grants no fire classification for seats.

When is this service needed?

  • One speaker or a clear platform

    The audience must see the mouth and hear the sentence from the last accepted row.

  • A dispute about how many seats

    Comfort is row spacing; the law is exits. Design writes both numbers and does not mix them.

  • A flat hall wanted as a rake

    If every seat needs a stable view, the floor changes or the missing view is accepted in writing.

  • Wrong scope

    Dividing area to produce a legal capacity, designing a theatre tower, or building on a site whose section is not fixed. Those are other requests.

Where it is used

  • A university lecture hall

    Note-taking, a view of a screen, and long speech. A tablet arm changes absorption and the aisle.

  • A school or institutional assembly

    High occupancy and strict egress. Comfort does not cancel exit width.

  • A corporate hall with reinforced speech

    Reinforcement is part of the brief, but the room is still seen and its noise is still controlled.

  • A short rake without a balcony

    The simplest section, and C-value and the exit still apply. Shortness is not an exemption.

Parts of the solution

  • Speech platform

    Speaker height relative to the first eye. A high platform blocks; a low one is lost in heads.

  • Rake and steps

    Built from clearance, eye height, and row spacing, not from one slope pasted on the elevation.

  • Aisles and rows

    Seats between aisles and travel distance come from the adopted code. The page does not set the count.

  • Access places

    Wheelchair and companion space on an acceptable view, and a route that does not depend on others standing.

  • Lecture screen or board

    Viewing angle and glare are part of the section. Projector choice stays on its page after geometry is stable.

Engineering considerations

  • Capacity is two numbers

    Comfort seats are not legal occupancy. Writing one in place of the other is a design error.

  • Speech before ornament

    An early reflecting ceiling may help a lecture; a fully absorbent ceiling may dry it out. The decision is written down.

  • STI is not a badge

    A 0-to-1 scale in IEC 60268-16. The pass mark is in the brief, and the method is named when it is measured.

  • Noise is the floor under the sentence

    HVAC in lecture mode is discussed as a curve, not as a single decibel this page publishes.

  • The seat changes the room

    A writing tablet, the fabric, and an open back change absorption between an empty room and a full one.

Materials

  • The seat, not the cloth alone

    Absorption comes from a mounting report. The page states neither a coefficient nor a fire class.

  • The nearby ceiling

    An early surface for speech, or absorbent control. Position matters more than the panel's name.

  • Floor and aisle

    The floor finish does not reduce the legal aisle width or raise the step so the sightline drops.

Related systems

  • The lecturer microphone

    Close to the mouth if reinforcement is in the brief. A distant room microphone is not an STI plan.

  • Hearing loop

    Drawn with the slab steel if access is required. Field performance is referred to its own standard, not to this page.

  • Image display

    Screen height enters the sightline section. Lens and throw ratio follow once distance is stable.

  • HVAC

    Air outlets are not placed where their velocity is heard on the speaker if that was avoidable. The criterion is in the brief.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Write the kind of speech: lecture, discussion, or reinforced, and who in the last row must understand.

  2. 02

    Draw the platform and the first eye, then the rows with a C-value clearance and a spacing set by the seat and the code.

  3. 03

    Place aisles and access positions, and take legal capacity from the code, not from area.

  4. 04

    Put RT60 and STI criteria in the brief as project targets, with an HVAC noise curve.

  5. 05

    Freeze the section before the construction page. A seat bought on a moving rake is the wrong scope.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Design issues a section and counts tied to the code, not a purchase order.

  2. 02

    Structure reviews the rake build-up and the seat anchor as a load.

  3. 03

    Audio and video routes are kept inside the section before the ceiling closes.

  4. 04

    A sample row is checked: view, aisle, and a moving seat.

  5. 05

    The count is not called final before the egress path is accepted.

Testing

  • A view from a sample

    First row, last row, and an access seat, against the section.

  • A speech measurement if required

    STI by the method in IEC 60268-16. The page does not set the pass limit.

  • RT60 after the seats

    To ISO 3382-1 when the standard's limits apply, and in the condition the brief named.

Commissioning

  • Two numbers in the record

    Seats drawn, and occupancy the code allows. They are not mixed in one cell.

  • HVAC in lecture mode

    Acoustic handover in the mode people will sit in, not with the fans stopped for the visit.

  • No permit from this page

    Occupancy is a permit from the authority. Knowledge here does not sign a capacity.

Maintenance

  • Adding chairs in the aisle

    It breaks the egress width the capacity was built on. It is not innocent maintenance.

  • Replacing the seat

    A new back height changes C-value. Review the section before buying.

  • A thicker carpet

    It raises the aisle level and may change absorption. A small detail touches sight and sound.

Common mistakes

  • Capacity from area

    The most common wrong scope. The quotient is not a legal occupancy.

  • A concert hall on this page

    A speech criterion does not close an unamplified music design. Open the theatre gate and the right programme.

  • A wheelchair with no view

    A flat blocked corner is not access. The view is part of the place.

  • Copying another project's rake

    Platform height and row spacing change the step. A photograph is not a calculation.

Any number here is a preliminary discussion range from practice or a named reference. Replace it with the project criterion, laboratory data, and on-site measurement.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Why not calculate capacity from area?

Because the result is not a legal occupancy. The exit, the row length, and the adopted code are the reference. Using the division as a permit is the wrong scope.

Do all halls need the same rake?

No. Speaker height, C-value, and spacing make the step. Copying a slope fails one end of the room.

What is the lecture intelligibility criterion?

It is written in the brief, and often discussed through STI to IEC 60268-16 together with RT60 and background noise. The page does not choose the score.

Does a tablet-arm seat change the design?

Yes. It changes row width, absorption, and egress movement. It is chosen with the section, not after it.

When do I move to the construction page?

When the section and the legal count are signed. Building on a moving drawing is the wrong scope and wastes the acoustic envelope.

Technical sources

  • IEC 60268-16

    The STI reference. This page does not replace the standard and does not set the project's acceptance score.

  • The C-value method in theatre planning literature

    A clearance method often discussed around 90–120 mm. The page replaces neither the section nor a code that may impose a view or an aisle.

  • The building, fire, and egress code adopted by the project

    Legal capacity, aisle width, and the fire performance of seats via their own reports. The page names no city and carries no classification.
