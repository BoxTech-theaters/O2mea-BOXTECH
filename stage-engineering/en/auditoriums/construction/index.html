Auditorium construction

Auditorium construction is installing the rake, the seats, the envelope, and AV routes while the room is still a site. The seat is not used to hide unfinished work, and capacity is not extracted from area during the pour.

Definition

The page assumes design has fixed the rake, the sightline, and the speech criterion. Site work is step levels, an anchor that does not wreck a floating floor if there is one, doors that close the envelope, and routes buried before the ceiling. An RT60 measurement in bare concrete is not a handover. ISO 3382-1 and IEC 60268-16 are used by the method the brief wrote, after the agreed condition is complete.

This is not a supply of loose chairs on a flat floor, and not a late design for a concert hall. If the section is a draft, starting the seats is the wrong scope. The page states no STC, no fire class, and no kilopascal figure. The adopted code, product reports, and measurement are the reference. The page does not replace them.

When is this service needed?

  • The section is issued for construction

    The steps and the number of seats between aisles are no longer an idea.

  • The room is still open

    A level or a route can still be repaired before the ceiling and the step close.

  • Seats are fixed, not lined up

    The anchor and the load are part of the slab. Loose supply is a different scope.

  • Wrong scope

    An unsigned rake, loose furniture named a hall, or a request for an occupancy certificate from a knowledge page.

Where it is used

  • A cast or built rake

    Concrete or steel carries the step. Tolerance stacks up to the seat.

  • A hall inside an existing building

    The old level may not accept the rake. A survey comes before the order.

  • An envelope next to a noisy void

    The door, the ceiling, and the void above are a path. Finish alone does not close it.

  • A site shared with general finishes

    The seat is damaged and dust changes the measurement. Sequence protects the sample.

Parts of the solution

  • The rake body

    Tread, nosing, and riser. It matches the section after finish, not only before it.

  • Seat anchor

    Its place comes from the seat maker. Its penetration of a floating floor is detailed so it does not short the isolation.

  • Envelope: door, ceiling, rear wall

    Seals and openings are part of speech. A door gap raises the noise floor.

  • AV routes

    Containment for the platform, the ceiling, and a hearing loop before the pour or before close-up.

  • Protection until handover

    A seat on a building site gets dirty and breaks. Protection is an item, not a courtesy.

Engineering considerations

  • Tolerance kills the line

    A few millimetres on each step become a head that blocks at the last row. The tolerance limit is on the drawing.

  • Anchor and load

    A fixed chair and a crowd are a structural load. The page publishes no load figure.

  • Do not short a floating floor

    A bolt to the structural slab brings the noise path back. The detail comes before the drilling.

  • Measure in the final condition

    Seats, doors, and HVAC as in a lecture. Anything else is a site note.

  • Egress during installation

    Seat storage does not close an exit. The code applies to the later occupied room and to the work now.

Materials

  • Step body

    Concrete, steel, or composite as the structural engineer has it. The page chooses neither a system nor its fire class.

  • Tread finish

    Slip resistance and a visible nosing are a safety requirement from the code and the product report, not from this page.

  • Door and ceiling seals

    Materials that match both the acoustic detail and the fire detail, on their own documents.

Related systems

  • Lectern infrastructure

    Power, signal, and a microphone route are closed before the platform face is finished.

  • Loudspeakers and ceiling

    Fixing points are coordinated with reflectors, not added after the cut.

  • Hearing loop

    The cable sits where random steel will not kill it. Design stays on the loop page.

  • HVAC

    The damper and silencer are installed before the ceiling closes if they are in the brief. There is no NC curve here.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Read the signed section and mark the levels the contractor will be held to after finish.

  2. 02

    Match the seat detail to the floor: floating or solid, load, and aisle edge.

  3. 03

    Draw the routes that must be buried: platform, ceiling, loop, and power.

  4. 04

    Define the measurement condition for handover: seats, doors, HVAC, and the named standard method.

  5. 05

    Agree a sample row before releasing the whole order.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Form the rake and verify levels before any seat.

  2. 02

    Run the routes and close the penetrations that will not be seen again.

  3. 03

    Install a sample, and check view, aisle, and fixing.

  4. 04

    Complete the seats with protection, and do not use them as a working aisle.

  5. 05

    Measure after balance and adjustment, not in an open shell that is then called final.

Testing

  • Levels against the drawing

    The finished step, not only the rough concrete. The acceptance limit is in the documents, not here.

  • A sightline and row sample

    A real sitter or an agreed eye height, from the start of the row and the end.

  • Measurement of the final condition

    RT60 to ISO 3382-1 and STI to IEC 60268-16 if the brief wrote them. No pass figure from this page.

Commissioning

  • A technical snag list

    Level, seal, route, and a seat that rocks. Not fabric colour alone.

  • HVAC running with the measurement

    If speech is the programme, handover and noise are in the same mode.

  • A record, not a page certificate

    This page grants neither occupancy nor a hall accreditation.

Maintenance

  • Removing a seat for repair

    The anchor goes back as detailed. A random plug can short a floating floor.

  • Repainting the tread

    Thickness builds up and changes the level. Review it if it grows.

  • A door that lost its closer

    The acoustic envelope is practically open. Noise returns before anyone notices it in a measurement.

Common mistakes

  • Seats on a draft section

    Wrong scope. The envelope and the anchors are wasted when the rake moves.

  • Hiding incomplete work with chairs

    The seat is not a cover for an open duct or a wrong step.

  • A shell measurement as handover

    RT60 will change with fabric and the door. An early record is not acceptance.

  • Loose furniture called hall construction

    If there is no rake, no envelope, and no load, the scope is furniture, not this page.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Do we install seats while the drawing is changing?

No. That is the wrong scope. Rake, anchors, and envelope are wasted, and a sightline is not repaired by upholstery.

When is a measurement acceptable for handover?

After the condition the brief named is complete: seats, doors, and HVAC. ISO 3382-1 and IEC 60268-16 are not replaced by a shell visit.

Is a floating floor drilled for the seat?

Only with a detail that does not bridge the gap to the slab. A direct bolt brings the noise back. The page does not offer an STC as a cure.

Who sets the seat load?

The structural engineer, with the maker's data and the adopted code. This page refuses to publish a kilopascal figure to be copied into a contract.

Is a supply of loose chairs on a flat floor enough?

If the ask is a raked Auditorium with an envelope, no. That is a furniture scope, and calling it hall construction is wrong.

Technical sources

  • ISO 3382-1

    Measurement of room parameters after completion. The page does not replace the standard and does not treat a shell measurement as handover.

  • IEC 60268-16

    If speech intelligibility is a handover item, the STI method is here. The page sets no score and does not replace the document.

  • The building, fire, and egress code adopted by the project

    Levels that affect safety, exits during installation, and material performance. The page names no city and no product class.
