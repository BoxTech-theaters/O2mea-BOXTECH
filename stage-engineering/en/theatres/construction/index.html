Theatre construction

Construction coordination is making structure, slab loads, service penetrations, and isolation agree with a section that is already finished. It is not a sentence that says we build everything, including scenery and show content.

Definition

This page is what happens after design is stable: the slab that carries fixed seats, a rake build-up, and rigging reactions; the line a duct or sprinkler may not cross without a detail; and the difference between an isolating shell and a finish screwed through the resilient layer. If C-value is still moving, construction has gone ahead of the decision, and that is the wrong scope.

The work is coordination, not a general contract by slogan. The structural engineer signs the load, the isolation trade builds the line, HVAC, power, and fire services are drawn on it, then the seat and the lining arrive. There is no STC on this page, no product fire class, and no legal capacity. The adopted code, test reports, and site measurement are the sources. ISO 3382-1 is for measurement, not a substitute for a penetration detail.

When is this service needed?

  • The section is signed

    Sightlines and preliminary loads exist, and the site needs a detail rather than an idea.

  • Services cut the box

    Ducts, cables, or sprinklers will cross the isolation line if they are not coordinated now.

  • Seats and rigging on the same slab

    The seat load and the point load are not the architectural floor allowance.

  • Wrong scope

    Geometry not yet decided, show scenery, or a promise to build every trade including content. That is not this page.

Where it is used

  • A box within a box

    Where the Auditorium must be separated from a neighbour or the street. The break is built before the lining.

  • A slab under a fixed rake

    The rake build-up and the fixed chair load the slab beyond an office void.

  • A fly tower or a grid

    Loads are concentrated. They are coordinated with the rigging page and not hidden in a finishes margin.

  • Doors and exits that break isolation

    A legal exit is a door, and a door is an acoustic hole if it is not detailed. The code is not cancelled for isolation, nor the reverse, without a drawing.

Parts of the solution

  • Slab and rake edge

    Step level, build-up thickness, and the seat anchor. Tolerance is written on the drawing, not on this page.

  • Isolation line

    Walls, a floating floor if there is one, and a separated ceiling. The flanking path is part of the line.

  • Penetration schedule

    Every duct, cable, and sprinkler that crosses the line has a sealing detail. An open-area percentage is not invented here.

  • Rigging points

    Point load and safety factor are signed by the rigging design and the code. The page states no kilonewtons.

  • Coordination with the seat

    The finished step height matches the seat fixing. Pouring first and being surprised is not coordination.

Engineering considerations

  • The load is not an office allowance

    Fixed seats, a crowd, and rake build-up are reviewed structurally. There is no kilopascal figure on this page.

  • Isolation is not the finish

    An absorbent sheet on a continuous wall does not make a box. Mass, separation, and seals are the isolation.

  • A penetration brings the noise back

    An unsealed opening cancels the wall's intent, whatever the laboratory report of the single product.

  • Tolerances stack

    Structure, then rake build-up, then finish, then seat. If the sum exceeds the drawing, the sightline drops.

  • Fire and egress during isolation

    A seal that stops sound may conflict with a fire stop. The adopted code decides, not this page.

Materials

  • Layers of the break

    Mass, a gap or a resilient fixing, and a seal. A product name does not replace the detail.

  • Sealants at penetrations

    Chosen to match both the isolation detail and the fire detail, on their own test reports, not on a ruling from this page.

  • Lining after the line

    Reflective or absorbent lining is an acoustic design decision. Fixing it does not bridge the resilient layer with a rigid path.

Related systems

  • HVAC

    The duct is either outside the box or fitted with a silencer and a crossing detail. Fan noise is a brief criterion, not a figure here.

  • Stage and audio power

    Routes are drawn before the wall closes. Surface conduit later is a sign that coordination was missed.

  • Fire suppression

    A sprinkler head is a penetration. It is coordinated with the isolation line and the code, not left to the workshop.

  • Rigging

    Points and grid are handed to structure as signed loads. System design stays on its own page.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Receive the sightline section and the programme from design. Refuse to start linings if C-value is still a draft.

  2. 02

    Turn seats, rake, and rigging into loads the structural engineer reviews, with no figure invented on the page.

  3. 03

    Draw the isolation line continuously in plan and section, and mark every penetration.

  4. 04

    Match doors and exits to the code and to the acoustic seal in the same detail.

  5. 05

    Fix the closing order: structure, then the break, then sealed services, then lining, then seat.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Pour and verify the levels the rake will sit on before building the steps.

  2. 02

    Build the resilient break and inspect it before any finish that could short it.

  3. 03

    Close penetrations to the schedule, not to whatever opening is left at the end of the week.

  4. 04

    Install a sample seat on a finished step and check sightline and fixing.

  5. 05

    Do not accept final lining before the items that will be invisible afterwards are closed.

Testing

  • Continuity check of the break

    Looking for a rigid bridge: a screw, a duct, or contact at the edge of a floating floor.

  • Level check

    The built step against the sightline section. The allowed tolerance is what the drawing wrote, not a figure from this page.

  • A measurement after closing, if required

    Field isolation or RT60 by the method written in the brief. No STC is copied from a catalogue into the record.

Commissioning

  • Hand over the schedule, not the taste

    Penetrations closed, fixings that do not short the break, and an accepted sample seat.

  • HVAC as it will run

    Noise is reviewed in audience mode if the brief asked. The page sets no NC curve.

  • Limits of the contract

    This handover is coordinated construction. It is not a show accreditation and not a room certificate.

Maintenance

  • A later penetration

    A camera or a duct after opening reopens the line. It is sealed to the original detail.

  • Finish screws

    Hanging a plaque on an isolated wall can bridge the gap. Check before drilling.

  • Exit doors

    A worn seal or a weak closer drops everyday isolation. Maintenance is an acoustic item, not only appearance.

Common mistakes

  • Building before geometry is stable

    Wrong scope. Construction does not invent a C-value while the rake is poured.

  • The sentence that we build everything

    Structure, isolation, and services are one scope. Dramatic scenery and content are another.

  • A finish that punctures the break

    A screw through the resilient layer turns isolation into a continuous lining.

  • Copying an STC to site

    The page does not state the figure in the first place. A product laboratory report is not the performance of a penetrated wall.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is construction late design?

No. If the sightline is still open, the scope is wrong. Construction coordinates a decision that has been issued.

Why are there no loads in figures?

Because a figure here would be copied wrongly into a contract. The load comes from the structural model and the code adopted for the project.

Does an absorbent panel isolate the Auditorium?

No. Absorption inside the room is not isolation from outside it. Mixing them is a common wrong scope.

When is RT60 measured relative to construction?

After the seats, after penetrations are closed, and in the agreed HVAC mode, to ISO 3382-1. A bare-concrete measurement is not a handover.

Does this page include show scenery?

No. That is the wrong scope. Permanent rigging loads are coordinated; scenery making and content are another contract.

Technical sources

  • The building, fire, and egress code adopted by the project

    Loads, exits, fire separations, and the performance of sealing materials. This page does not replace the code and names no city.

  • ISO 3382-1

    The measurement reference after the room is complete, not a substitute for a construction detail. The page does not replace the standard.

  • The C-value method in theatre planning literature

    Construction builds the value the section fixed. It does not reinvent the 90–120 mm discussion range on site. The page does not replace the drawing.
