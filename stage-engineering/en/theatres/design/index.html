Theatre design

Definition

Theatre design fixes what a seated person sees and what remains of the sound after a sentence or a note. Plan, volume per seat, rake, stage opening, and the depth of a balcony under a soffit all change the result. A speech programme wants less late energy. Music wants volume and an RT60 that allow decay. A room that refuses to say which one yields has not been designed.

Reinforcement and a black box do not excuse geometry. Flexibility is rigging, power, and audio that can be rearranged, not black paint. C-value is often discussed in the literature between 90 and 120 mm, then fixed by geometry and code. RT60 is a project criterion measured to ISO 3382-1, not a number from this page. The page replaces neither those documents nor the egress code.

When is this service needed?

  • The brief is still a sentence

    Speech, opera, a mix, or assumed reinforcement: the decision is written before the section is frozen.

  • A balcony or forestage is being copied

    Copying a photograph drops the view under the balcony or leaves the stage outside the front-row angle.

  • A renovation that changes hearing

    Replacing seats or linings changes absorption. Design resets the criterion, not only the colour.

  • Wrong scope

    A finishes mood board, a site construction sequence, or a conference room named a theatre: this page is not that. Construction has its own page.

Where it is used

  • End stage with a proscenium

    The opening is a visual and acoustic gate. Its width and the forestage depth are design, not a picture frame.

  • Thrust or courtyard

    An audience on more than one side changes the early sightline and the sound path. It is not treated with a single end-stage section.

  • Black box

    Design it for the audience layouts that will actually be used: grid, points, and exits that stay legal in each layout.

  • A room with a balcony

    The depth of cover under the balcony is drawn. A seat that neither sees nor hears is not a seat that counts.

Parts of the solution

  • Sightline section

    The seated eye, the head in front, C-value, and the stage or curtain edge. First and last rows are checked, not only the middle.

  • Volume and absorption

    Volume per seat tends, in the literature, to be smaller for speech than for music. The figure used is calculated from the programme, not copied.

  • Stage relationship

    Stage height, the curtain line, and whether a seated and a standing actor are visible. The fly tower is sent to the stage page with its loads.

  • Seats as an acoustic surface

    Occupied and empty seats change absorption. Design names the condition in which RT60 will be measured.

  • Access positions

    Wheelchair and companion positions on a sightline, with a route that does not cut a whole row without an aisle.

Engineering considerations

  • Programme before form

    The sentence "multi-use" is not a criterion. Write what is sacrificed: speech clarity or length of decay.

  • C-value is not a slope percentage

    Spacing and eye height make a step that may vary. One slope from another project fails at one end.

  • Balcony and acoustic shadow

    A deep overhang denies rear seats the early ceiling. Later treatment does not restore a blocked sightline.

  • RT60 is a consequence, not a decor target

    It is discussed as shorter for speech than for music, then measured to ISO 3382-1. The page does not set the seconds.

  • Egress is not a last layer

    Row length and aisle width change the seat count the code allows, so volume and income change together.

Materials

  • Surfaces near the source

    An early reflector over the forestage is an acoustic decision. An absorbent panel in the same place can delete what should have arrived.

  • Seat and linings

    Absorption is taken from declared mounting data, not from fabric colour. The page states no absorption coefficient.

  • Finish with no class assigned here

    Fire performance is what the product report proves under the adopted code. This page grants no classification.

Related systems

  • Reinforcement when it is in the brief

    Point Source or Line Array is a coverage decision that follows room shape. The page does not choose the product.

  • A hearing loop for speech

    If hearing access is required, the loop is drawn with the steel in the slab, not after the carpet.

  • HVAC as the noise floor

    The noise criterion is a curve in the brief, not a decibel figure this page publishes.

  • Show lighting and power

    Bridge positions are respected in the sightline section. DMX design itself sits on the lighting page.

Design sequence

  1. 01

    Write the programme and whether it is unamplified or reinforced, and who must understand or hear from the last seat.

  2. 02

    Draw the stage opening and its height relative to the eye, then build the rows from C-value and a spacing set by the seat and the code.

  3. 03

    Estimate volume and the RT60 criterion as a project target, and separate what belongs to speech from what belongs to music.

  4. 04

    Check balconies, wheelchair rows, and exits. Revise the section before it is called final.

  5. 05

    Hand loads and the isolation line to the construction page. Do not close finishes on a section that is still moving.

Delivery sequence

  1. 01

    Design does not pour the slab. It issues a signed section that others build on.

  2. 02

    The structural engineer reviews seat and rigging loads as points, not as a finishes allowance.

  3. 03

    Service routes are fixed outside the isolation line, or with a detail at every crossing.

  4. 04

    The seat is chosen after spacing and height are stable, not the reverse.

  5. 05

    A sightline is checked again from a sample seat before final linings are approved.

Testing

  • Sample sightline check

    From the first row, the last row, and under the balcony, seated, against the drawing rather than the render.

  • Prediction, then RT60 measurement

    A prediction is not a handover. Measure to ISO 3382-1 after the seats, in the condition the brief named.

  • Reinforced speech if it exists

    If STI is in the brief, the IEC 60268-16 method is the reference. The page sets no score.

Commissioning

  • A record that names the condition

    Seats in or out, HVAC in audience mode, and doors as they will be in performance.

  • Gaps against the criterion, not against taste

    The snag list is sound, sight, and egress. Fabric colour does not close an engineering item.

  • No certificate from the page

    Handover is a measurement record and compliance with the adopted code. This page issues no accreditation.

Maintenance

  • Changing seats resets the measurement

    A more absorbent seat changes RT60. The model is not swapped quietly.

  • Do not add panels without a criterion

    Treatment after opening can kill an early reflection that was intentional.

  • Isolation doors are checked

    A damaged seal brings back the noise the wall was designed against.

Common mistakes

  • A mood board instead of a section

    Colours produce neither a C-value nor a volume. This page refuses that scope.

  • Copying a concert RT60 into drama

    Speech usually needs a shorter tail. The figure is in any case a project criterion, not a clipping.

  • A deep balcony from a photograph

    The seat under it may neither see the actor's mouth nor hear the ceiling. Draw it before it is counted in capacity.

  • Counting capacity from area

    This is a design error, not a commercial dispute. The code, the aisle, and the row length are the legal capacity.

What this page does not claim

No fire classification, absorption coefficient, sound pressure level, or legal capacity on this page may be copied into a quotation. Those values come from a product test report, a measurement, or the local code adopted for the project.

Questions designers and owners ask

Is a material board enough to design a theatre?

No. That is the wrong scope. Without a sightline section, a volume, and a programme, the board is decoration, not design.

Who chooses between speech and music?

The brief does. The designer shows what each option loses in RT60 and volume. The page does not choose on the programme's behalf.

Is 100 mm the correct C-value?

It sits inside a range often discussed between 90 and 120 mm. It is not a ruling. Geometry and code fix the value. The page replaces neither the literature on the method nor the code.

Is a black box excused from sightlines?

No. Each audience layout has a section. Flexibility whose layouts are not drawn is not flexibility.

Where does this page stop?

At an agreed geometry for the room. Loads and penetrations are on the construction page. RT60 and STI are measured against their references, not against a catalogue.

Technical sources

  • The C-value method in theatre planning literature

    Vertical clearance over the person in front, often discussed around 90–120 mm. This page replaces neither the section nor the reference text the team uses.

  • ISO 3382-1

    Measurement of performance-space parameters, including RT60. A prediction on this page is not a measurement, and the page does not replace the standard.

  • The building, fire, and egress code adopted by the project

    Capacity, exits, and finish performance. The page names no city and does not carry a fire classification into a specification.
