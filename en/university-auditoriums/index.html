University auditoriums and theatres: clear lectures and academic and cultural events

A university needs several kinds of hall: a large lecture theatre for shared courses, a theatre for student activities, a ceremony hall for graduations and conference rooms for scientific symposia. At BoxTech O2mea we help universities plan and equip these spaces to serve teaching, recording and streaming.

How we approach university auditoriums

A large lecture hall is judged by speech clarity and by whether the student in the back row can read the screen. We therefore prioritise short reverberation, well-distributed sound and a screen sized to the viewing distance.

Lecture capture and streaming are now part of teaching, requiring cameras, microphones, camera-friendly lighting and network routes. We plan for this from the start instead of retrofitting.

A student-activities theatre needs flexibility and mid-level equipment that the university’s own team can run.

Project phases

  1. Faculty needs survey — Uses and student numbers.
  2. Design — Rake, acoustics and projection.
  3. Capture and streaming — Cameras, audio and networks.
  4. Construction — Risers, finishes and seats.
  5. Training — IT and operating teams.

Engineering aspects

Reverberation

Relatively short for speech, about 0.6–0.9 s depending on volume.

Screen size

Calculated from the furthest seat and content type.

Capture

Microphones close to the talker and suitable face lighting.

Accessibility

Wheelchair positions and assistive listening.

Checklist before you start

  • Is the dominant use defined: lectures, concerts or conferences?
  • Have sightlines been drawn to the platform and to the bottom of the screen?
  • Is screen size calculated from the furthest seat and content type?
  • Is the target reverberation suitable for speech?
  • Are aisles and exits sufficient to code?
  • Have recording, streaming and camera positions been planned?

Common mistakes we help you avoid

  • Placing the screen after the rake is built so rear rows lose it
  • A hard flat rear wall echoing back to the platform
  • High-velocity HVAC outlets near the audience
  • Seats unsuited to long sitting times

Requirements in Saudi Arabia

Large halls in the Kingdom are places of public assembly and follow Civil Defense requirements on egress, capacity, emergency lighting and finish materials. In universities and government bodies we also address accessibility and assistive listening requirements.

What you receive

  • An initial technical notes report after drawing review or site visit
  • A clear itemised scope of work
  • Approved drawings and specifications before construction
  • A programme linked to project stages
  • Records of concealed works and handover with documents and training

How to prepare your request

To speed up the study, prepare what you have: the architectural plan (PDF or CAD), building sections and clear heights, the expected audience size, the main uses in order of importance, photos of the existing site if any, and the target programme. The information need not be complete; helping you complete it is part of our work.

Frequently asked questions

Can lectures be recorded automatically?

Yes, by integrating cameras and audio with the university’s capture platform.

How does a lecture hall differ from a university theatre?

A lecture hall focuses on speech and projection; a theatre needs a stage and stage lighting.

Do you support assistive listening?

Yes, hearing loops or assistive listening systems can be included.

Can works be phased?

Yes, to fit the academic calendar.
