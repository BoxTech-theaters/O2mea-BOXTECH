Acoustic materials: types and when to use them

The acoustic materials market is large and the terms are similar. This guide groups materials by how they work, so you know what you are buying.

Updated October 2026 · BoxTech O2mea technical team

Porous absorbers

Such as mineral wool, polyester fibre and fabric-wrapped panels. They absorb mid and high frequencies efficiently, with more low-frequency absorption as thickness or air gap increases.

Perforated and slotted timber

Timber or MDF with holes or slots and absorbent behind. They combine a premium timber look with good absorption; the open area sets performance.

Resonant absorbers

Panels or cavities tuned to absorb specific low frequencies, used in studios and halls with low-frequency boom.

Diffusers

Shaped surfaces that scatter sound in many directions instead of absorbing it, keeping the room lively and preventing focused reflections.

Isolation materials

High-density plasterboard, heavy membranes, resilient mounts and sealants. These do not improve sound inside the room; they stop it travelling.

Reading specifications

NRC or αw gives average absorption (roughly 0 to 1). Compare values tested with the same mounting, and check fire classification and suitability for public spaces.

Key takeaways

  • Choose material by how it works.
  • Thickness and air gap change performance.
  • Fire classification is essential in public spaces.

This article is for general education; final design decisions are based on each project’s study and the competent authorities’ requirements.
