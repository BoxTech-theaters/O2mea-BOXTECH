Acoustic walls in theatres and halls

A wall in a hall may reflect to reinforce sound, absorb to prevent echo, diffuse to spread it, or isolate to keep it in. Knowing each wall’s role is the key to the design.

Updated October 2026 · BoxTech O2mea technical team

Side walls

In theatres and mid-size halls, early lateral reflections support clarity and envelopment. Side walls near the platform therefore usually stay reflective or diffusive, at studied angles.

Rear wall

The wall facing the platform is the most common source of echo. It is treated with absorption or diffusion, especially in deep rooms.

Acoustic cladding

Perforated, slotted or batten timber cladding over absorbent achieves the required performance with a premium look. Batten spacing and cavity depth set the absorption.

Isolating walls

Between adjacent halls or between a hall and noisy corridors. They rely on multiple plasterboard layers, two separate frames and mineral wool, running up to the structural slab.

Key takeaways

  • Define each wall’s role.
  • The rear wall usually needs treatment.
  • An isolating wall runs to the slab.

This article is for general education; final design decisions are based on each project’s study and the competent authorities’ requirements.
