Insulation atlas/Halls

A multi-use hall fails if it is built as one room with a moving wall.

A conference in the morning, a concert at night, an exam in the next hall. An operable wall sold at Rw 50 in the laboratory can hand over far less if the track and the ceiling leak.

Fixed walls

Between two halls that operate at once, a double wall with a cavity and absorption in the cavity beats a single heavier wall of the same mass. The rough mass law: doubling the mass of a single leaf adds about 6 dB, until the coincidence frequency arrives and the curve drops. The double leaf wins only if nothing bridges it: a continuous screw, a floor that runs through, or a rigid duct.

The operable wall

An operable leaf is tested in the laboratory as a complete specimen with its track. On site the performance falls where it meets a suspended ceiling, at the pocket the panels store in, and at the floor if the seal does not drop. Anyone who wants two independent halls for an exam or two simultaneous shows does not stop at the catalogue paragraph. They ask for a field DnT,w after installation, and they close the ceiling above the track up to the slab.

  • An absorbing ceiling over both halls is a flanking path if the void above it is not closed.
  • Escape doors in the operable wall count inside the insulation; they are not exempt.
  • Dual use (speech / music) needs variable absorption inside each hall after the insulation is settled.

The shared foyer

A foyer gathers noise and returns it to every door. A lobby with two doors between foyer and hall raises the insulation more than changing the leaf alone. Absorption in the foyer lowers the noise that reaches the door; it does not replace the lobby if the programme is sensitive.
