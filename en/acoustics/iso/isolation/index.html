Insulation atlas

Insulation for theatres, halls, stadiums and classrooms.

An atlas of 51 chapters, added to the absorber catalogue and not a replacement. Absorption sets the inside of a room. Insulation stops sound leaving and entering. Figures are planning ranges under ISO, ASTM and school guidance, not certificates stuck to a product.

  • Theatres

    House, stage, foyer, plant.

  • Halls

    A fixed wall and a wall that moves.

  • Stadiums

    The bowl, the suite, the neighbour’s window.

  • Classrooms

    Room, corridor, and the hall above.

Language of the schedule

Six sentences for the schedule. And what is not written in their place.

This is planning language for a specifier, not a product certificate. The catalogue names the absorber. These sentences name the insulation limit, so a panel is not bought and handed over as if it were a wall.

  1. 01

    Absorption inside the room

    Supply a BOXTECH ISO absorber by its system name, thickness, position and area, with the absorption class published for that system, not for the whole family. The target is an approximate reverberation time, checked on the calculator and then by measurement. The line does not mention Rw or DnT,w.

    Do not write: “acoustic insulation, class A.” Class A is absorption. Insulation is a different figure for a different element.

  2. 02

    Theatre house towards the foyer

    DnT,w is measured between house and foyer with doors closed, under ISO 16283-1. The entrance is a lobby of two doors, not both open during a quiet scene. A single leaf’s Rw is not accepted in place of measuring the two rooms. The pit is not a party to this line: it is open to the seats on purpose.

    Do not write insulation between the audience and the player. They share one volume. A ceiling panel shortens the tail and does not close the foyer.

  3. 03

    Two halls and a moving wall

    DnT,w is measured after the operable wall is installed, not from the panel’s sheet while it is still on the floor. The ceiling above the track is closed to the slab. If the hall sits under bedrooms, add L'nT,w after the finished floor: mass on a resilient layer, and a perimeter strip that does not touch the wall. Reinforced music below 125 Hz is named on its own, because a single number hides it.

    Do not replace a floating floor with an absorbing ceiling panel. Impact climbs the slab. Absorption stays with the guests inside the hall.

  4. 04

    A stadium suite

    Three separate lines. The bowl: reverberation and layout, with no DnT,w between the crowd and the pitch. The suite: installed glass and a door, measured as DnT,w with the crowd in the bowl. The neighbour: the permit limit at the façade, and Ctr if the spectrum is low. Jumping on the stands is a fourth, impact line if the frame reaches the suite floor. Rain on a light roof is a fifth line for the commentary room.

    Do not fold the three into “stadium insulation, 50 dB.” The bowl is not insulated from itself, and the suite is not measured by the neighbour’s window.

  5. 05

    A speech classroom beside music

    The two rooms should not touch if the plan allows it. If they do, the music limit is higher than a speech-classroom limit, the door is a complete set, and the duct is not shared. A suspended ceiling is closed to the slab above the partition. After the shell is closed, an absorber from the catalogue is specified for an area that can bring reverberation toward the school guide, often about 0.6 seconds for an ordinary speech room. That is a planning target, not a panel certificate.

    Do not promise a volume without a door the insulation of a closed classroom. An open corridor is a door. A practice room is not a classroom that gets the same panel.

  6. 06

    Handover, not opening night

    Insulation is handed over by a field measurement: ISO 16283-1 for airborne sound and ISO 16283-2 for impact, in third octaves, with doors and services as they will be in use. Absorption is handed over by a reverberation measurement in the same room after installation. An impression on opening night is not a record.

    Do not accept a laboratory certificate for a door specimen and then measure the whole room expecting the same figure. A small hole caps a large wall.

  • Foundation

    Absorption is not insulation. Confusing them ruins the schedule.

    A ceiling absorber shortens the tail inside the room. An isolating wall stops sound reaching the next room. Neither does the other’s job.

  • The numbers

    Rw, DnT,w, STC: which number belongs in the contract

    A laboratory figure describes a specimen. A field figure describes the building after installation and flanking. A contract that mixes them cannot be tested.

  • Theatres

    A theatre is isolated from the foyer, the plant and the neighbours, then absorbed inside.

    An audience in the foyer, a show on stage, a control room. Three worlds. If the contract does not separate them, opening night will.

  • Halls

    A multi-use hall fails if it is built as one room with a moving wall.

    A conference in the morning, a concert at night, an exam in the next hall. An operable wall sold at Rw 50 in the laboratory can hand over far less if the track and the ceiling leak.

  • Stadiums

    A stadium is heard in the bowl, in the suite, and at the neighbour’s window. Three limits, not one.

    Commentary, a crowd, and generators. Insulation here is structural and environmental. Absorption belongs only inside the enclosed rooms.

  • Classrooms

    A classroom is isolated from the next classroom, from the corridor, and from the gym above.

    Consonant clarity inside the room is absorption. A pupil not hearing the next lesson is insulation. A school that treats one and leaves the other builds a tiring row of rooms.

  • The envelope

    Wall, floor, door, glass: the shell equals its weakest opening.

    A calculation that repeats on every project: a 55 dB element beside a 30 dB element is heard close to 30 if the opening is not very small.

  • Paths

    Sound goes around the wall more often than through it.

    A continuous floor, a shared suspended ceiling, a glass façade, a duct. Four ways a laboratory Rw becomes a promise that is not kept.

  • The contract

    How to write an insulation line so it can be measured on handover.

    The sentence ‘soundproof to good practice’ cannot be tested. The figure, the standard, the place, and the state of flanking are the line.

  • Doors

    A door is a set, not a leaf. The threshold and the frame are the insulation.

    A wall at Rw 55 and a door without seals is handed over as an opening in the twenties. In a theatre, a classroom and a hall, the door is the line that is measured first.

  • Glass

    Glass limits the suite, the control room and the façade. Thickness alone is not enough.

    A single thick pane meets coincidence and then stops. Unequal double glass, a wide cavity and a closed frame, is the road for suites facing a pitch or a street.

  • Impact

    Stadium footsteps and a hall’s ball are measured as L'nT,w. A higher figure here is worse.

    A heavy slab raises airborne insulation. Impact needs a resilient break and a perimeter strip. Carpet does not replace a floating floor under a gym.

  • Air

    One duct without a silencer defeats a theatre wall and the NR curve.

    Quiet in the hall is a background-noise figure, not an insulation rating. And insulation between two rooms falls if they share a short air branch.

  • Music

    A music room is not a heavier classroom. It is a louder source beside a quieter neighbour.

    A speech classroom may live with DnT,w around 45. A band room or a piano beside a library or an exam room sits higher, and its door is the limit.

  • Lectures

    A lecture theatre is isolated from the foyer, then absorbed until the last consonant is clear.

    An audience outside, a lecturer on the platform, sometimes a recording. Three requirements: a shell, a short tail, and a low background.

  • Cinema

    Two neighbouring screens are isolated from each other before the cloth is chosen.

    A high continuous level, and low frequencies from the screen channel. A single wall between two auditoria is usually not enough, and a shared door drops the figure.

  • Sport

    A sports hall over a classroom is measured as impact and airborne together.

    A ball, a whistle, and a moving stand. Carpet is not enough. The resilient floor, the separating ceiling and the door are the lines.

  • Worship and majlis

    A prayer hall and a stone majlis: echo inside the room, insulation from the neighbour and the classroom.

    Marble and glass lengthen the tail. Amplification raises the level. Insulation is written when the hall sits beside a school, a home or an office — not when the only request is clarity of the imam.

  • Banquet

    A banquet hall is split one night and joined the next. The operable wall is measured after it is installed.

    Amplified music, table talk, and sometimes a second hall behind the wall at the same time. A catalogue does not replace a field DnT,w.

  • Control and commentary

    A commentary box and a control room are isolated by glass, then quieted by the air.

    Isolate the bowl or the house and then broadcast the fan, and the figure that reaches the ear is not DnT,w.

  • Vibration

    What does not travel in air travels in the frame: a generator, a winch, a crowd.

    Airborne insulation does not stop a structural knock. The resilient mount and the break are drawn with the structural engineer before the pour.

  • Measurement

    The measurement day is agreed before the build, not after the complaint.

    ISO 16283-1 is airborne sound in the building, ISO 717-1 the weighting. The laboratory is ISO 10140, which is something else. The contract names which.

  • Failures

    Eleven failures that repeat in theatres, halls, stadiums and schools.

    Most of them are details, not a ‘weak product’. Reading them before the pour saves the repeat measurement.

  • Standards

    The standards shelf: which document is named in which line.

    Mixing ISO 10140 with ISO 16283, or STC with Rw by adding a constant, makes a contract that cannot be tested.

  • Theatre · shell

    The auditorium is a box inside the building, not a hall with absorbent paint.

    Stage, house, foyer, backstage and control are four limits. Each is drawn before the cloth is chosen.

  • Theatre · plant

    One duct cancels the auditorium wall.

    The silencer, the velocity and the resilient base are part of the insulation. An absorbing ceiling in the house does not treat a fan above it.

  • Hall · divide

    An operable wall is a system: track, pocket, ceiling and floor seal. Not a panel.

    Two exam halls, or a wedding beside a quiet room, fail in the ceiling above the track more often than in the panel catalogue.

  • Stadium · enclosed rooms

    The suite, the commentator and the locker room are isolated from the bowl. The bowl itself is not isolated from the sky.

    Glass towards the pitch is the usual limit. The frame, the slot and the air-conditioning undo a good figure on paper.

  • Stadium · neighbourhood

    A neighbour hears aim and level, not the rating of a wall that is not there.

    An open stadium lets sound out. A partial roof returns echo into the bowl. Neither is suite insulation.

  • School · plan

    A school is a row of limits: classroom, corridor, music, hall and prayer room.

    One figure for every wall mis-specifies music and the hall. The plan places the source before the wall is thickened.

  • Classroom · clarity

    Insulating a classroom from its neighbour does not make the consonant clear inside the room.

    STI and RT60 inside the room. DnT,w towards the other room. Two lines in the schedule, not one line called ‘acoustics’.

  • Assembly

    A schedule for theatres, halls, stadiums and classrooms: eight lines that repeat.

    Writing them once for every project avoids buying an absorber as the answer to an insulation complaint.

  • Theatre · pit

    The orchestra pit is open to the house on purpose. Insulation is to what sits under it and beside it.

    The drum and the trombone are not isolated from the audience. They are isolated from a room under the pit, from the backstage corridor, and from a neighbouring hall on the same corridor.

  • Theatre · control

    Control-room glass is the house wall. The frame decides the figure, not the pane thickness alone.

    Tilting the glass sets the reflection towards the director. It does not raise the insulation. Insulation is unequal thicknesses, a wide cavity, and a seal with no slot.

  • Theatre · dressing

    A dressing room is isolated from the house and from the next room. A curtain is not a door.

    Talk before an entrance, a shower, and a paging loudspeaker. Three paths that are not the backstage wall drawn on the section.

  • Theatre · house doors

    A two-door lobby at the audience entrance raises the limit more than a heavier leaf alone.

    Latecomers, a crowded foyer, and panic hardware. A seal that is lifted for a wheelchair is specified to drop again in the contract.

  • Hall · hotel

    A hotel hall at night is impact towards the rooms above, and airborne sound towards the next hall.

    Dancing and reinforced music are not treated by an absorbing ceiling in the hall. The guest in bed hears the slab.

  • Hall · meetings

    Simultaneous meeting rooms fail in the shared ceiling before they fail at the door.

    Interpretation, a call, and a quiet room beside applause. Privacy is intelligible speech at the neighbour, not only a drop in decibels.

  • Hall · hearings

    A hearing room isolates intelligible speech from the corridor and from the next room.

    Clarity inside the room is absorption and quiet air-conditioning. Stopping speech leaving is insulation. Mixing them exposes a file.

  • Stadium · lockers

    A locker room under the bowl hears jumping above it, and sound between it and the media.

    Impact from the concourse, airborne sound between two teams, showers, and a nearby generator. Four lines before any panel on the wall.

  • Stadium · vibration

    Jumping in the bowl reaches the suite through the frame. Glass does not stop it.

    A structural break, or a resilient base under a sensitive room, is studied with the engineer before the bowl is poured, not after the complaint.

  • Stadium · roof

    Rain on a light roof raises the background of a closed bowl and of the commentary room. That is impact, not horn aim.

    A large metal roof without mass or a break carries rain more clearly than a weak suite wall.

  • School · open plan

    A classroom without a door is not promised the insulation of a closed one. Say so in the brief.

    Distance, screens and absorption lower distraction. They do not create a DnT,w between two groups in the same volume.

  • School · practice

    A small practice room is a higher line than a speech classroom. The door and the ventilation drop the figure.

    One instrument in a small room produces levels the next classroom cannot take. A box within a box is a tool, not an assumption.

  • Detail · double leaf

    A double wall wins if the cavity stays a cavity. A bridge turns it back into one wall.

    The rough mass law: doubling the mass of a single leaf adds about 6 dB, until coincidence appears. A cavity goes beyond that if it is not tied.

  • Detail · floating floor

    A floating floor is mass on a resilient layer. One rigid point is enough to spoil it.

    A hall over a classroom, a dance floor over rooms, or a bowl over lockers. The line is L'nT,w after the finishes, not before the carpet.

  • Detail · penetrations

    A small hole puts a ceiling on the insulation of a large wall. The rough rule is known before the measurement.

    An electrical box, a cable tray, and a fire collar that is not an acoustic collar. Close them densely and offset them from their twin.

  • Detail · junctions

    A good wall loses where it meets the floor, the ceiling and the façade.

    A continuous screed, a curtain wall, and the void above a suspended ceiling. Three flanking paths that repeat in halls and classrooms.

  • Numbers · façade

    A façade on a road or a stadium is written as Rw with Ctr, not Rw alone.

    Ctr is an adaptation term for a spectrum rich in low frequency. Turning it into STC by adding a constant is the same kind of mistake.

  • Hot climate · plant

    In a hot climate the machine on the roof is the neighbour. Seals tire from dust and heat.

    A school, a stadium, a hotel and a prayer hall. The cooling does not stop, and the roof is the fan room. Insulation is maintained, not handed over once.

  • Contract · handover

    Handover is a measurement of the agreed rooms, not an impression on opening night.

    ISO 16283 for airborne sound and impact. The calculator on this site is Sabine for the tail. Neither signs for the other.

A short glossary

Rw
A laboratory airborne insulation rating, weighted to ISO 717-1.
R'w
Airborne insulation in the building, flanking included, so it is usually below Rw.
DnT,w
A field level difference standardised to the receiving room’s reverberation. Better when volumes differ.
STC
An American weighting under ASTM E413. Close to Rw, not a fixed offset from it.
L'nT,w
Field impact. A higher figure is worse, the opposite of Rw.
C / Ctr
Spectrum adaptation terms beside Rw. Ctr suits a low-frequency source such as traffic.
NR / NC
A background-noise curve inside the room. Not an insulation rating.
RT60
Reverberation time inside the same room. An absorption line; the calculator estimates it with Sabine.
αw
A weighted absorption coefficient under ISO 11654. Class A is from 0.90. It is not insulation.
Flanking
A path around the wall: screed, suspended ceiling, façade, duct.
Mass law
Doubling the mass of a single leaf adds about 6 dB until coincidence appears.
Coincidence
A frequency where a pane’s insulation falls because the wave matches its bending. Obvious in glass.
Doorset
Leaf, frame, threshold and seals together. The figure on the leaf alone is not enough.
Box-in-box
A room inside a room on resilient joints, for recording or for sensitive rooms beside a loud source.
ISO 12354
A prediction of field performance before construction. Not a handover measurement.
ISO 16283
Field measurement: part 1 airborne, part 2 impact.
BB93
British school guidance. Planning ranges, replaced by the project code when one exists.
STI
Speech transmission index inside the room. It describes clarity, not wall insulation.
NR
A background-noise curve, usually for air-conditioning. Not Rw.
Coincidence
The frequency where a single leaf’s insulation drops even as mass increases.
Rw+Ctr
Rw with the adaptation term for a low-frequency spectrum. Better for a façade on a road.
Mass-air-mass
The resonance of a double wall. If it falls inside speech, the sentence is heard despite the rating.
Floating floor
Mass on a resilient layer with no bridge. It serves L'nT,w, not reverberation time.
Sound lobby
A two-door lobby, not both open at once. It raises an entrance more than thickening one leaf.
