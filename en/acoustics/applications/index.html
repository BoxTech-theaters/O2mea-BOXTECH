BoxTech O2mea/Acoustics/Applications

Applications

Acoustic applications and theatre fit-out sections

Screens, audio, isolation, theatre decor, steel stands, boxes, cinema and events. Merged from the fit-out catalogue. BoxTech O2mea iso absorbers stay.

Screens and projectors

Stadium, hall and theatre screens: perimeter and centre LED, front and rear laser projection, sized from the furthest seat rather than the stage width.

Perimeter LED

A fine-pitch ribbon around the pitch, commonly 90–120 cm high so the first row still sees, bright enough for sun.

Centre LED

The scoreboard above the stand or at the end. Score, replay and commercial share the canvas without stealing the box sightline.

Laser projector

For halls and stages where the throw is long. Lumens follow screen width and the house light that stays on.

Front and rear projection

Front projection is simpler in an existing theatre. Rear projection needs a room and a throw, and leaves a cleaner stage.

Audio and loudspeakers

Even coverage from the first row to the last balcony: line array, point source, subwoofers and delay lines.

Line array

For theatres and covered bowls. Hung and tilted so the balcony is reached without shouting at the front row.

Point source

Smaller halls and conference rooms. One or two boxes over the stage when the grid cannot carry a heavy hang.

Subwoofer

On the floor or flown, so bass does not pool at mid-hall. Count follows air volume, not taste.

Delay and monitors

An under-balcony speaker restores time so the ceiling does not arrive early. Stage monitors stay off the house mix.

Acoustic isolation

Isolation from the street and the next hall, then treatment of the room itself. Isolation is structural; treatment is fabric and timber.

Structural isolation

Double walls, a floating floor, a lid on resilient hangers. Measured before finishes, because fabric will not fix a loud neighbour.

Acoustic doors

The hall door is the weak point. A double frame and a drop seal matter more than leaf thickness alone.

Absorption

Fabric and mineral wool behind the stage and under the balcony for speech. Not every wall, or music dies.

Diffusion

Timber of varying depth on the rear wall so applause does not return as one slap.

Theatre and hall design

Stage, wings, wall lining, architectural light, and the public path from door to seat.

Stage and wings

Stage depth, wing width and fly-tower height are drawn before the chair. Curtain, bars and lines are architecture, not the last finish.

Wall lining

Timber, stretched fabric, hidden absorbers. Colour follows stage light so the wall does not flare in the eye.

Architectural light

Aisle and step light for safety, balcony-front light for form. Emergency is a separate circuit from the opening look.

Public path

Foyer to seat without crossing the wings. Door width and exit count follow capacity, not the façade.

Steel grandstands

Fixed, telescopic and temporary stands. Steel carries the seat, the tread carries the foot, and the sum starts from live load.

Fixed steel

A permanent frame for a ground or hall. Rake, riser and tread are tied to the seat centre before steel is ordered.

Telescopic

Folds to clear the hall. The seat must fold within the unit depth, as with flexible Micra systems, not a deep club chair.

Temporary

Built and struck. Floor fixing or ballast, slip resistance and the exit path are not shortened because the run is brief.

Stadium boxes and royal boxes

Hospitality sees the pitch and stays hidden from the public stand. Deeper seats, calculated glass, service that does not cross the sightline.

Hospitality box

One or two rows, a ledge, service from behind. Centres are wider than the public stand, and the glass is raked so it does not mirror the floodlights.

Royal box

A private entry, a deeper lounge, protection, and a sightline that does not pass over stray heads. Detail is drawn with protocol, not after it.

Media suite

A longer desk, a cable aisle behind the chairs, and a view not cut by a thick mullion.

Public and home cinema

A public hall or a room at home. Screen, seat and sound are designed together, and isolation comes before the chair.

Public cinema

Floor rake, screen height, and a first row no closer than the picture is wide in a classic hall. Recliners only in premium rows if the aisle survives.

Home cinema

An isolated room, a screen or a wall, one or two rows. A deep chair is acceptable because the count is small and the exit is not a stadium discharge.

VIP hall

Fewer seats, wider centres, service. Sound is tuned for low occupancy so a half-empty room is not harsh.

Events, stands and booths

Built stands, stages and booths: a structure that strikes, a screen, light, and a visitor path that does not choke at the entry.

Exhibition stand

Island or wall. Permitted height, rigging points and power are read from the organiser’s manual before the drawing.

Booth

A smaller unit for a short meeting. A counter, a screen, one or two seats, and closed storage for the catalogue.

Event stage

A temporary stage with a stated height, stairs wide enough to exit, and a fascia that hides the cable.

Install and strike

The build is often overnight. Parts are numbered, and the truck is sized in cubic metres, not in photographs.
