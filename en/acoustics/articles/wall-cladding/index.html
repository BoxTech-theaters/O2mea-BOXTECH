Wall cladding

Wood and fabric reflect or absorb according to the cavity and the material behind them. Perforation without a cavity and an absorber is a texture. Fire and impact come from the assembly report, not from a picture of the veneer. PET felt is not a coefficient.

The face of the wall is not its behaviour. Solid wood of enough mass reflects in the speech range. The same wood, perforated, with an absorber and a cavity, absorbs in a band set by hole diameter, open area, and depth. Without those three the hole is ornament. Thin PET felt on a hard wall is weak at low frequency.

Definition

Cladding is a visible layer on a support. Acoustics ask whether the face is open to air, how thick it is, and what is behind it. The coefficient α comes from ISO 354 for a named mounting, with its frequencies. An open-area percentage on a drawing with no diameter and no cavity is not a specification. This page publishes no coefficient for any product and none for PET felt. At 100 Hz the wavelength is about 3.4 m, so a thin layer hard against the wall is not a bass tool.

Fire is the report of the assembly: face, adhesive, core, and substrate. A handsome veneer on an untested core inherits no class. The lower wall in an aisle is hit by a trolley and a chair. The impact layer may be acoustically harder than the upper wall, and the difference is calculated instead of assuming the whole wall absorbs. This page gives neither a fire class nor an impact joule.

Common mistakes

Perforation with no absorber — A texture. Energy returns from the back.

PET as bass treatment — A thin layer is not enough against the wavelength at 100 Hz. No coefficient on this page proves otherwise.

A class from a picture of the veneer — Fire belongs to the assembly. The picture has no report.

An absorbent wall where a reflector is needed — Speech goes dry. Position matters more than covering every metre.

When needed

  • Before the face is approved — The cavity and the absorber can still be drawn. The picture alone is late.
  • A rear wall that echoes — Cladding there is absorbent or tilted. On the side it may stay reflective to support speech.
  • An aisle that gets hit — The lower wall is a layer that takes impact. The fragile acoustic face sits above hand height.
  • A fabric substitution — It seals the pores or changes the fire behaviour. The report is tied again.

Where used

  • Hall walls — Side, rear, and a low ceiling. The acoustic role differs in each place.
  • Public corridors — Impact, cleaning, and exit fire. Absorption is secondary if it conflicts with those.
  • Rear theatre walls — A late echo. Perforation without an absorber is not enough.
  • Short-life exhibition walls — A visual skin may be enough. Do not call it acoustic without a build-up.

Parts

  • The face — Wood, perforated metal, fabric, or PET. Open area and diameter if there is a hole.
  • The cavity — A spacing off the hard surface. Changing it changes the band. It is written in millimetres on the detail, not here as a product figure.
  • The inner absorber — Fibre or felt at the tested thickness. Its absence makes the hole a texture.
  • Support and fixing — A frame that does not close the cavity and does not form a hard bridge over the absorber.
  • The impact layer — At the bottom of the aisle, denser, to a height above the line a trolley hits.

Engineering

  • The hole is not absorption — Without a cavity and an absorber, energy returns from the hard back.
  • Low frequency — It needs thickness or spacing. Thin PET on the wall does not treat 100 Hz because the wave is long.
  • Distribution — A fully absorbent wall can kill a useful early reflection. Position comes from the section.
  • Fire for the assembly — Face, adhesive, and core. This page has no class.
  • Impact — The lower zone is separated on the detail. Assuming the same face from floor to ceiling breaks the bottom and changes the sound.

Materials

  • PET — A common porous felt. α comes from the report of its thickness and mounting. The name is not enough.
  • Wood — Solid and reflective, or perforated on condition of a cavity. A heavy varnish can seal the pores.
  • Fabric — Open or closed. A closed weave kills the absorber behind it. The tested one is the one installed.

Related systems

  • Treatment and RT60 — Cladding is one way to change A. Coverage is not the time.
  • Isolation — Internal cladding does not close flanking. Do not mix the two pages.
  • Lighting — A spotlight punches the cavity or closes it. Its place is coordinated.
  • Furniture — A cupboard in front of the absorber removes it from the calculation. The free face is the area.

Questions

Why does the perforated wall not absorb?

Because behind it is hard, or there is no absorber in the cavity. The hole alone is a texture. The report names the build-up that absorbs.

Does PET treat bass?

Thin felt hard against the wall does not. Low frequency needs thickness or spacing. The coefficient comes from ISO 354, not from the name.

Where do I take the fire class?

From the report of face, adhesive, and core together. This page does not give it.

Why is the bottom of the wall different?

Because it is hit. A harder layer protects it and may reflect more. Do not assume the whole wall absorbs.

Does cladding isolate the neighbour?

No. That is isolation and paths. Cladding is inside the room.
