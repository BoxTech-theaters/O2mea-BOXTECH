Sound isolation

Sound isolation stops transmission through the wall, the door, the duct, and the penetration. A laboratory Rw or STC is not a field result, and absorbent panels do not close a flanking path.

Isolation is not treatment. Treatment changes what sound does inside the room. Isolation decides what crosses into the corridor, the next cinema, or the control room. Isolation usually fails at an opening, not for lack of foam.

Definition

Airborne sound isolation is the reduction of energy crossing a partition: wall, slab, door, or glass. The laboratory single number, Rw in the ISO 717-1 family after an ISO 10140 measurement, or STC after ASTM E90 and ASTM E413, describes a specimen in a laboratory. The building adds flanking through a continuous slab, the facade, and the duct. An Rw or an STC is therefore not quoted as a field result.

On-site performance is asked by a field measurement, such as the ISO 16283-1 family, then rated with a figure such as DnT,w through ISO 717-1 if that path is adopted. This page gives no such figure for a project, no absorption coefficient, and no noise level. Internal treatment and RT60 are other subjects: a well isolated room can still be reverberant.

Common mistakes

Foam inside the room — Treatment does not raise partition isolation. A neighbour-noise complaint is not treated by panels on the inner wall alone.

Quoting Rw as a result — The laboratory figure is not what will be measured between the two rooms. This page does not move any Rw or STC into the field column.

Back-to-back boxes — Two facing penetrations make a hole shorter than the wall thickness. The detail offsets one of them.

A shared duct with no silencer — Air crosses even when the wall is double. HVAC is part of isolation.

When needed

  • Two halls sharing a boundary — A cinema, a lecture hall, or a meeting room behind the stage. The programme in one must not become the noise in the other.
  • Outside noise entering the show — A street, a mechanical roof, or a public corridor. Isolation here protects speech clarity inside the hall, not only the neighbour’s comfort.
  • A shared HVAC path — When one duct serves more than one sensitive room. The duct is a hole even if the wall is heavy.
  • A door in a high-rated partition — An ordinary door, or a gap under it, spends the benefit of the laboratory wall. The door is part of the partition, not a separate joinery item.

Where used

  • Multi-screen cinemas — The partition between two screens, the corridor door, and the projection room. Low-frequency leak from the soundtrack is the usual complaint.
  • Theatres and rehearsal rooms — A rehearsal next to a performance. Isolation is what allows them to overlap. Treatment inside the rehearsal room does not solve it.
  • Adjacent conference rooms — Privacy and intelligible speech across the partition. A hearing loop can add magnetic spill. That is a separate path and is checked separately.
  • The building envelope — Plant on the roof, the fresh-air intake, and the street facade. Incoming noise raises the noise floor and lowers STI.

Parts

  • The partition itself — Mass, decoupling, or a lined cavity, as the design requires. The laboratory number of the specimen does not describe the partition after cutting and installation.
  • Door, frame, and threshold — A designed leaf or a pair of leaves, hinges, and seals that close on four edges. The undercut is an open path.
  • Glass and the control window — A control room is isolated with a designed glass build-up, not with a single pane because someone must see. Vision is a constraint, not a waiver.
  • Duct treatment — Silencers, lining, and separate paths for sensitive rooms. A silencer without enough length at the frequency that matters is metal ornament.
  • Penetration details — A back-box that does not face a back-box on the other side, a seal around the pipe, and a cable that is not left in an open hole.

Engineering

  • The weakest path governs — Wall, door, glass, duct, and flanking. Energy takes the easiest path. Raising the wall rating alone does not raise the room’s isolation.
  • Flanking through the structure — A continuous slab or a continuous facade links the two rooms around the partition. Breaking the path or adding mass is an early structural decision.
  • Low frequency — Music and picture shake the partition where mass and stiffness matter more than a speech seal. A mid-band single number hides that.
  • Noise floor and STI — Weaker isolation raises incoming noise. IEC 60268-16 rates speech clarity. Isolation is not swapped for an STI target, or the reverse.
  • Coordination with circulation — An isolating door is heavier and needs a closer and a swing. The corridor and the code see the door as a possible obstruction, not only as an acoustic panel.

Materials

  • Mass and separation — Heavier layers, separated frames, or a cavity. The material is named on a detail with the joint. A product name without a detail does not isolate.
  • Seals — A gasket that compresses around the perimeter and is replaced when it takes a set. A seal cut at the lock is a path. No isolation number is stuck to the seal profile on this page.
  • Duct lining and silencers — Chosen for frequency, pressure drop, and fire performance from the product report. This page gives no decibel insertion loss for a silencer.

Related systems

  • HVAC — The air path, grille velocity, and the machine body on the roof. Architectural isolation does not include fan noise unless it is designed.
  • Doors and access control — The lock, the hinge, and the closer have to complete the seal, not defeat it. Coordination with the joiner is electrical and mechanical.
  • Internal acoustics — Treatment and RT60 come after what noise gets in is known. An internal absorber does not raise the field Rw.
  • Listening systems — The sound-system level inside the hall, and a hearing loop in the neighbouring wall. Both can be heard outside by a different path.

Questions

Why will you not write the Rw we will get?

Because laboratory Rw is not the room result. The site has a field measurement and an acceptance criterion in the brief. Copying the figure misleads.

Is an acoustic door enough?

Only if its seal closes and there is no shorter path through a duct or a continuous slab. The door is one item in a chain of paths.

Does isolation shorten RT60?

No. A heavier wall can reflect more inside the room. Reverberation time is handled by absorption and volume, and in performance spaces it is measured to ISO 3382-1.

What about intelligible speech in the corridor?

That is a failure of the partition, the door, or the duct. Intelligibility for the neighbour is not the audience STI inside the hall, even though the index describes speech transfer on its path.

Is foam on the shared wall isolation?

No. It is internal treatment, and it is weak at low frequency if it is thin. Isolation is mass and a closed path.
