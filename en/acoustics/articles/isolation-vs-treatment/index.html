Isolation versus treatment

Isolation stops sound moving between rooms. Treatment changes sound inside a room. Confusing them is the most common hall mistake.

An absorbing panel does not isolate a room. A heavy wall does not kill internal reverberation if the other surfaces stay reflective. The questions are different, and a drawing that mixes them fails twice.

Definition

Internal treatment: absorption, diffusion, or a controlled reflection. Its aim is reverberation time and the shape of early reflections inside the same room.

Isolation: reducing energy that crosses into another room, by air or by the structure. It is discussed with laboratory figures such as Rw or STC, then defeated on site by openings and flanking.

Why it matters

A cinema beside a cinema fails by isolation, not by seat colour. A lecture in a marble hall fails by treatment, not by the thickness of the outside wall. Budget spent on the wrong question is not recovered by more loudspeakers.

Principle

The airborne path needs mass, seals, and separation. A gap around a door, a duct with no silencer, or back-to-back sockets opens a path shorter than the wall.

The structure-borne path needs a resilient break. A screw that joins two “isolated” layers can reconnect them. Isolation is drawn as a chain. The weakest link is the result.

Measurement

Inside the room — RT and the internal parameters are measured inside the room. Success there does not prove isolation.

Between two rooms — The level difference between source room and receiving room, with area, volume, and condition stated, is the language of field isolation.

Background noise — Excellent isolation in a building whose internal noise is high does not make a quiet hall. NC, NR, or an equivalent spectrum describes the inside.

Units

Rw and STC — Single-number laboratory ratings for airborne insulation. They are not the site result.

α and RT — The language of internal treatment. An absorption coefficient without a frequency is not comparable with Rw in the first place.

Design

Write the question — Is the complaint “echo in my room” or “I hear the other room”? Each sentence leads to a different drawing.

Detail the flanks — Wall-to-floor and wall-to-soffit junctions, the air path, and the service door. The detail matters more than a mass figure in a table.

Do not cover an isolation problem with panels — Panels change the inside of the room. The neighbour’s sound remains, and may seem clearer if your own decay drops.

Use

Adjacent cinemas — Isolation is the product. Treatment tunes the inside after the separation works.

A confidential meeting room — Privacy means intelligible speech at the door. Isolation and seals are the work.

A school theatre under classrooms — Structure, services, and timetable. Internal treatment does not protect the class above.

Limits

The laboratory figure is a clean ceiling — The site is worse unless flanking is controlled. Do not write the wall’s Rw as the room result.

The door is usually weaker than the wall — An ordinary door in a heavy wall sets the result. An acoustic door without a sealed frame and a drop seal does not earn the name.

Isolation does not decorate away echo — A well-isolated concrete room can still be loud inside.

Common mistakes

Buying “soundproofing” because of the product name — Read whether the data is an absorption coefficient or an insulation rating. The trade name does not decide.

Layers with no break — More boards on one continuous frame may not double the isolation the way a table imagines.

Openings after handover — A camera eye, a sprinkler cut, or a duct opening after the test brings the problem back.

Example

A wall with a high laboratory rating, an unsealed door, and a shared air duct: the listener in the corridor hears speech. A ceiling cloud will not close the duct. The example is architectural, not numeric, because the real figure is the weakest opening.

Questions

Do fabric panels isolate?

Usually no. They are internal treatment if an absorber sits behind them. Isolation needs mass, seals, and a break.

Is an acoustic door enough?

Only if its frame, threshold, and closers belong to the same system, and the wall beside it does not leak around it.

Is room-in-room excessive?

When the aim is high isolation from a light structure or a vibration source. In an ordinary speech hall the waste is often in simpler flanking first.

Why did the room sound worse after isolation?

Because a leak that was absorbing or escaping was closed, and the surfaces stayed reflective. Isolation partly worked. Treatment has not started.

What do I ask for in the brief?

An internal sentence: a reverberation and clarity target. An external sentence: who must not hear whom, and in what condition. Do not write only “soundproof”.
