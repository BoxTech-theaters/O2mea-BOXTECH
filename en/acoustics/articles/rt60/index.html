Reverberation time RT60

RT60 changes with frequency, volume, and use. One number for a hall type is a discussion target only. Sabine fails when absorption is high, and ISO 3382-1 is the measurement reference, not this page.

RT60 is the time for energy to fall 60 dB after the source stops, and it is not one number for the hall. A serious measurement reads it in bands, and T20 or T30 are extrapolated because noise hides the full fall. ISO 3382-1 describes that for performance spaces.

Definition

The definition ties the time to a fall, not to the operating level. A speech room and an unamplified music room of the same volume are not discussed with the same tail: speech wants the syllable to die, and music may want the energy to remain. This page sets no universal target in seconds for any hall type. A mid-band figure opened in a meeting is a discussion target until the brief and the measurement fix it.

The metric Sabine equation RT = 0.161 V / A ties the time to volume V and to total absorption A. The picture is sound: a larger volume lengthens the time if absorption per square metre stays put. The formula assumes a diffuse field and modest, distributed absorption. When panels cover a large area, or absorber gathers in the ceiling, it drifts from measurement. Eyring handles a higher mean and is still not the room.

Common mistakes

One second for the whole project — A lecture, a sports hall, and music do not share a target. One figure deletes use and volume.

Sabine after dense coverage — The formula is used where its assumption is weak, then the team is surprised by measurement. The formula’s limit is written with the result.

Reading only the mid band — The bass stays long and speech sounds coloured despite a neat mid-band figure.

Mixing RT60 with STI — A shorter time does not guarantee understanding if noise is high. The index is in IEC 60268-16.

When needed

  • Before volume is frozen — Added height lengthens RT60 if absorption does not rise. The architectural decision comes before the panels.
  • When the programme differs — A lecture by day and music at night in the same hall. One figure does not serve both settings, and variable acoustics may be the discussion.
  • After a finish change — A mineral tile, a curtain, or a carpet changes A in a band, not in every band. Recalculate, then measure.
  • Before judging the sound system — STI is affected by the tail and by noise. Raising the gain does not shorten RT60. IEC 60268-16 is a separate question.

Where used

  • Performance spaces — Theatres and music rooms, where ISO 3382-1 is the reference for measuring parameters, including decay.
  • Speech rooms — Lecture and conference. Discussion tends toward a shorter mid-band tail so syllables separate. The value lives in the brief, not here.
  • Multipurpose halls — A compromise, or curtains and movable absorption. Each setting is measured. No setting is perfect for both programmes.
  • Small rooms — Modes show, and Sabine is weaker. Measurement and a geometric model matter more than one second.

Parts

  • Volume — V in the formula. More seats in the same volume tend toward a shorter time because the audience is absorption, if the surfaces stay.
  • Absorption A — Areas times frequency coefficients from ISO 354 reports. Without those data A is unknown. This page does not fill the table.
  • Distribution — Scattered absorption is closer to the Sabine assumption. Concentrated absorption leaves live surfaces and late paths.
  • Audience and seats — Occupancy is part of the criterion. Empty and full are two figures. The report states which was measured.
  • Noise that hides the fall — T20 and T30 exist because a full 60 dB may not be visible above HVAC. The method is named.

Engineering

  • Frequency dependence — Bass longer than the mid band is common in rooms untreated at low frequency. An average of 500–1000 Hz hides it.
  • Sabine at high absorption — When mean α is large or absorber covers most of the surface, the field is not diffuse and the result drifts from 0.161 V / A.
  • Volume and use together — A larger hall tends toward a longer time for the same finish. Speech and music do not share one target because of that. There is no universal table here.
  • Clarity is not the time — C50 and STI describe speech in other ways. A short RT60 with high noise can still fail understanding.
  • Prediction then measurement — The calculator on this site is instructional. ISO 3382-1 is the measurement in a performance space. This page does not perform the standard.

Materials

  • Live surfaces — Solid plaster, dense wood, and glass reflect. Keeping them is a decision about the tail, not a forgotten treatment.
  • The absorber and its build-up — Thickness, cavity, and face change the band. Thin PET felt is not bass treatment. The coefficient comes from the report.
  • Variable elements — Heavy curtains and rotating panels. Measure them open and closed. The mechanism’s name is not two figures.

Related systems

  • Treatment — Panels are one way to change A. Coverage on the drawing is not the result.
  • Isolation — More isolating walls can reflect more inside. Isolating the neighbour does not shorten the room’s tail.
  • The sound system — A Line Array or a Point Source lights surfaces. Poor aim asks absorption to hide a mistake.
  • HVAC — The noise floor decides whether T30 is valid. Lowering noise is part of being able to measure, not of elegance.

Questions

Is there a correct value for every theatre?

No. Volume and programme change the discussion. This page rejects a universal target. The brief writes the band curve.

Why not trust Sabine?

Because it assumes low, distributed absorption. A hall covered in panels breaks the assumption. Measurement shows the gap.

Is T30 the same as RT60?

T30 estimates the full fall from part of the curve, inside the standard’s method. The report names the symbol. Do not mix the result with a Sabine calculation without saying so.

Is the calculator on the site enough?

No. It applies the formula to numbers you type. Replace the coefficients with laboratory reports, then with an ISO 3382-1 measurement.

Does a short RT60 mean a high STI?

Not always. Noise lowers the index. STI is in IEC 60268-16. The time is part of the story.
