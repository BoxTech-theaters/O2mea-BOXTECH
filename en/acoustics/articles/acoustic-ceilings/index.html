Acoustic ceilings

A cloud or a baffle adds absorption while the soffit stays open for services. A black ceiling over the stage is a visual decision and may still reflect. A mineral tile in a theatre is a choice with consequences for RT60. Coverage is not a time.

A hall ceiling either reflects, absorbs, disappears visually, or leaves a void for the duct, the spotlight, and the rigging. A cloud is a hung panel that absorbs from one face or two and does not close the room. A baffle is a vertical fin that adds area. Black paint on a hard soffit is not an absorber. A mineral-fibre tile is an acoustic decision, not a theatre default.

Definition

A cloud leaves the structural soffit visible or open, so services and rigging pass between panels. If both faces absorb, both areas are counted if the report allows, and that is still not an RT60: volume and the rest of the room are in the calculation, then ISO 3382-1. Hang height changes the low-frequency behaviour of a porous panel. This page gives no α. A baffle adds area when there is not enough horizontal area, and it collides with a sprinkler and a spotlight if it is packed with no coordination.

A black ceiling over the stage hides the tower. If it is hard, it is a dark reflector. If it is a drape, its absorption is that drape’s report, and it moves in the air. A mineral tile in an office absorbs mid and high easily and opens for services; in an unamplified theatre it can kill brilliance and remove a shaped reflector. In a speech room it may be the right tool. The choice is written with its consequences, not as a building habit.

Common mistakes

Office tile in a music theatre — It absorbs highs and removes shape. Speech may accept it and unamplified music may not.

Black assumed absorbent — Paint on concrete is reflective. Colour is visual concealment.

A baffle in the bar’s path — The first rigging move tears the acoustic ceiling. Coordination comes first.

A square metre equals a second — No conversion. RT60 is a calculation or a measurement.

When needed

  • Services fill the soffit — Duct, sprinkler, and rigging. A closed ceiling hides them or is punctured until it loses its role.
  • Absorption is wanted without closing the room — A cloud or a baffle. A full tile is another option to compare, not to assume.
  • The tower must disappear — Black paint or a drape. Decide whether the surface reflects before approving the colour.
  • Speech room versus music — Tile may suit the first and hurt the second. The programme comes before the catalogue.

Where used

  • Lecture halls — Distributed absorption and a ceiling that opens for maintenance. Tile is an intelligible option if speech remains the target.
  • Theatres — A reflector in some places and a cloud in others. A blanket of tile rarely suits unamplified music.
  • Over the stage — A visually black soffit, and rigging that moves. Absorption, if needed, comes from a drape or a panel, not from paint.
  • Booths and corridors — A short-life baffle or a tile. Do not call the office a theatre or the reverse.

Parts

  • The cloud — A hung panel at a written height, one face or two according to the report.
  • The baffle — A vertical fin. The spacing between fins is part of the calculated coverage.
  • The mineral tile — A grid and a tile. NRC, if the sheet mentions it, is a laboratory average, not a theatre curve. This page does not quote it.
  • The black soffit — Paint, drape, or panel. Each behaves differently.
  • The penetrations — Sprinkler, loudspeaker, spotlight, and access hatch. Every hole is coordinated with the acoustic role.

Engineering

  • Coverage is not RT60 — A cloud’s square metres, with no volume and no other surfaces, do not produce a second.
  • Hang height — It spaces the porous panel off the hard soffit and changes the low band. The figure comes from the detail, not from here.
  • Two faces or one — Count only what the report allows. Assuming the back face absorbs without a test is a fictional gain.
  • Black reflects if it is hard — Visual colour. α comes from the build-up. Paint is not an absorber.
  • Tile is a decision — It absorbs highs easily. In unamplified music that can be a loss of brilliance, not a gain in comfort.

Materials

  • Fibre or PET in the cloud — Thickness and mounting from ISO 354. This page has no coefficient.
  • Mineral tile — Easy to replace and cheap to maintain. Its office look may be unwanted in the house, and that is a reason separate from sound.
  • Black paint or drape — Paint reflects if the surface is hard. The drape has its own report, and it moves.

Related systems

  • Sprinklers and detection — The ceiling cover does not delay a sprinkler. Coordination with fire protection is mandatory.
  • Stage rigging — Baffle and cloud stay out of the bar’s path. A collision drops both.
  • Sound — The loudspeaker is not buried behind a tile with no calculated opening. The grid blocks or it does not, by design.
  • Lighting — The spotlight needs an angle the baffle does not cut. The ceiling serves the light and does not sever it.

Questions

Do I fill a theatre ceiling with tile?

Rarely for unamplified music. It may suit a speech room. The decision is written for its effect on the highs and on the reflector, not as an office habit.

Does a black ceiling treat sound?

Not if it is painted and hard. It treats sound if it is a drape or a panel with a report. Colour hides and does not absorb.

Does the cloud area equal an RT60?

No. The time needs the volume and the other surfaces, then a measurement. The square metre is coverage.

Where does the sprinkler go?

On a clear path the cloud or baffle does not block. Fire protection is coordination, not a later detail.

Is NRC enough to choose the tile?

No. It is an average if it is on the sheet. A theatre needs the bands and the shape. This page does not quote an NRC.
