Soundproofing for theatres and halls: stopping noise getting in or out

Soundproofing keeps the performance inside the hall and keeps traffic, aircraft and plant noise outside. It is fundamentally construction work: mass, decoupling and sealing. At BoxTech O2mea we design and build sound isolation so the room reaches the quietness its use requires.

How we approach soundproofing

Three principles govern isolation: mass (heavier materials isolate more), decoupling (two unconnected leaves beat one thick leaf) and sealing (any small gap weakens the whole wall). Good isolation therefore uses double walls with a cavity and mineral wool, and careful details at doors and services.

Flanking paths cause many failures: sound travelling through a shared suspended ceiling, HVAC ducts or a continuous floor. We treat the room as a complete box, not a single wall.

In sensitive rooms such as cinemas and studios, a room-within-a-room may be needed, with a floating floor and independent walls and ceiling on resilient mounts.

Checklist before you start

  • Is the problem sound transfer (isolation) or internal echo (treatment)?
  • Has existing and target reverberation been measured or calculated?
  • Have flanking paths such as ceilings and ducts been identified?
  • Do materials have tested absorption or isolation values?
  • Is the fire classification suitable for a public space?
  • Is treatment integrated into the interior design?

Common mistakes we help you avoid

  • Using foam to soundproof
  • Covering every wall with absorbers so the room goes dead
  • Neglecting doors and openings in isolation projects
  • Relying on an absorption coefficient without knowing the tested mounting

Requirements in Saudi Arabia

Acoustic materials in public spaces in the Kingdom must meet the approved fire-protection requirements, so we request supplier test certificates and check them against the project specification before procurement.

To speed up the study, prepare what you have: the architectural plan (PDF or CAD), building sections and clear heights, the expected audience size, the main uses in order of importance, photos of the existing site if any, and the target programme. Frequently asked questions

Does foam soundproof a room?

No. Foam reduces echo inside the room but does not stop sound passing through. Isolation needs mass, decoupling and sealing.

Can an existing room be soundproofed?

Yes, by adding independent layers to walls and ceiling and treating doors, with a small loss of space.

Why does sound pass despite a thick wall?

Usually through flanking paths such as the suspended ceiling, ducts or gaps around doors.

Is acoustic glazing enough?

It helps a lot, but the frame and installation determine the real performance.
