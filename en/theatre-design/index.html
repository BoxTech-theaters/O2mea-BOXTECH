Theatre design in Saudi Arabia: from brief to construction drawings

Designing a theatre is not about drawing an attractive room. It is a set of engineering decisions that decide whether every seat can read the performer’s face, whether a spoken line reaches the back row clearly, and whether the stage will work safely for years. At BoxTech O2mea we start from the programme, the seat count and the site, then build the architectural, acoustic and stage design around them as one coordinated package.

How we approach theatre design

Most of the theatre problems we are later asked to fix were created at design stage: a rake too shallow for clear sightlines, a reflective ceiling that produces echo, or a stage with no wing space for scene changes. Fixing these after construction is expensive; solving them on paper costs only design time.

We therefore treat theatre design as system design: the room form (proscenium, thrust, in-the-round, black box), the relationship between house and stage, the circulation of audience, performers and services, and then the technical layers of acoustics, lighting, sound and seating. Each layer is designed with the constraints of the others in view.

In the Kingdom, demand for theatres is growing with the culture and performing arts sector, and owners need clear design files that pass Civil Defense requirements and the Saudi Building Code. We help owners and consultants reach a design package that can be priced and built without surprises.

Project types

  • Traditional proscenium theatre with main stage and wing space
  • Multipurpose theatre for drama, concerts and conferences
  • Black-box studio with flexible seating
  • School or university theatre with a mid-size stage
  • Theatre inside a cultural centre or mixed-use complex
  • Outdoor or semi-open venue for seasonal events

Project phases

  1. Needs assessment — We define the programme, seat count, level of equipment, indicative budget and site constraints.
  2. Concept planning — We propose the room form and its relation to the stage, with first sightline sections, aisles and exits.
  3. Acoustic and stage design — We size the room volume and target reverberation, and set proscenium, wing and rigging dimensions.
  4. Interiors and seating — We select the seating system and finishes so they serve acoustics, sightlines and the project’s character.
  5. Construction documents — We issue drawings, material schedules and specifications ready for pricing and build.
  6. Site support — We review samples and submittals and answer site queries during construction.

Materials

Fire-treated timber cladding

For side walls and reflectors, selected to meet fire-safety requirements.

Fabric-wrapped or perforated timber absorbers

For rear walls and zones where echo must be controlled.

Sprung timber stage floor

Protects dancers and actors and takes scenery loads.

Flame-retardant stage textiles

For the house curtain, borders and legs.

Engineering aspects

Sightlines

We target a C-value of roughly 90–120 mm so each row sees over the head in front.

Distance to stage

For drama we keep the furthest seat at about 20 m from the stage edge so facial expression stays readable.

Acoustics

A target reverberation time of about 0.9–1.3 s for speech and drama, with low background noise (around NC 20–25).

Life safety

Aisle widths, exit numbers and travel distances follow the applicable code and Civil Defense requirements.

Figures quoted are general guidance from common professional practice; final values for each project are set by its use and the requirements of the competent authorities.

Checklist before you start

  • Has the dominant performance type and annual use been defined?
  • Have sightline sections been drawn for the furthest and nearest seats?
  • Are wing space and the scenery get-in route adequate for expected productions?
  • Are rigging points and loads approved by a structural engineer?
  • Have target reverberation and background noise been set?
  • Does the control room see the stage directly and hear the real house sound?

Common mistakes we help you avoid

  • Reducing the rake to save height and discovering blocked views
  • Choosing glossy finishes near the stage that reflect show lighting
  • Postponing front-of-house lighting positions until after the ceiling is closed
  • Neglecting isolation of plant rooms next to the house

Requirements in Saudi Arabia

In the Kingdom, theatre projects pass Civil Defense requirements and the Saudi Building Code, particularly on egress, fire performance of materials, alarm and suppression. We prepare drawings and specifications so these requirements are reviewed early with the approved consultant, and use materials with test certificates suitable for public venues.

What you receive

  • An initial technical notes report after drawing review or site visit
  • A clear itemised scope of work
  • Approved drawings and specifications before construction
  • A programme linked to project stages
  • Records of concealed works and handover with documents and training

How to prepare your request

To speed up the study, prepare what you have: the architectural plan (PDF or CAD), building sections and clear heights, the expected audience size, the main uses in order of importance, photos of the existing site if any, and the target programme. The information need not be complete; helping you complete it is part of our work.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to start a theatre design?

A site or building plan, an approximate seat count and the type of performances are enough to begin. We complete the rest with you in a needs-assessment session.

Do you only design, or also build?

We offer design on its own or as design-and-build. Many owners prefer the same team to stay responsible for turning the design into reality, which narrows the gap between drawing and site.

How long does theatre design take?

It depends on size and level of detail. We set a clear timeline after the needs assessment instead of quoting a generic figure that may not fit your project.

Can an existing hall be converted into a theatre?

Often yes, provided height and structure allow a rake, a stage and rigging points. We start with a technical visit to assess feasibility before any commitment.
