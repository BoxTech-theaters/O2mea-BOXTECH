Theatre and hall design

Hall interiors

Timber, cloth and light after the seat sample. The interior serves hearing and exit, not the picture alone.

Interior is a last layer with rules

Hall interior is not a colour on a board. A wall may absorb or reflect depending on what is behind the timber. A ceiling may send sound to the last row or swallow it. Light may wash the stage or stop at the step. boxtech ties these decisions to the approved BoxTech seat: arm depth, cloth colour, and a row number that must be readable in the dark.

In universities, schools and corporate halls the repeated error is copying an opera-house interior onto a lecture room. The sitting is shorter, the tablet folds, and the aisle is used between classes. The interior here is durability, cleaning and speech clarity, not ornament that steals absorption or narrows the exit.

Final figures come from the drawing, the current manufacturer datasheet and a test of the room. This page is a design method, not a performance certificate and not a price.

Coordination sequence

Theatre, hall and stadium design starts with the brief, then the geometry, then the seat, then the interior. Reversing that order produces a picture, not a room.

Sightline, exit and eye height come before timber colour and the length of the masking. A box is a room. The bowl is a sector. One is not named as the other.

boxtech coordinates these layers for the seat, and for isolation or an event where they are part of the job. The architect signs the building. These pages do not replace that signature.

Mistakes that repeat

  • Solid timber on every wall, then a complaint that the hall is loud.
  • Architectural light that will not dim during the show.

Questions about this type

Does the interior include loose furniture?

If loose furniture stays in the exit or in front of a sightline it is a safety clause, not a decor clause. A fixed table is drawn with the seat. A loose chair with nowhere to be stored returns to the aisle on opening night. We ask for the store on the drawing.
