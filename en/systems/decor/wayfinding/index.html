Theatre and hall interiors

Wayfinding and numbering

The number is read in the dark from the aisle, and the row plate sits at its head.

Numbering is an operations clause

A number that needs a phone torch has failed. In a theatre, darkness is a condition of the show. In a stadium, sun and broadcast are the conditions. The place of the number, on the seat or the aisle standard, the contrast, and counting from the gate rather than from midfield: these are decisions written before printing. A plate at the head of the row helps both the usher and the public.

Final figures come from the drawing, the current manufacturer datasheet and a test of the room. This page is a design method, not a performance certificate and not a price.

Coordination sequence

Interior work serves sight, hearing and exit. The seat is approved before the timber colour, the sightline before the length of the masking, and the aisle before the depth of the joinery.

Timber may absorb or reflect depending on what is behind it. The name is not enough. Fire classification is taken from the material document as the authority requires. It is not invented. A number is readable under show light, not in the workshop.

Archive photographs explain the type of room. They are not a palette to copy, and they are not proof of authorship for every hall that appears.

This clause goes into the bill of quantities, with the signatory and the date of the test after installation: A reading test from the aisle under show light, not workshop light.
