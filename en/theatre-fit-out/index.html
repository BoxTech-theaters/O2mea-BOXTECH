Theatre fit-out: stage machinery, drapes, lighting, sound and control in one package

A theatre without technical fit-out is just a handsome room. Fit-out is what lets a stage host a show: a curtain that opens on cue, lighting that reveals the performer, sound that arrives clearly, and an operating team controlling it from one place. At BoxTech O2mea we fit out theatres as an integrated package that starts with the performance needs and ends with training the operators.

How we approach theatre fit-out

We organise fit-out into layers: mechanical (flying bars, drapes and masking), lighting (fixtures, power and control), sound and video (loudspeakers, processing, displays) and control (control room and networks). Each layer is specified at a level that matches the theatre’s real use.

Many theatres are fitted out above or below their needs. A school rarely needs complex motorised flying, while a cultural theatre hosting touring companies needs more flexibility. We help you set the right level in plain language before you buy.

We also care about what follows installation: operating documents, as-built drawings and training technicians in safe operation and routine maintenance.

Project phases

  1. Defining the level — By show type, frequency and operating team.
  2. Design and specification — Equipment lists, distribution drawings and containment.
  3. Procurement — Equipment from authorised suppliers with documentation.
  4. Installation — Rigging, cabling, termination and testing.
  5. Commissioning and training — Basic scene programming and team training.

Checklist before you start

  • Has the dominant performance type and annual use been defined?
  • Have sightline sections been drawn for the furthest and nearest seats?
  • Are wing space and the scenery get-in route adequate for expected productions?
  • Are rigging points and loads approved by a structural engineer?
  • Have target reverberation and background noise been set?
  • Does the control room see the stage directly and hear the real house sound?

Common mistakes we help you avoid

  • Reducing the rake to save height and discovering blocked views
  • Choosing glossy finishes near the stage that reflect show lighting
  • Postponing front-of-house lighting positions until after the ceiling is closed
  • Neglecting isolation of plant rooms next to the house

Requirements in Saudi Arabia

In the Kingdom, theatre projects pass Civil Defense requirements and the Saudi Building Code, particularly on egress, fire performance of materials, alarm and suppression. We prepare drawings and specifications so these requirements are reviewed early with the approved consultant, and use materials with test certificates suitable for public venues.

What you receive

  • An initial technical notes report after drawing review or site visit
  • A clear itemised scope of work
  • Approved drawings and specifications before construction
  • A programme linked to project stages
  • Records of concealed works and handover with documents and training

How to prepare your request

To speed up the study, prepare what you have: the architectural plan (PDF or CAD), building sections and clear heights, the expected audience size, the main uses in order of importance, photos of the existing site if any, and the target programme. The information need not be complete; helping you complete it is part of our work.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need motorised flying?

Not always. School theatres and multipurpose halls may be well served by safe manual bars, while busy producing theatres need more advanced systems.

Do you train the operators?

Yes, handover includes practical training on operation and basic maintenance.

Can fit-out be phased?

Yes. We design the infrastructure to accept later expansion without rework.

Do you work in existing theatres?

Yes. We assess the current condition first, then propose an upgrade plan.
